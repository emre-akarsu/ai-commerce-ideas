# UK bathroom tiling and finishes: budget / most_used / premium options (2024-2026)

Notes researched 2026-10-06. All prices are **as observed** in search results or retailer pages on or around that date (some listings may be cached older prices). Prices are inc VAT unless marked ex VAT. Brands are named only as examples. Research budget was limited (~17 tool calls), so several parameters have thin evidence; see Gaps.

## Q1. Wall and floor tiles (ceramic vs porcelain, sizes, GBP/m2 bands)

### Takeaway
Porcelain and ceramic are roughly equally stocked at mass retailers. 600x300 and 600x600 porcelain is available from about £12-15/m2 at Wickes, while 300x600 designer porcelain is about £38-50/m2. Default: **300x600 (600x300) wall tile, ceramic or porcelain, in the £12-£25/m2 band (most_used)**. Floors default to porcelain because it is denser and more durable.

### Cited Findings
- Wickes bathroom wall and floor tiles category: 219 products. Material filter: Porcelain 109, Ceramic 91, Natural stone 8. Finish filter: Matt 101, Gloss 58, Polished 27. Colour filter: Greys 67, Whites 57. 83 products are priced £0-£19.99/m2 and 3 are over £200 (observed 2026-10-06) — [Wickes](https://www.wickes.co.uk/Products/Tiles/Bathroom-Wall+Floor-Tiles/c/1000801)
- Wickes examples: Sigford Grey Matt porcelain 600x300 £12/m2 (13 reviews); Calacatta Charm Polished porcelain 600x600 £15/m2 (85 reviews); Livingstone Greige porcelain 600x600 £14/m2; Metro White Gloss ceramic 200x100 £15/m2 (**863 reviews**, the most-reviewed item listed) (observed 2026-10-06) — [Wickes](https://www.wickes.co.uk/Products/Tiles/Bathroom-Wall+Floor-Tiles/c/1000801)
- Wickes Luna Light Grey ceramic wall and floor 600x300 at £13/m2 (search snippet) — [Wickes via search](https://www.wickes.co.uk/Products/Tiles/Bathroom-Wall+Floor-Tiles/c/1000801)
- Mid/premium 300x600 examples: Stonehouse Studio Jardine 300x600 £38.15/m2 (Victorian Plumbing); Arkesia Polished Graphite 300x600 £43.73/m2; Contempora Beige £49.96/m2 (search snippets, observed 2026-10) — [Victorian Plumbing](https://www.victorianplumbing.co.uk/stonehouse-studio-jardine-gloss-blue-marble-effect-wall-and-floor-tiles-300-x-600mm)
- Supply-only bands (article dated 8 June 2026): ceramic £10-£25/m2 supply (+£25-£40 fitting), "low-traffic domestic bathrooms"; porcelain £20-£50/m2 supply (+£35-£55 fitting), "durable; excellent for UFH". Tiles remain "the most popular bathroom floor finish in the UK" — [Trade2Base](https://www.trade2base.com/blog/bathroom-flooring-options-pricing-uk)

### Inferences

Metro 200x100 is a popular style (high review count), but it means more grout lines and more labour.

## Q2. Tile adhesive (ready-mixed tub vs powder; standard vs flexible S1; rapid-set)

### Takeaway
Ready-mixed tub adhesive is limited to small ceramic wall tiles in non-power-shower areas. It cannot be used on floors, for porcelain or for large tiles. The UK default is a **cement-based flexible S1 powder in a 20kg bag**. The most-reviewed product at Wickes is Mapei Mapeker Rapid Set Flexible S1 (669 reviews, £19/20kg).

### Cited Findings
- Wickes tile adhesives (34 products, default sort): EVO-STIK Waterproof Wall Tile Adhesive 10L ready-mixed £32 (22 reviews); UltraTrade S1 Standard Set White 20kg £21; UltraTrade S1 Rapid Set Grey 20kg £21; **Mapei Mapeker Rapid Set Grey Flexible 20kg £19 (£0.95/kg), 669 reviews**; Mapei Fast Set Grey Ceramic (non-flexible) 20kg £12.50 (£0.63/kg), 170 reviews (observed 2026-10-06) — [Wickes](https://www.wickes.co.uk/Products/Tiles/Tile-Adhesives/c/1000877)
- Other Wickes prices (search snippet): Mapei Super Flexible S1 Grey 20kg £25; Mapei Standard Set Plus White ceramic and porcelain 20kg £22; UltraTrade Standard Set White 20kg £18 — [Wickes](https://www.wickes.co.uk/featured/mapei-grey-tile-adhesive)
- Ready-mixed paste "cannot be used to tile floors". Tiles over 30x30cm, or porcelain tiles of any size, "will need a powdered adhesive". BAL Green Star is for ceramics up to 30x30cm and not for porcelain, power showers or wet rooms. BAL White Star Plus takes ceramic up to 30x30 and porcelain up to 15x15 — [Topps Tiles knowledge base (search snippet; page 403 on fetch)](https://www.toppstiles.co.uk/knowledge-base/what-tile-adhesive-to-use)
- Why: ready-mixed adhesives cure by losing moisture. Porcelain (water absorption ≤0.5%) slows bond formation — [Norcros Shower White datasheet (search snippet)](https://www.specifiedby.com/norcros-adhesives/shower-white/b13%20Norcros%20Shower%20White%20Wall%20Tile%20Adhesive%202014.pdf)
- BAL SPF S1: flexible S1, standard set, "PorcelBond" technology for porcelain. Suitable for large-format wall tile, showers and total immersion, heated screeds, and limited movement or vibration. 30 min open time — [Pro Tiler Tools](https://www.protilertools.co.uk/product/bal-spf-s1-standard-setting-flexible-wall---floor-adhesive-white-20kg)
- Timber or plywood floors: Weber says an improved flexible (S1) adhesive suits tiles up to 400x400. Larger or heavier tiles need ultra-flexible **S2**. Plywood should be at least 18mm exterior grade, primed, and screwed every 200-300mm — [Weber](https://www.uk.weber/tile-fixing/tiling-plywood); [Weber timber](https://www.uk.weber/tile-fixing/tiling-timber-or-non-tongue-and-groove-floorboards-and-chipboard)
- Rapid-set S1 products are specified for plywood overlays and timber panels (e.g. NX Semi-Rapid Flexible S1, Palace Multi-Flex rapid) — [NBS Source](https://source.thenbs.com/en/product/palace-multi-flex-rapid-setting-s1-tile-adhesive/x3aziiqZHd1dez3fsY4LH4/8pYzQFiGWXRbYLQzEzSmJd)

### Inferences
- Options: **budget** = non-flexible cement powder (e.g. Mapei Fast Set, £12.50/20kg) for ceramic on solid walls, or a ready-mixed tub for small ceramic wall tiles away from showers; **most_used** = flexible S1 powder, standard or rapid set (£19-£25/20kg); **premium** = S2 or fibre/pourable rapid flexible (e.g. BAL PTB Fibre Plus) for large format or timber floors.
- Rules that force a choice: porcelain or tiles over 300x300 need cement powder, not a tub. Floors need powder. Timber or plywood floors need flexible S1 (S2 above 400x400). Underfloor heating needs flexible. Gypsum substrates need a primer (see Q8).

## Q3. Grout (cementitious vs flexible vs epoxy; colours)

### Takeaway
The trade default is a flexible, water-repellent, mould-resistant cementitious grout (e.g. Mapei Ultracolor Plus 5kg at about £15-£22). Epoxy is the premium choice for wet rooms and floors.

### Cited Findings
- Mapei Ultracolor Plus 5kg: about £12.71 ex VAT (£15.25 inc) up to £18.49 ex VAT (£22.19 inc), depending on colour and retailer (observed 2026-10) — [Selco](https://www.selcobw.com/mapei-ultracolor-plus-5kg-527732496p); [Pro Tiler Tools](https://www.protilertools.co.uk/product/mapei-ultracolor-plus-fast-setting-water-repellent-flexible-grout-5kg--choice-of-colour-)
- Ultracolor Plus is a fast-setting flexible polymer-modified grout with water-repellent "DropEffect" and mould-resistant "BioBlock", aimed at kitchens and bathrooms. Kerapoxy is epoxy: non-absorbent and chemical- and microorganism-resistant, suitable for pools — [Mapei](https://www.mapei.com/za/en/tools-and-downloads/coloured-grouts-and-sealants)
- A UK tiler's opinion: use epoxy at least in wet-room areas and on floors, and Ultracolor Plus elsewhere — [Tilers Forums](https://tilersforums.com/threads/kerapoxy-vs-ultracolor-plus.48814)
- Mapei publishes a matching grout and sealant colour chart — [Mapei colour chart](https://www.mapei.com/za/en/tools-and-downloads/coloured-grouts-and-sealants)

### Inferences
- Options: **budget** = standard non-flexible cement grout (white or grey, own brand); **most_used** = flexible mould-resistant cement grout in white, grey or anthracite; **premium** = epoxy (e.g. Kerapoxy Easy Design). Use flexible grout wherever flexible adhesive is used (timber floors, UFH).

### Gaps
- No UK epoxy grout price found. No colour sales-share data. White, grey and dark grey are assumed common but this is unverified.

## Q5. Tanking / waterproofing (liquid kit vs sheet membrane)

### Takeaway
BS 5385-1:2018 recommends a proprietary tanking system in wet areas such as showers and wet rooms. The standard allows either a sheet membrane or a liquid-applied product. The DIY/mass default is a liquid kit with tape (about £55-£70 for about 4m2).

### Cited Findings
- BS 5385-1:2018 §6.1.1.3: "In wet areas, e.g. showers, wet rooms and steam rooms … substrates should be protected with a suitable proprietary tanking membrane system". The system must carry the load of tile and adhesive. It "can be a proprietary sheet membrane or a liquid applied water impermeable product". Water-resistant adhesives and grouts are not a substitute — [Palace Chemicals / Tile & Stone Journal (search snippet)](https://palacechemicals.co.uk/dont-get-wet-understanding-tanking/); [Instarmac, 18 Sep 2018](https://www.instarmac.co.uk/news/tanking-kits-made-compulsory-in-latest-british-standard/)
- NHBC technical guidance on substrates for tiling bath and shower enclosures (2024) exists — [NHBC 9.2.06 PDF](https://nhbc.co.uk/binaries/content/assets/nhbc/tech-guidance/9.2.06-substrates-for-tiling-bath-and-shower-enclosures-2024.pdf) (not read in full)
- Mapei Shower Waterproofing Kit (Mapegum WPS liquid membrane, primer, tape; about 4m2): £45.99 ex VAT / £55.19 inc at Selco; £70 at Wetrooms Online; AKW Mapei 5m kit £62.95 at Heat and Plumb (observed 2026-10) — [Selco](https://www.selcobw.com/mapei-shower-waterproofing-kit-527732527); [Wetrooms Online](https://www.wetrooms-online.com/products/mapei-5m-tanking-kit); [Heat and Plumb](https://www.heatandplumb.com/acatalog/akw-mapei-5m-tanking-kit-21320)

### Inferences
- Options: **most_used** = liquid tanking kit (about £55-£70 per shower); **premium** = sheet membrane (e.g. Schlüter Kerdi type) or tile-backer board plus membrane system for wet rooms; **budget** = none acceptable in a shower zone. Rule: showers and wet rooms need tanking.

## Q7. Primer

### Takeaway
Gypsum plaster, plasterboard or anhydrite substrates need a primer before cement-based adhesive. An acrylic primer such as Mapei Primer G is the common example.

### Cited Findings
- Mapei Primer G regulates absorbency and improves adhesion. Use it on gypsum plaster, gypsum panels and anhydrite screeds to prevent the sulphate reaction with cementitious adhesives. Apply neat on gypsum or 1:1 on porous cementitious substrates. Allow 30-60 min drying. Coverage about 25m2 — [Victorian Plumbing](https://www.victorianplumbing.co.uk/mapei-primer-g); [NBS Source](https://source.thenbs.com/product/primer-g/cnNNti5JxidCvNYmB9qaHS/bt6x5PppHuVFxpYXs64p1G)
- Weber: prime the back and edges of plywood before tiling — [Weber](https://www.uk.weber/tile-fixing/tiling-plywood)

### Inferences
- Options: **most_used** = acrylic tile primer (e.g. Primer G, BAL APD); **premium** = epoxy or specialist primers for non-porous or anhydrite substrates. The rule above forces a primer on gypsum or anhydrite.

### Gaps
- No primer price was captured. Search found no BAL APD detail.

## Q9. Floor finish (vinyl sheet vs LVT vs tiles; safety vinyl)

### Takeaway
Tiles are the most popular UK bathroom floor. Sheet vinyl is the cheapest waterproof option, and LVT is the mid/premium warm alternative but not for wet-room zones. Safety vinyl (R10, PTV 36+) is the wet-room and accessible option.

### Cited Findings
- Supply/fit per m2 (article dated 8 June 2026): vinyl sheet £10-£30 + £15-£25 fit, "most cost-effective waterproof option"; budget LVT £20-£35 + £15-£25; premium LVT (Karndean, Amtico) £40-£80 + £20-£35; ceramic tile £10-£25 + £25-£40; porcelain £20-£50 + £35-£55; safety flooring £25-£50 + £20-£35, R10-R12. LVT is "not suitable for shower trays, wet room zones with direct water drainage". The article states R10 minimum for domestic bathroom floors (author claim, not a cited regulation) — [Trade2Base](https://www.trade2base.com/blog/bathroom-flooring-options-pricing-uk)
- Checkatrade data quoted: LVT £15-£60/m2, UK average about £40/m2 — [MyJobQuote/Checkatrade via search](https://myjobquote.co.uk/costs/lvt-flooring)
- Polyflor Polysafe Hydro: PTV 36+ wet (slider 96), R10 (DIN 51130), Class B (DIN 51097) barefoot. For residential and commercial bathrooms and wet rooms — [Flooring King](https://flooringking.co.uk/vinyl-flooring/polyflor-vinyl-flooring/polysafe-hydro/); [NBS](https://www.nationalbimlibrary.com/polyflor/polysafe-hydro-evolve?lo=en)

### Inferences
- Options: **budget** = sheet vinyl; **most_used** = porcelain tile (or vinyl in budget rentals); **premium** = premium LVT or large-format porcelain; **safety** = Polysafe Hydro / Altro Aquarius type for wet rooms and accessible bathrooms.

### Gaps
- No Altro product data was captured. Sources for the LVT and vinyl share split are trade blogs, not sales data.

## Q10. Bathroom ceiling

### Takeaway
The two common routes are moisture-resistant plasterboard with bathroom or anti-condensation paint (default), or PVC ceiling cladding (low-maintenance upgrade, about £80 per pack covering about 7m2).

### Cited Findings
- PVC cladding is waterproof, wipe-clean, never needs repainting and is DIY-installable. Example: £79.99 per pack of 8 planks covering about 6.96m2 — [National Plastics](https://www.nationalplastics.co.uk/blog/post/the-advantages-and-disadvantages-of-pvc-ceiling-panelling.html); [Aurino](https://aurino.co.uk/ceiling-cladding-guide/)
- Plasterboard is acceptable if fitted and plastered correctly, with anti-condensation mildew-resistant paint recommended — [MyBuilder Q&A](https://www.mybuilder.com/questions/v/3077/i-have-a-enclosed-quadrant-shower-on-the-ceiling-i-have-used-ordinary-8mm-plasterboard-is-this-o-k-with-a-plaster-skim-bathroom-paint)

### Inferences
- Options: **most_used** = moisture-resistant plasterboard plus skim plus bathroom emulsion; **budget** = repaint the existing ceiling with bathroom paint; **premium/low-maintenance** = PVC ceiling cladding with integrated downlights.

### Gaps
- No share data. Sources are cladding sellers, so their claims may be biased.
