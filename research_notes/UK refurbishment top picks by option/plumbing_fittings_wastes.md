## Q1. Supply pipe: 15/22 mm copper vs plastic barrier pipe (PE-X / polybutylene)

### Takeaway
Copper (Table X, BS EN 1057) is still the trade default where pipe is visible or near the boiler and cylinder. Plastic barrier pipe (JG Speedfit PE-X/PB, Wavin Hep2O PB) is very widely stocked and is the usual choice for hidden runs under floors in refurbs. Recommend pre-selecting **copper 15/22 mm for exposed and boiler/cylinder runs, and plastic barrier pipe for concealed runs**. If a single default is needed, choose copper.

### Cited Findings
- Old survey only: Mueller Europe surveyed 2,000 IPHE members via consultants. 78% preferred copper overall and 57% preferred it for new build. 30% named the "anyone-can-fit" nature of plastic as its worst feature. Published 2007, so evidence for 2024-26 is weak. — [Heating & Ventilating Review](https://www.heatingandventilating.net/pipes-and-fittings-survey-finds-copper-is-installers-choice)
- JG Speedfit 15 mm PE-X barrier pipe at Screwfix (observed 2026-10-06): 15 mm x 3 m 10-pack £55.48 (about £1.85/m); 100 m coil £164.79 (about £1.65/m); 150 m coil £267.39. PB layflat 15 mm x 100 m £214.89. Speedfit carries a 50-year manufacturer guarantee. — [Screwfix 10-pack](https://www.screwfix.com/p/jg-speedfit-15bpex-10-3l-push-fit-pe-x-barrier-pipes-15mm-x-3m-white-10-pack/86066); [Screwfix 100 m](https://www.screwfix.com/p/jg-speedfit-pe-x-barrier-pipe-15mm-x-100m/69170); [Screwfix 150 m](https://www.screwfix.com/p/jg-speedfit-15bpex-150c-push-fit-pe-x-barrier-pipe-15mm-x-150m-white/85164); [Screwfix PB layflat](https://www.screwfix.com/p/jg-speedfit-15bpb-100c-push-fit-polybutylene-layflat-pipe-15mm-x-100m-white/33489)
- Wavin Hep2O 15 mm x 3 m PB barrier pipe at Screwfix (observed 2026-10-06): £8.59 single (about £2.86/m); 10-pack £80.63 (about £2.69/m). 50-year guarantee; fittings are demountable with the Hepkey. — [Screwfix single](https://www.screwfix.com/p/hep2o-hxx03-15ws-push-fit-barrier-pipe-15mm-x-3m-white/6009f); [Screwfix 10-pack](https://www.screwfix.com/p/hep2o-hxx03-15w-push-fit-polybutylene-barrier-pipes-15mm-x-3m-white-10-pack/5445f)
- Forum (diynot, 2008, old): one tradesperson said soldering "will always be a plumbers first choice". Another advised against soldering in floor voids because of the fire risk. — [diynot](https://www.diynot.com/diy/goto/post?id=882015)

### Inferences
- Tags: budget = PE-X barrier coil (JG Speedfit PE-X, about £1.65-1.85/m); most_used = copper 15/22 mm Table X (traditional trade default); premium / easy-remake = PB barrier (Hep2O, about £2.7-2.9/m, demountable). This tagging is my inference from price and the old survey. No 2024-26 share data was found.
- Trade-off: the flexible plastic coil feeds through joists with fewer joints, which saves labour on concealed runs. Copper is rigid and fire-safe, looks better when exposed, and is expected near boilers. Plastic generally should not be used within a set distance of the boiler; check the manufacturer's instructions.

### Gaps
- No observed 2026 price for a 15 mm x 3 m or 22 mm copper length (searches did not return a Screwfix or Toolstation copper pipe price). Copper prices also move with LME metal prices.
- No 2024-26 installer survey of copper vs plastic share.

## Q2. Joint system: compression vs push-fit vs solder (end-feed / solder-ring) vs press-fit; refurb default

### Takeaway
Plumbers mix methods by location: solder where joints are hidden and permanent, compression where they need to come apart (valves, appliances), and push-fit where speed or access matters. For a refurb product, pre-select **compression for exposed or serviceable connections and push-fit for concealed plastic runs**. Offer end-feed solder as the low-cost-per-fitting option for skilled trade. Press-fit is premium and is moving into domestic work through M-profile jaws.

### Cited Findings
- Trade guide consensus: "soldered where it's hidden and permanent, compression where it needs to come apart again, push-fit where speed and access win". Push-fit "removes the skill requirement almost entirely" but depends on fitting the pipe insert every time. Compression needs deburred, round pipe and correct tightening. — [squote.app](https://squote.app/knowledge/plumbing/push-fit-vs-compression-vs-soldered-joints)
- Forum view: avoid compression joints under floorboards because a leak cannot be seen. Push-fit or solder is preferred under floors. — [diynot (2008)](https://www.diynot.com/diy/goto/post?id=882015)
- End-feed (Endex) at Screwfix (observed 2026-10-06): 15 mm equal 90° elbows 10-pack £5.39 (about £0.54 each); 15 mm couplers 10-pack £3.29 (about £0.33 each); stop end £1.51. — [Screwfix elbows](https://www.screwfix.com/p/endex-copper-end-feed-equal-90-elbows-15mm-10-pack/88036); [Screwfix couplers](https://www.screwfix.com/p/endex-copper-end-feed-equal-couplers-15mm-10-pack/89904); [Screwfix stop end](https://www.screwfix.com/p/endex-copper-end-feed-stop-end-15mm/1010g)
- JG Speedfit 15 mm push-fit elbow: Wickes £2.65 each. On ManoMano the per-piece price is £2.98 in a 30-pack and up to £5.17 in a 3-pack (observed 2026-10-06, snippet). — [Wickes](https://www.wickes.co.uk/John-Guest-Speedfit-PEM0315WP-Elbow---15mm/p/421651); [ManoMano](https://www.manomano.co.uk/p/john-guest-speedfit-elbow-15mm-pem0315w-pack-of-30-91556177)
- Hep2O fittings are stocked at Screwfix (elbows, tees, stem elbows, stop ends), so they are a mainstream line. — [Screwfix Hep2O elbow](https://www.screwfix.com/p/hep2o-plastic-push-fit-equal-90-elbow-15mm/5295f)
- Press-fit: Conex Bänninger opened >B< Press (15-28 mm) to the domestic market after approval for M-profile jaws. It names boilers, bathrooms and kitchens as typical uses. Conex says "the UK [is] predominantly an M-profile market" and launched the <A> Press M-profile range in January 2026 with a 25-year guarantee. Pegler Yorkshire XPress offers "Leak Before Press" so unpressed joints can be spotted. — [Installer Show news (Nov 2025)](https://www.installershow.com/exhibitor-news/conex-banninger-expands-portfolio-m-profile-offering-press-copper-fittings); [ACR News](https://www.acr-news.com/sales-boost-for-conex-banninger-thanks-to-technical-teams-testing-regime); [City Plumbing XPress listing](https://www.cityplumbing.co.uk/p/pegler-yorkshire-xpress-copper-straight-female-union-connector-22mm-press-fit-x-34-inch-bsp-38834/p/735354)

### Inferences
- Tags: budget = end-feed solder (about £0.35-0.55 per 15 mm fitting, but needs a torch, solder, flux and skill, plus a fire risk in floor voids); most_used for refurb = compression (serviceable, no flame) combined with push-fit on concealed runs; premium = press-fit (fast and flame-free, but needs a press tool costing hundreds to thousands of pounds) and branded push-fit (about £2.65-3 per elbow, roughly 5-8 times end-feed).
- Cost trade-off: push-fit fittings cost several times more than end-feed, but they need no hot works, no flame permit and less skill. On a small refurb with about 10-20 joints, the extra fitting cost is roughly £25-50, which is usually less than the labour saved. This is an inference and was not sourced as a time study.

### Gaps

Travis Perkins listed a compression backplate elbow at £47.16 per bag of 10 (retail), which is not comparable. — [Travis Perkins](https://www.travisperkins.co.uk/brassware-sundries/compression-backplate-elbow-fitting-15mm-x-12in-bag-of-10/p/950278)

## Q3. Isolation valves: slotted (screwdriver) vs lever

### Takeaway
The 15 mm slotted-screwdriver compression isolating valve is the cheap, ubiquitous default under basins, WCs and appliances. Lever-handle and chrome or decorative valves are the premium option.

### Cited Findings
- Flomasta full-bore isolating valve 15 mm (slotted, ¼-turn, compression, brass, WRAS) at Screwfix: £3.55, or £3.37 each for 5+ (observed 2026-10-06, snippet). — [Screwfix](https://www.screwfix.com/p/flomasta-full-bore-isolating-valve-15mm/46860)
- Chrome isolating valves are sold by Victorian Plumbing, and BES lists a lever-operated 15 mm chrome valve with a black handle (WRAS). — [Victorian Plumbing](https://www.victorianplumbing.co.uk/pair-of-15mm-chrome-isolating-valves); [BES lever valve](https://www.bes.co.uk/lever-operated-isolating-valve-black-handle-15mm-chrome-23660)

### Inferences
- Tags: budget/most_used = slotted brass or chrome-plated compression valve (about £3-4); premium = lever-handle or decorative chrome valve (price not captured). Lever valves suit cases where users must isolate without tools, for example under sinks.

## Q5. Basin, bath and shower wastes and traps (bottle vs tubular; 32/40 mm)

### Takeaway
Sizes follow convention: 32 mm for basins, 40 mm for baths, showers and sinks. Depth of water seal: 75 mm is typical for basin traps, while shallow-seal traps suit baths. McAlpine dominates the merchant range. At Screwfix the top-rated items are a McAlpine 32 mm tubular P-trap and the A10A bottle trap, both about £6.50-8.50. Pre-select a **32 mm bottle trap** for pedestal or vanity basins (compact and easy to clean), a **tubular P-trap** where space and flow allow, and a **40 mm shallow-seal bath trap** for baths.

### Cited Findings

Top items: McAlpine adjustable inlet tubular P-trap 32 mm £8.49, 4.9★ (381 reviews); McAlpine A10A bottle trap 32 mm £7.88, 4.9★ (207 reviews); McAlpine basin bottle trap 32 mm £6.49, 4.9★ (164 reviews). — [Screwfix traps](https://www.screwfix.com/c/heating-plumbing/traps/cat831610)

## Q7. Waste pipe: solvent-weld (ABS/PVC-U) vs push-fit (PP)

### Takeaway
Sources agree that solvent weld is the "go-to" for professionals and for permanent or impact-exposed runs. Push-fit (PP, ring-seal) is cheaper, quicker, demountable and favoured for DIY and concealed internal runs. Recommend **solvent weld as the trade default** and push-fit as the budget/DIY option.

### Cited Findings
- "Solvent weld is usually the go-to system for professional plumbers". Push-fit is "best for domestic DIY" and can be taken apart. Solvent weld is recommended wherever impact is possible, for example on external walls. — [Gutter Supplies](https://guttersupplies.co.uk/soil-and-waste/push-fit-and-solvent-weld-waste-pipes-explaining-the-differences); [JDP](https://www.jdpipes.co.uk/knowledge/above-ground/push-fit-vs-solvent-weld.html)
- Push-fit systems "are cheaper, easier to work with, and quicker to install". — [easymerchant](https://www.easymerchant.co.uk/blog/difference-between-push-fit-and-solvent-weld-pipe/)

### Inferences
- Tags: budget = push-fit PP; most_used (trade) = solvent-weld ABS/MUPVC; premium = none clearly distinct (possibly a branded system with a long guarantee).

### Gaps
- No price per metre or per fitting was captured for either waste system.

## Q8. Thread sealing: PTFE tape vs jointing compound

### Takeaway
Neither product is needed on compression olives: Pegler states sealants are "not recommended" with compression fittings. In practice, PTFE tape is the default on BSP threads, and a non-setting compound (for example Boss White, Fernox LS-X) is the trade "belt-and-braces" choice when remaking old olives.

### Cited Findings
- The olive and seat form the seal, and the thread does not. Pegler says "the use of PTFE tape or any jointing compound is not recommended for use with any compression fittings". Some professionals use LS-X, or a turn of PTFE on the olive, when reusing an old nut and olive. — [diynot thread](https://www.diynot.com/diy/goto/post?id=1363418); [diynot](https://www.diynot.com/diy/goto/post?id=1787383)
- Toolstation how-to on PTFE tape use for threaded joints. — [Toolstation](https://www.toolstation.com/help-and-advice/how-to-guides/how-to-use-ptfe-tape)

### Inferences
- Tags: budget/most_used = WRAS PTFE tape (on threads); premium = WRAS jointing compound (LS-X type). Product logic should not auto-add sealant to new compression joints.

### Gaps
- No prices were captured for PTFE tape or LS-X.

## Q9. Soil stack connections

### Takeaway
To add a 32/40/50 mm waste to an existing 110 mm plastic stack, the usual retrofit is a strap-on boss with a solvent-weld or compression/ring-seal adaptor, fitted into a 54-56 mm hole. New stacks use branch or boss pipes.

### Cited Findings
- Strap-on boss: cut a 54-56 mm hole and strap the boss on. Adaptors take 32, 40 and 50 mm waste. Made from PVC-U to BS EN 1329-1. Silicone or solvent cement is used around the hole. — [Gutter Supplies FloPlast strap boss](https://www.guttersupplies.co.uk/products/soil---cast-iron-effect/strap-boss-cast-effect-soil/); [Selco](https://selcobw.com/110mm-strap-on-boss); [Wickes FloPlast SP319AG](https://www.wickes.co.uk:443/Floplast-110mm-Soil-Pipe-Strap-On-Pipe-Connector---Anthracite-Grey-SP319AG/p/277070)

### Inferences
- Tags: most_used = solvent-weld strap-on boss plus a compression or solvent waste adaptor; premium = a mechanical (rubber-seal) strap boss or a replacement multi-boss branch. Branch connections must respect Approved Document H rules on branch position relative to the WC connection. This is not verified in this research.

- No prices were captured. No Approved Document H clause was cited (search did not return the primary document). No council or social housing specifications were found for any parameter in this note.

## Summary of recommended pre-selects (inference, based on the citations above)
- Pipe: copper 15/22 mm for exposed runs; PE-X/PB barrier pipe for concealed runs.
- Joints: compression where joints are exposed or serviceable; push-fit on concealed plastic; end-feed as the trade budget option; press-fit as premium.
- Isolation valve: 15 mm slotted compression (about £3.55).
- Tap connector: 15 mm x ½" x 300 mm braided compression (about £2.14).
- Basin trap: McAlpine-type 32 mm tubular P or bottle trap (about £6.50-8.50). Bath: 40 mm shallow-seal trap.
- WC pan connector: rigid straight or small offset (about £8); flexible (about £25-28) if misaligned.
- Waste: solvent weld for trade; push-fit for DIY or budget.
- Sealant: none on compression olives; PTFE on threads.
- Stack: strap-on boss.
