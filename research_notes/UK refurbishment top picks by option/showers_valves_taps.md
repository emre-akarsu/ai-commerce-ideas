# UK refurbishment top picks: showers, shower valves and taps (budget / most_used / premium)

Scope: UK domestic bathroom refits, 2024-2026. Unless stated otherwise, prices are **observed list prices inc VAT on 2026-10-06**, read from merchant pages or from search-result snippets of those pages. Merchants change prices often, so treat every price as a dated observation, not a quote. Brands are examples only. Evidence on market share is thin and mostly comes from manufacturers. Each claim below is labelled with its source and its weakness.

## Q1. What proportion of UK homes use electric showers vs mixer showers?

### Takeaway
The only quantified split found is old. It says installed UK showers are about **46% electric, 38% mixer (no pump) and 16% pumped/power**. It is an MTP 2007 estimate, cited in a United Utilities / Waterwise report from 2007. No newer independent figure on household shares was found. Manufacturer claims from 2025-26 still describe electric showers as roughly half the market, so "about half of homes have an electric shower" is a defensible working assumption. It is not a measured 2024-26 statistic.

### Cited Findings
- "MTP (2007b) estimate that installed showers in the UK are 46% electric, 38% mixer and 16% pumped." Typical flow rates are 3-8 l/min for electric, 5-15 l/min for mixer without a pump, and 10+ l/min for pumped (text truncated in the extract). — [United Utilities / LJMU, *Water and Energy Efficient Showers: Project Report*, (c) 23 May 2007, hosted by Waterwise](https://database.waterwise.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/United-Utilities_Water-and-Energy-Efficient-Showers_Project-Report.pdf)
- The same report says an electric shower "is suitable for fitting with any plumbing system", because it heats cold mains water. It also recommends that electric showers be "strongly encouraged in preference to mixer or pumped showers" for water and energy efficiency. — [same report](https://database.waterwise.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/United-Utilities_Water-and-Energy-Efficient-Showers_Project-Report.pdf)
- The Energy Saving Trust *At Home with Water* analysis covers more than 86,000 homes. It found showers are about 25% of household water use, ahead of toilets at 22%, and that GB households spend about £2.3bn a year heating water for showers. I could not open the report page (HTTP 403), so I could not confirm a split by shower type from it. — [Energy Saving Trust, home water consumption analysis](https://energysavingtrust.org.uk/report/home-water-consumption-analysis/) (figures via search snippet)
- A Triton survey reported that 53% of respondents said they used an electric shower to manage their energy and water use. This is a manufacturer survey of attitudes, not an ownership census. — [designbuybuild.co.uk on the Triton report](https://designbuybuild.co.uk/latest-report-from-triton-showers-shows-sustainably-designed-homes-wanted-by-homebuyers/)
- Triton press release, 23 Jan 2026: it urges government to back electric showers in the Warm Homes Plan and cites Talan modelling of electric shower plus WWHRS against an 8 l/min mixer. It gives **no** figure for ownership share. — [Electrical Times](https://www.electricaltimes.co.uk/?p=34146)
- AMA Research *Shower Market Report UK 2023-2027* says the shower market fell 2% in value in 2023. It segments shower controls into electric, mixer, digital and power, but the public summary gives no shares. — [ConstructUK, 27 Jul 2023](https://constructuk.com/shower-market-report-uk-2023-2027-new-from-ama/)
- One secondary search summary said "electric showers now make up 50% of all new shower sales in the UK". It was attributed to a ShowerDoc blog that I could not open (403), so it is unverified. — [ShowerDoc blog](https://www.showerdoc.com/blog/electric-shower-units-a-friendly-guide-for-uk-homeowners)

### Inferences
- **The default shower type depends on the hot-water system and cannot be one national default.**
  - **Combi boiler** (the most common UK heating system): a thermostatic mixer bar valve, rated "HP/Combi", is the natural default. An electric shower also works and gives a backup when the boiler fails.
  - **Gravity / vented cylinder (low pressure)**: choose an electric shower, a power shower (mixer with integral pump) or a mixer plus a separate pump. Many mixers need at least 0.1 bar, and a "low pressure" or "universal" valve is needed.
  - **Unvented cylinder (mains pressure)**: a thermostatic mixer, either a bar valve or concealed. Power showers must **not** be fitted to unvented or combi systems.
  - **No stored hot water, or an extra en-suite**: electric.
- If the system is unknown, the safest single pre-selection that fits *any* home is **electric 8.5-9.5 kW**. The most common *new refit* in a combi home is probably a thermostatic bar mixer with riser kit. I found no sales data to settle which of the two outsells the other.

### Gaps
- No independent 2020-2026 survey of UK household shower-type shares was found. Candidate sources that could not be accessed or found are the EST *At Home with Water* breakdown by type, BEAMA showering data, and the AMA/Mintel full reports.
- No current sales shares for electric vs mixer units by volume.

## Q2. Which electric shower kW rating sells most?

### Takeaway
No hard sales data was found. Retailer guidance calls **8.5 kW** "the UK's most popular wattage", because it usually runs on the existing 6 mm² cable and 40 A MCB. However, the current Wickes range is weighted towards **9.5 kW**: 16 of 52 listed units, against 7 at 8.5 kW and 11 at 10.5 kW. On Wickes, the 8.5 kW and 9.5 kW versions of the same entry models carry the most reviews.

Recommended pre-select: **8.5 kW for a like-for-like replacement** where the existing circuit is not known. Offer **9.5 kW as the common upgrade** and 10.5 kW (or 10.8 kW) as premium. All of these need an electrician to check the circuit.

### Cited Findings
- "The 8.5kW electric shower is the UK's most popular wattage: it delivers reasonable performance without requiring heavy electrical work." At 8.5 kW and below a shower typically runs on 6 mm² cable with a 40 A MCB. At 9.5 kW and above it needs 10 mm² cable and a 45 A MCB, which may mean rewiring. — [QS Supplies buying guide](https://www.qssupplies.co.uk/how-to-choose-the-right-electric-shower-for-your-home.html) (retailer guide; no sales data shown)

## Q3. Bar valves, concealed valves, power showers, TMVs, taps: options and the default UK bathroom refit

### Takeaway
For a standard refit the default is:
- **chrome single-lever mono (monobloc) basin mixer**
- a **deck-mounted bath shower mixer**, or bath taps plus a separate shower
- a **thermostatic bar mixer with riser kit** where hot water is pressurised (combi or unvented)
- an **electric shower** where it is not.

Pillar taps are now the budget or traditional choice. Concealed thermostatic valves are premium. In new dwellings, Building Regulations Part G caps bath outlet temperature at 48 °C, which usually means a TMV2-type in-line blending valve. TMV3 is for healthcare and care settings.

**TMV2 vs TMV3**:
- TMV2 is the domestic scheme (BS EN 1111 / BS EN 1287) and shows compliance with Building Regulations Part G.
- TMV3 meets the NHS D08 / HTM 04-01 specification for healthcare and care settings.

— [Altecnic](https://www.altecnic.co.uk/tmv2-and-tmv3-what-do-you-know-about-them/); [Hydro-X](https://www.hydro-x.co.uk/testarea/differences-between-tmv2-tmv3-valves/); [BrodexTrident](https://www.brodextrident.com/blog/tmv2-and-tmv3-whats-the-difference)

**Part G (England)**: baths in new dwellings must have a device limiting hot water to a maximum of 48 °C, typically an in-line blending valve or TMV with a maximum temperature stop. — [Homebuilding & Renovating, Approved Document G](https://www.homebuilding.co.uk/self-build/approved-document-g); [H&V News](https://www.heatingandventilating.net/new-homes-get-temperature-limit-of-48-degrees); [Harrow Council guidance note](https://www.harrow.gov.uk/downloads/file/32471/Guidance_Note_Approved_Document_G_2010.pdf)
