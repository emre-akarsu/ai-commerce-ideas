## 1. Which IP rights (database right, copyright, trade marks) constrain retrieving, storing, combining and displaying merchant product and price data in the UK?

### Takeaway
The UK sui generis database right can stop extraction or re-utilisation of a substantial part of a merchant's catalogue (including repeated, systematic extraction of small parts), but it only protects makers who are UK or Isle of Man persons since 2021 and does not count investment in creating data such as prices. Copyright in descriptions, images and database selection or arrangement, plus communication-to-the-public and trade-mark rules, apply separately and are the main exposure when content is stored or displayed.

### Cited Findings
**Database right (Copyright and Rights in Databases Regulations 1997, SI 1997/3032)**
- Subsistence: the right arises "if there has been a substantial investment in obtaining, verifying or presenting the contents of the database", whether or not contents are copyright works — [reg 13](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1997/3032/regulation/13) (primary)
- Infringement: extracting or re-utilising "all or a substantial part" without consent; "repeated and systematic extraction or re-utilisation of insubstantial parts ... may amount to" a substantial part — [reg 16](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1997/3032/regulation/16) (primary)
- Definitions: extraction is "permanent or temporary transfer of those contents to another medium by any means or in any form"; re-utilisation is "making those contents available to the public by any means"; "substantial" means quantity or quality or both — [reg 12](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1997/3032/regulation/12) (primary)
- Term is 15 years from the end of the calendar year of completion (or first public availability), restarted by substantial change — [reg 17](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1997/3032/regulation/17) (primary)
- Qualification as amended from 31 Dec 2020: only a maker who is a UK (or Isle of Man) national or habitual resident, or a UK/Isle of Man body with a UK economic link, qualifies; EEA references were removed by SI 2019/605 (the amendment notes say "with reg. 38", i.e. transitional rules I did not analyse) — [reg 18](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1997/3032/regulation/18) (primary)
- Lawful-user protection: a lawful user of a publicly available database may "extract or re-utilise insubstantial parts ... for any purpose", and a contract term is void to the extent it prevents this — [reg 19](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1997/3032/regulation/19) (primary)
- Fair dealing for substantial parts is limited to illustration for teaching or research "not for any commercial purpose" — [reg 20](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1997/3032/regulation/20) (primary)
- Database copyright is separate: a database is original only if "by reason of the selection or arrangement of the contents" it is the author's own intellectual creation — [CDPA s3A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/3A) (primary)
- A person with a right to use a database may do what is "necessary for the purposes of access to and use of the contents", and contrary terms are void (via s296B) — [CDPA s50D](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/50D) (primary)
- Investment in creating data does not count: "obtaining" means "resources used to seek out existing independent materials and collect them in the database, and not ... the creation as such of independent materials" (CJEU BHB v William Hill, C-203/02, quoted at [33] of the Court of Appeal judgment) — [Football Dataco v Sportradar [2013] EWCA Civ 27](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2013/27) (primary)
- UK reach of foreign operators: jurisdiction exists "where a party abroad has an intention to target members of the UK public" ([16]); foreign suppliers targeting UK users were "potentially liable as primary infringers" ([93]) — same judgment (primary)
- Getty v Stability AI: Getty abandoned its database-right claim together with the training claim because there was no evidence training took place in the UK ([9]), so no UK judgment yet on database right and AI scraping — [Getty v Stability [2025] EWHC 2863 (Ch)](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/ch/2025/2863) (primary)
- French follow-up: Paris High Court, 21 Feb 2025 (leboncoin v Directannonces) held database right subsisted and that the defendant systematically extracted a substantial part (about 89% of listings), yet found no infringement because the competing service "contributes to a better structuring of the information and provides added value" (commentator's words) — [JP Karsenty commentary](https://www.jpkarsenty.com/en/database-scraping-legal/) (secondary; French law, not binding in the UK)

**Copyright, copies and display**
- The Government's March 2026 report states that if a dataset is downloaded in the UK, "section 17 CDPA ... applies to every type of copyright work and to copies stored by any means", and that making a dataset available to the public in the UK "may constitute an infringement by communication to the public under section 20 CDPA" — [UK Govt report on Copyright and AI, 18 Mar 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/report-and-impact-assessment-on-copyright-and-artificial-intelligence/report-on-copyright-and-artificial-intelligence) (primary)
- The temporary-copies exception covers a copy that is "transient or incidental", an "integral and essential part of a technological process", made solely to enable a "lawful use", with "no independent economic significance" — [CDPA s28A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/28A) (primary)
- The Supreme Court applied that exception to ordinary browsing copies (on screen and in cache): the exception "must ... apply to the ordinary technical processes associated with internet browsing" ([28]) — [PRCA v NLA [2013] UKSC 18](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/uksc/2013/18) (primary). It concerned end-user browsing, not a crawler's retained dataset.
- The Government report says developers have cited s28A and s29A but "these contain a number of conditions" and apply only where met — [UK Govt report](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/report-and-impact-assessment-on-copyright-and-artificial-intelligence/report-on-copyright-and-artificial-intelligence) (primary)

**Trade marks and comparative display**
- Using a sign "in comparative advertising in a manner that is contrary to the Business Protection from Misleading Marketing Regulations 2008" is a form of trade-mark infringement — [TMA 1994 s10(4)(e)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1994/26/section/10) (primary)
- Comparative advertising is permitted only if, among other conditions, it compares products meeting the same needs, "objectively compares one or more material, relevant, verifiable and representative features ... which may include price", does not confuse traders and does not discredit a competitor — [BPRs 2008 reg 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2008/1276/contents) (primary)

### Inferences
- Merchant-set prices are "created" data, so the Sportradar/BHB reasoning suggests the price points themselves are weak database-right subject matter; the exposure sits in extracting a merchant's compiled catalogue (attributes, structure, images) at scale, and reg 16(2) makes daily re-pulling of a small SKU set a live "repeated and systematic" question. SOLICITOR.
- Reg 18 means many large non-UK merchants may not hold a UK database right at all, but copyright, contract, confidence and unfair-competition routes remain; do not treat "no database right" as permission.
- Safest data posture: store facts (merchant SKU, price, UoM, availability, validity), not merchant prose or images; generate own templated descriptions from licensed manufacturer data; show merchant names as plain text unless the licence permits logos.
- A multi-tenant display of one merchant's data to many customers is arguably "making available to the public" for reg 12 and s20 purposes unless licensed; that is why per-source display rights belong in offer metadata (Section 6).

### Gaps
- Primary text of CJEU Innoweb (C-202/12, dedicated meta-search engines), BHB (C-203/02) and CV-Online Latvia (C-762/19) could not be opened (EUR-Lex bot challenge). I recall Innoweb treating a real-time meta-search over another site's database as extraction and re-utilisation, but I did not verify it and it is not cited as a finding. ## 2. What do website terms, robots.txt, the Computer Misuse Act 1990 and recent scraping cases mean for automated access to merchant sites?

### Takeaway
In the UK and EU the practical lever against scrapers is contract plus IP: terms can restrict automated access to unprotected data (Ryanair v PR Aviation), but cannot override lawful-user rights in protected public databases, and robots.txt is a voluntary signal, not an access control, though some terms now incorporate it. The Computer Misuse Act needs knowledge that access is unauthorised, so risk rises with blocks, credentials, circumvention or ignored cease-and-desist letters; US cases (hiQ, Meta/X v Bright Data, Amazon v Perplexity, Google v SerpApi) are mixed, not UK law, and several are unresolved.

### Cited Findings
**Contract and terms of service**
- Ryanair v PR Aviation (CJEU C-30/14, 15 Jan 2015): Directive 96/9 does not apply to a database protected by neither copyright nor the sui generis right, so its articles on lawful-user rights do not prevent contractual limits on use by third parties — [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/website-operators-can-prohibit-screen-scraping-of-unprotected-data-via-terms-and-conditions-says-eu-court-in-ryanair-case) (secondary); same holding in a search-result summary of [SCL](https://www.scl.org/3280-the-cjeu-takes-flight-databases-and-air-fares/) (secondary; page not opened). Pinsent Masons notes contractual restrictions "must conform to national laws", leaving room for unfair-competition and competition-law limits. Primary judgment: [EUR-Lex C-30/14](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62014CJ0030) (not retrievable by the fetch tool).
- UK statute for protected databases: terms that stop a lawful user taking insubstantial parts are void (reg 19(2); s50D(2) with s296B) — see Section 1 (primary)
- Germany: the Federal Court of Justice (BGH, 30 Apr 2014, I ZR 224/12) held that breaching a no-scraping term did not by itself make a flight meta-search unfair competition, and distinguished technical barriers (CAPTCHA) as potentially different — [Husovec blog](https://husovec.eu/2014/04/bgh-screen-scraping-does-not-constitute-html/) (secondary)
- Ireland: the High Court was reported as granting Ryanair a permanent injunction against online travel agents' screen scraping on terms-of-use grounds (reported Dec 2023; news only) — [Airways Magazine](https://airwaysmag.com/irish-court-screen-scraping-ota-illegal/) (secondary)
- UK merchant terms I read directly: Bearing Boys cl 7(III) "You must not conduct any systematic or automated data collection activities (including ... scraping ...) ... without our express written consent" — [Bearing Boys terms](https://www.bearingboys.co.uk/Terms--Conditions-66-a) (primary). Farnell UK API terms (trade account required; no scraping or mass capture; see Section 6) — [Farnell API terms](https://uk.farnell.com/terms-of-use-for-api) (primary).
- Earlier verification in this repo (not re-read today; Screwfix returned 403): Screwfix cl 4.2 bars "Reproduce, crawl, frame, link to or deep-link into this Website" except own personal or internal business use; Amazon UK Conditions of Use bar data mining and building a database of prices — [V9 supplier-terms verification](../../research/uk2/verify/V9-supplier-terms.md) (repo)
- Google's Terms (effective 30 Jul 2026) prohibit "using automated means to access content from any of our services in violation of the machine-readable instructions on our web pages (for example, robots.txt files that disallow crawling, training or other activities)" — [Google Terms of Service](https://policies.google.com/terms?hl=en-GB) (primary)

**robots.txt**
- RFC 9309 (IETF, Sept 2022): "These rules are not a form of access authorization", and the protocol "is not a substitute for valid content security measures" — [RFC 9309](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309.html) (primary)
- The Government report calls the Robots Exclusion Protocol "a voluntary standard but ... widely adopted by industry"; the IETF working group on finer-grained AI preferences "had not reached consensus on certain key issues such as how to treat RAG" — [UK Govt report](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/report-and-impact-assessment-on-copyright-and-artificial-intelligence/report-on-copyright-and-artificial-intelligence) (primary)
- The same report notes the EU text-and-data-mining exception requires rights to be reserved "in a machine-readable form" and that UK has no equivalent exception today — same source (primary)

**Computer Misuse Act 1990**
- s1: an offence to cause a computer to perform a function with intent to secure access to any program or data where the access is unauthorised and "he knows at the time ... that that is the case" — [CMA 1990 s1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1990/18/section/1) (primary)
- s17(5): access is unauthorised if the person "is not himself entitled to control access of the kind in question" and "does not have consent to access ... of the kind in question"; s17(8) applies a similar test to acts — [CMA 1990 s17](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1990/18/section/17) (primary)
- CPS guidance: "mere recklessness is not sufficient" for s1; Allison and Bignell show limits of authority must be clearly defined; s3 (impairment) covers intent or recklessness and can include a series of acts such as flooding — [CPS Computer Misuse Act guidance](https://www.cps.gov.uk/legal-guidance/computer-misuse-act) (primary)
- Anti-circumvention analogue: civil liability where "effective technological measures have been applied to a copyright work other than a computer program" and someone circumvents them knowing or with reason to know; measures are "effective" if use is controlled by the owner through an access control or copy control — [CDPA s296ZA](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/296ZA), [s296ZF](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/296ZF) (primary)

### Inferences
- A tenant's own trade-account or API access is clearly governed by the merchant's contract; anonymous access to public pages is where incorporation of website terms is genuinely uncertain, but ignoring an express "no automated access" term plus any technical block makes a contract, confidence or CMA 1990 argument easy for the merchant. SOLICITOR.
- Treat robots.txt as binding product policy: it is not law, but Google-style terms incorporate it and it is clear notice of the owner's wishes.
- The Amazon v Perplexity reasoning (user, not vendor, "accesses") is a CFAA argument; the UK s17(2)/(5) test turns on who "causes a computer to perform any function" and on consent, so I would not assume the same result here.
- Google v SerpApi is a US DMCA s1201 case and turns on whether the access control protects copyrighted content with the owner's authority; the UK analogue (s296ZA/s296ZF) is similar in structure. Its practical lesson for sourcing is vendor-continuity risk for SERP-scraping suppliers, not UK liability.

- No UK judgment found on scraping a merchant's public site, on terms binding an automated agent acting for a user, or on robots.txt as contract; "not found" is not proof none exists.
- Google v SerpApi: outcome of the 29 Sep 2026 hearing unknown (searches exhausted); whether the hearing took place or was moved is unverified.
- Hamburg holdings on whether natural-language terms satisfy "machine-readable"; Meta v Bright Data primary order; hiQ later history (tertiary only); Irish High Court judgment itself; any BGH 2025 decision (an aggregator claimed one; I could not verify and do not rely on it).

## 3. How do the UK text-and-data-mining exception and the 2025-26 AI and copyright developments affect using product pages to train or ground models?

### Takeaway
UK law has no commercial text-and-data-mining exception: s29A covers only copies made for computational analysis "for the sole purpose of research for a non-commercial purpose". On 18 March 2026 the Government dropped its preferred opt-out exception and proposed no reform, and its report states that copies downloaded in the UK for retrieval-augmented generation "will need to be licensed, unless the use falls within an exception". Training or grounding a commercial product on merchant pages therefore needs a licence (or facts-only extraction), whatever robots.txt says.

### Cited Findings
- s29A permits a copy only to carry out "a computational analysis of anything recorded in the work for the sole purpose of research for a non-commercial purpose" by a person with lawful access, with acknowledgement; transfer to others or other use infringes; a contract term that restricts a copy s29A permits "is unenforceable" — [CDPA s29A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/29A) (primary)
- Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 s135 required an economic impact assessment and s136 a report on use of copyright works in AI development, each within 9 months of the Act being passed; s136 must cover technical measures "for example, by web crawlers", TDM, disclosure, licensing and enforcement, including AI developed outside the UK; s136 in force 20 Aug 2025 — [s135](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/section/135), [s136](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/section/136), [s137](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/section/137) (primary)
- The report and impact assessment were published 18 Mar 2026 by DSIT, DCMS and IPO — [GOV.UK publication page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/report-and-impact-assessment-on-copyright-and-artificial-intelligence) (primary)
- Consultation outcome: 11,520 responses; the Government's preferred opt-out exception was supported by 3%, licensing-in-all-cases (option 1) by 81%; "a broad copyright exception with opt-out is no longer the government's preferred way forward"; "In the absence of any new legislation, the status quo will continue" — [UK Govt report](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/report-and-impact-assessment-on-copyright-and-artificial-intelligence/report-on-copyright-and-artificial-intelligence) (primary). A law-firm summary gives 88% for option 1; I prefer the primary 81% — [Simmons & Simmons](https://www.simmons-simmons.com/en/publications/cmkeau1lx00e0vle42b8j7ny0/ai-and-copyright-in-the-uk-where-are-we-now-and-what-is-to-come-) (secondary).
- Report proposals: gather more evidence; "not to intervene in the licensing market at this stage"; work with industry on input-transparency best practice and market-led technical standards; no new regulator; and "we do not amend copyright law in respect of systems developed outside the UK at this time. This means that the law as interpreted in the Getty Images case will continue to apply" — same report (primary)
- Grounding: "Assuming the external database comprises copyright-protected content, and copies of it are downloaded locally within the UK for processing, section 17 CDPA will apply. Those copies will need to be licensed, unless the use falls within an exception"; an implied licence may exist in some settings (its example is email) — same report, section on RAG (primary)
- The report treats crawlers used for "grounding" for RAG as a distinct use from training, and notes the proposed opt-out exception (now "no longer the government's preferred way forward") "would also apply to material used in RAG"; the Government also proposes that "no new regulator should be created specifically to oversee matters of AI as it relates to copyright" — same report (primary)
- Provider contracts add their own AI limits: Amazon's affiliate API licence bars use "in the direct training or fine-tuning of a machine learning and/or foundational model"; Google's Gemini grounding terms bar users from caching, analysing or "train[ing] on, or otherwise learn[ing] from" grounded results; Google's Terms bar "using AI-generated content from our services to develop machine learning models" (see Sections 2 and 6 for URLs)
- ICO on web-scraped personal data: lawfulness "will not be met if the scraping of personal data infringes other legislation ... such as intellectual property or contract law" — [ICO first call for evidence](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/what-we-do/our-work-on-artificial-intelligence/generative-ai-first-call-for-evidence/) (primary)

### Inferences
- For stage 2 the live question is grounding and retrieval, not training a base model: persistent storage of merchant pages or prose in prompts, vector stores or caches is copying under s17 unless licensed or confined to uncopyrightable facts; s29A is unavailable (commercial) and s28A is unlikely to cover a retained store.
- Design consequence: pass structured facts and licensed data to the model; keep merchant prose and images out of prompts unless the licence says otherwise; record per-source "may be processed by LLM" and "may be used for training" flags (default no).
- Tenants' private price data must never be used to train or fine-tune shared models; pair this with LLM-vendor terms (no training, UK/EEA processing; see repo checklist A5/A6).

### Gaps
- Court of Appeal outcome in Getty not located; whether extracting only prices and SKUs from a page via an LLM copies a "substantial part" of a work is untested.
- I searched the full text of the 18 Mar 2026 report by keyword (and read the key sections) rather than reading all 125 pages; the impact assessment was not opened.
- No IPO or Government guidance found on whether facts-only extraction by AI tools from commercial web pages needs a licence.

## 4. What do competition law, consumer and B2B price-display rules, and affiliate disclosure require when merchant prices are monitored, compared or shown?

### Takeaway
Using genuinely public price data to inform your own decisions is generally lawful, but the CMA treats algorithmic price monitoring as a hub-and-spoke risk when a third-party tool aggregates competitively sensitive, non-public information from competing users; for a buyer-side tool the exposure is pooling tenants' negotiated prices. Consumer price-display rules (DMCC Act 2024) apply only to business-to-consumer practices, but the Business Protection from Misleading Marketing Regulations 2008 and the CAP Code apply to B2B, and affiliate links must be disclosed.

### Cited Findings
**Competition law**
- Chapter I prohibits agreements, decisions and concerted practices with the object or effect of preventing, restricting or distorting competition, including those that "directly or indirectly fix purchase or selling prices"; infringing agreements are void — [Competition Act 1998 s2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1998/41/section/2) (primary)
- CMA horizontal guidance (CMA184, 16 Aug 2023): firms may independently use algorithms "to monitor the prices of competitors and to inform their own price setting", but this "could result in a concerted practice"; where third parties offer tools that help set prices "this could create a 'hub-and-spoke' structure and facilitate information exchange" (paras 8.25-8.26) — [CMA184 PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/64dba33bc8dee400127f1d25/Horizontal_Guidance_FINAL.pdf) (primary); [CMA page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/guidance-on-horizontal-agreements)
- "Genuinely public" information is "readily accessible (in terms of costs of access) to all competitors and customers" and exchanges of it are unlikely to infringe (para 8.36); the more granular and attributable the data, the more sensitive; "genuinely aggregated" data is less likely to restrict competition (paras 8.46-8.47) — same (primary)
- Indirect exchange via "a platform operator or optimisation tool provider" can make competitors and the third party liable; "While using publicly available data to feed algorithmic software is legal, the aggregation of competitively sensitive information into a pricing tool offered by a single IT company to which various competitors have access could amount to horizontal collusion" (paras 8.66-8.69) — same (primary)
- CMA Trod/GB eye (case 50223, infringement decision 12 Aug 2016): two online sellers used automated repricing software to implement a no-undercutting cartel on Amazon Marketplace; Trod fined £163,371 after 20% settlement discount; GB eye got leniency — [CMA case page](https://www.gov.uk/cma-cases/online-sales-of-discretionary-consumer-products); [CMA press release](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/online-seller-admits-breaking-competition-law) (primary)
- CMA research paper "Algorithms: How they can reduce competition and harm consumers" (19 Jan 2021) identifies collusion via algorithms as a harm — [CMA paper page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/algorithms-how-they-can-reduce-competition-and-harm-consumers) (primary)
- Joint purchasing example: where an independent third party negotiates for competing buyers and the buyers' specifications, quantities and maximum prices are "only disclosed to the third party who has measures in place to ensure it is not shared with the other manufacturers", the CMA considered the information exchange unlikely to restrict competition (Example 7, p142-144) — CMA184 (primary)

**Consumer rules and B2B boundary**
- DMCC Act Part 4 Chapter 1 (unfair commercial practices) protects "consumers", defined as "an individual acting for purposes that are wholly or mainly outside the individual's business" (s225(3)); in force 6 Apr 2025 — [DMCC Act s225](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/section/225) (primary)
- An invitation to purchase must give the "total price of the product", which "includes any fees, taxes, charges or other payments that the consumer will necessarily incur"; omitting material information is unlawful — [DMCC Act s230](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/section/230) (primary)
- CMA207 (18 Nov 2025): a trader selling "specialist tractor parts to businesses only" need not consider the UCP provisions but "should however consider" the BPRs 2008 (p12); "price comparison websites" are among those responsible for compliance of an invitation to purchase even if they do not sell (para 4.13, p37); drip pricing is prohibited and detailed in CMA209 — [CMA207 PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/691b9bd821ef5aaa6543ee6f/Unfair_commercial_practices_CMA207_18_Nov_2025__2_.pdf) (primary); [CMA209 page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/price-transparency-cma209)
- CMA's first direct enforcement under the new powers was announced 18 Nov 2025; it can fine "up to 10% of global turnover" — [CMA press release](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-launches-major-consumer-protection-drive-focused-on-online-pricing-practices) (primary)
- B2B: "Advertising which is misleading is prohibited"; it is an offence for a trader to engage in it; "the price or manner in which the price is calculated" is a listed matter — [BPRs 2008 regs 3, 6](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2008/1276/contents) (primary)

**Advertising and affiliate rules (ASA/CAP)**
- The CAP Code treats as a consumer "anyone who is likely to see a given marketing communication, whether in the course of business or not", and its scope includes "preferential listings on price comparison sites" — [CAP Code scope](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_folder/scope-of-the-code.html) (primary)
- Rule 2.1: "Marketing communications must be obviously identifiable as such" — [CAP Code section 2](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/02.html) (primary)
- Price rules: quoted prices must include non-optional taxes, "However, VAT-exclusive prices may be given if all those to whom the price claim is clearly addressed pay no VAT or can recover VAT", with a prominent VAT statement (3.18); comparisons must "objectively compare one or more material, relevant, verifiable and representative feature" (3.34); price comparisons must make "the basis of the comparison clear" (3.38) — [CAP Code section 3](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/03.html) (primary)
- ASA/CAP affiliate guidance (last updated 22 Mar 2023): "Both the business and the affiliate marketer are responsible under the Code"; use an upfront "Ad" label, as "affiliate" alone is unclear (wording as returned by the fetch tool) — [ASA affiliate marketing guidance](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/affiliate-marketing.html) (primary)

### Inferences
- The competition risk in this product is not scraping public prices; it is any feature that pools tenants' non-public negotiated prices or lets one merchant see another's quote. Tenants (refurbishment contractors) compete downstream, so shared cost data is the sensitive item; keep it tenant-private (repo rule R10) and never relay one merchant's quote content to another. SOLICITOR if any benchmark or "market price" feature is ever planned.
- If the product stays B2B-only, DMCC UCP rules are mostly out of scope but BPRs 2008 and CAP still bite on "best price" marketing claims; a sole trader or homeowner can be a "consumer", so onboarding must gate this. If consumers are admitted, total-price, drip-pricing and comparison-site duties arrive (CMA207 para 4.13).
- Commission-driven ranking conflicts with a "best price" promise; if affiliate links are ever used, label them upfront, disclose the commercial relationship, and rank on price/lead time, not commission.

### Gaps
- No CMA or ASA guidance found specific to buyer-side price-comparison tools in B2B markets; the CMA's earlier digital comparison tools market-study page could not be retrieved (guessed URL failed).
- Hub-and-spoke through a buyer-side platform is untested in UK case law as far as I found; Argos/JJB and Eturas not retrieved (CMA184 states the principle).
- Price Marking Order 2004, Consumer Rights Act and CAP B2B-specific advice not checked.

## 5. How should negotiated trade prices, trade-account terms, multi-tenant handling and personal data be treated?

### Takeaway
Customer-specific trade prices are confidential business information that can qualify as trade secrets and are often wrapped in contractual confidentiality, internal-use-only and no-aggregation clauses, so they should be tenant-private by default and never pooled, benchmarked or used to train shared models. UK GDPR is relevant only to personal data (named contacts) inside feeds, price files or pages.

### Cited Findings
- A "trade secret" is information that is secret, has commercial value because it is secret, and has been "subject to reasonable steps ... to keep it secret"; acquisition, use or disclosure is unlawful where it "constitutes a breach of confidence" — [Trade Secrets (Enforcement, etc.) Regulations 2018 regs 2-3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/597/regulation/2) (primary)
- Breach of confidence "is based ultimately on conscience"; the classic case is information received "in circumstances where she had agreed, or ought to have appreciated, that it was confidential", and liability can start from the moment a recipient is told or appreciates it is confidential — [Vestergaard v Bestnet [2013] UKSC 31, paras 22-25](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/uksc/2013/31) (primary)
- Farnell's UK API terms show typical trade-account wrapping: API access needs a trade account with "pay by trade account"; the user may not "aggregate Farnell's data other than for Your internal business use" or "with data of Farnell's competition other than for Your internal business use"; "Farnell shall be the sole and exclusive owner of all data and content"; mutual confidentiality; delete cached data and "adduce evidence" on request; English law — [Farnell API terms](https://uk.farnell.com/terms-of-use-for-api) (primary)
- The element14 developer docs separate "generic access" from "contract pricing", which is "customer specific" and requires a CustomerID and Secret Key obtained from the local sales representative — [element14 Product Search API](https://partner.element14.com/docs/Product_Search_API_REST__Description) (primary)
- PunchOut lets buyers "access supplier contracts" on the supplier's site, with "customer-specific content (contract pricing)", and "PunchOut websites allow access only to known, authenticated buying organizations" (guide dated 2018; cXML 1.2.071 was last updated 14 Aug 2026) — [cXML User's Guide](http://xml.cxml.org/current/cXMLUsersGuide.pdf); [cXML.org](https://cxml.org/) (primary)
- UK merchant quotes are often weak commitments: Würth quotes are an "invitation to treat" valid 30 days; Hayley quotes "not valid unless signed"; Edmundson quotes subject to written confirmation; RS "My Quotes" prices fixed for 30 days — [claims ledger UK-ACC-04](../../docs/uk/03-claims-ledger.md) and [V9 verification](../../research/uk2/verify/V9-supplier-terms.md) (repo)
- Prior repo research found Farnell and Amazon APIs "are not drop-in for a multi-tenant agent" (own trade account and key; cache caps; flow-down to third-party software) — [research/uk2/04-supplier-access-and-terms.md](../../research/uk2/04-supplier-access-and-terms.md) (repo)
- Product rule R10 (spec): only structured fields, "not free text, not vendor contract prices", enter shared datasets, only with recorded consent — [04-product-spec.md section 4](../../docs/product/04-product-spec.md) (repo)
- Personal data is "any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person"; where data were not obtained from the person, Art 14 notice duties apply, with a disproportionate-effort exception now in Art 14(5)(e) — [UK GDPR Art 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/4), [Art 14](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/14) (primary)
- ICO: scraping is "invisible processing" and legitimate interests can be a lawful basis only if the three-part test is passed — [ICO first call for evidence](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/what-we-do/our-work-on-artificial-intelligence/generative-ai-first-call-for-evidence/) (primary)

### Inferences
- Two confidentiality owners exist: the tenant (its negotiated rates) and the merchant (its price list and terms). The platform needs both the tenant's warranty that it may upload or share the data and, ideally, merchant consent for multi-merchant display. SOLICITOR.
- Treat "reasonable steps to keep it secret" as an engineering requirement (row-level security, per-tenant keys, no cross-tenant caches, LLM vendor no-training and zero-retention terms, access logging); this supports trade-secret status and answers customer due diligence.
- Competition design pattern from CMA184 Example 7 maps cleanly: each tenant's data goes only to the platform, which never shares it onward.
- UK GDPR: store only business contact fields needed; keep personal data out of shared datasets; existing repo checklist items A5, A7, A8 already cover roles, PECR and retention.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve a UK merchant trade-account agreement that expressly makes negotiated prices confidential; the actual account terms of each pilot merchant must be read. SOLICITOR/tenant.
- ICO position on B2B contact data in scraped or supplied files, and PECR for B2B contacts, not re-checked here (see repo checklist A5, A7).

## 6. What conditions do APIs and feeds typically impose, and what provenance, freshness and licence metadata must every offer carry?

### Takeaway
Real API and feed licences impose short cache windows (24-48 hours), mandatory refresh and deletion, timestamp and disclaimer display next to prices, source attribution, own-site-only display, and bans on sublicensing, aggregation, analysis, price tracking and model training. Every stored offer therefore needs machine-readable provenance, freshness, licence and tenant-scope fields so the system can enforce those limits and prove what it showed and why.

### Cited Findings
**Amazon (UK Associates programme policies and API licence, updated 14 Apr 2026; illustrative, B2C affiliate)**
- Licence is "limited, revocable, non-transferable, non-sublicensable, non-exclusive" to "copy and display Program Content solely on your Site"; no sale, resale, redistribution or sublicence of content or apps displaying it — [Associates Program Policies](https://affiliate-program.amazon.co.uk/help/operating/policies) (primary)
- Caching: images may not be stored (links up to 24 hours); other content may be stored "for up to 24 hours" and then must be refreshed; ASINs may be stored indefinitely — same (primary)
- Display: a date/time stamp beside price and availability if data come from feeds or are refreshed less than hourly, with a stated disclaimer that prices are "accurate as of the date/time indicated and are subject to change" — same (primary)
- Use limits: no use "for the purpose of aggregating, analyzing, extracting, or repurposing" content, or "in the direct training or fine-tuning of a machine learning and/or foundational model" without written approval; "your Site must not have price tracking and/or price alerting functionality"; prices shown only if Amazon serves the link or they come through the API; price-comparison formats must show Amazon's lowest new price — same (primary)
- Disclosure: "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases" must be stated — [Associates Operating Agreement](https://affiliate-program.amazon.co.uk/help/operating/agreement) (primary)
- The Creators API replaces PA-API 5.0 (deprecation 30 Apr 2026, endpoint retirement 15 May 2026 per a developer write-up) and requires Associates enrolment and "at least 10 qualifying sales within the past 30 days" — [Creators API intro](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/creatorsapi/docs/en-us/introduction) (primary); [DEV Community](https://dev.to/th3nate/amazon-pa-api-v5-is-shutting-down-april-30-2026-here-is-what-changes-at-the-auth-layer-22ek) (secondary)

**Farnell/element14 (UK API terms, undated)**
- Trade account required; prohibited: scraping or "capture of mass data", creating "a database by systematically downloading and storing content"; caching or keeping copies "longer than forty-eight (48) hours", delete at least monthly with evidence; no aggregation with competitors' data beyond internal business use; no passing API content to other parties; "mark and clearly identify Farnell as the source"; API key sharing barred — [Farnell API terms](https://uk.farnell.com/terms-of-use-for-api) (primary)
- "IMPORTANT NOTE: Anyone with access to the API key can place legally binding orders on Your trade account"; third-party software providers allowed only if bound by terms "at least as protective" — same (primary)
- The global element14 Partner Portal terms (July 2019) are stricter (no storing of Farnell content; principal purpose must be marketing Farnell) and it is unclear which governs a UK trade-account user — [V9 verification](../../research/uk2/verify/V9-supplier-terms.md) (repo)

**Search and grounding APIs**
- Google Gemini "Grounding with Google Search" (terms effective 23 Mar 2026): use only in an app you own and display grounded results with Search Suggestions to the prompting user; users may not "cache, frame, syndicate, resell, analyze, train on, or otherwise learn from" results or use Links to build an index or identify pages to crawl; limited storage up to two years for display optimisation and chat history — [Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms) (primary)
- Google's Custom Search JSON API is "closed to new customers"; existing customers must transition by 1 Jan 2027 — [Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/custom-search/v1/overview) (primary). Bing Search APIs retired 11 Aug 2025, replaced by "Grounding with Bing Search" in Azure AI Agents — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/lifecycle/announcements/bing-search-api-retirement) (primary)
- SERP access is being tightened by Google: it removed the num=100 parameter on 14 Sep 2025 (tools now need ten requests where one sufficed) and reportedly blocked vendor workarounds within days in 2025, so SERP-based price discovery has unstable cost and coverage — [PPC Land, 12 Aug 2026](https://ppc.land/serpapi-faces-revived-google-scraping-claims-built-on-reddit-licensing-terms/) (secondary)
- SERP-scraping vendor risk allocation (vendor marketing, partisan): SerpApi's "U.S. Legal Shield" offers up to $2M cover but only for "claims brought under U.S. state or federal law in a U.S. court"; claims "under foreign law or brought in non-U.S. jurisdictions fall outside" it; and it covers scraping only "as long as your use of the data or service is not illegal" (its examples of illegal use include copyright infringement and DMCA violations) — [SerpApi US Legal Shield](https://serpapi.com/us-legal-shield) (primary, partisan)

**Standards and industry channels**
- PunchOut and EDI exist at several UK suppliers (RS, Farnell, Rexel, Cromwell, Würth) — [claims ledger UK-ACC-06](../../docs/uk/03-claims-ledger.md) (repo); protocol background in Section 5 (cXML guide)

**Not retrieved:** eBay API licence (URL returned 404), affiliate-network publisher terms (Awin page returned almost no text), Octopart/Nexar terms.

## 7. Which acquisition methods can feed a quote line (a price the buyer might rely on), and which should only ever be indicative? (risk-ranked)

### Takeaway
Rank from lowest to highest combined legal and terms risk: (1) manual quote or phone call, (2) trade-account feed, EDI or PunchOut, (3) user-supplied price files, (4) official merchant API or feed, (5) affiliate feed, (6) third-party search or shopping API, (7) direct scraping. Only methods 1 and 2 (and 3 under conditions) can feed a quote line; 4 to 6 are indicative only; 7 should not be used in production without the merchant's written permission, and conflicts with the product's own rule of no scraping or link-fetching.

### Cited Findings
- Merchants' own quote terms limit how "firm" even a merchant-issued price is (invitation to treat; validity periods; written confirmation) — Section 5 (repo ledger UK-ACC-04)
- Product design rules in this repository already exclude scraping and third-party link fetching (R7) and set the price base to customer price lists and received quotes only (D21) — [docs/MASTER.md](../../docs/MASTER.md) and [04-product-spec.md](../../docs/product/04-product-spec.md) (repo); the spec says vendor content is untrusted input (R6)
- Prior repo research found automated-access bans at Screwfix, Bearing Boys, Amazon UK and Farnell, and no supplier term restricting receipt of an emailed RFQ — [docs/uk/00-uk-market-gaps.md section 4.2](../../docs/uk/00-uk-market-gaps.md) (repo)
- Supporting legal and terms evidence for each row: Sections 1 and 2 (scraping and database right), Section 6 (API and feed conditions), Section 5 (trade accounts, PunchOut), Section 4 (affiliate and consumer rules)

### Inferences
**Ranking table (analyst judgement, not legal advice; 1 = lowest risk). Legal and terms risk are rated Low/Medium/High; reliability and freshness describe typical behaviour, not measured data.**

| Rank | Method | Legal risk | Terms risk | Reliability | Freshness | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Manual quote or phone call (a human asks the merchant; merchant confirms in writing) | Very low | Very low (merchant gives price knowingly; check any "confidential" marking) | High if written and dated; low if verbal only | Quote date; good only for the merchant-stated validity | **Quote line** when written, dated, with validity and basis; verbal = indicative until confirmed |
| 2 | Trade-account feed, EDI or PunchOut (tenant's own account and credentials; contract prices) | Low | Medium: internal-use-only, no aggregation with competitor data, 24-48h caches, key = authority to bind, platform is a "third-party software provider" needing flow-down | High (authoritative, customer-specific) | Real time (session) or per EDI schedule | **Quote line** if offer carries validity and the licence allows tenant-private display; never shared across tenants |
| 3 | User-supplied price files (tenant uploads a merchant price list, quote PDF or CSV) | Low-medium: confidentiality and right to share; no access issue | Low (our terms) but merchant-tenant confidentiality unknown | Variable: staleness, transcription | As old as the file; validity often unstated | **Quote line only** if file names merchant, shows validity date and basis, and tenant attests; otherwise indicative; tenant-private only |
| 4 | Official merchant API or feed (public or partner access, not account-specific) | Low | Medium-high: cache windows, display and attribution, no aggregation or sublicensing, "principal purpose" clauses | High for list price and stock; may differ from the buyer's trade price | Near real time within cache cap | **Indicative** (list price); becomes method 2 if it returns account-specific contract prices with validity |
| 5 | Affiliate feed | Low (licensed) but ad-disclosure and conflict-of-interest risk | Medium: network and merchant programme terms (refresh, link-back, no price tracking, no ML use) | Medium: retail list prices, often VAT-inclusive; thin MRO coverage | Daily-ish | **Indicative only**; label as affiliate; do not rank by commission |
| 6 | Third-party search or shopping API (SERP-scraping vendors; or licensed grounding APIs) | Medium-high: vendor in litigation (Google v SerpApi), upstream ToS and DMCA-style claims, copyright in snippets and images; vendor cover limited to US claims | High for scraping vendors (upstream terms breached by vendor, silent downstream); licensed grounding APIs bar caching and analysis | Low-medium: parsing errors, merchant and price mismatches | Variable (cached results) | **Indicative lead only** (merchant discovery); never shown as a quote; do not build a price database from it |
| 7 | Direct scraping of public merchant pages | High: website terms, possible database right and copyright, CMA 1990 if blocks or notices ignored, s3 risk from load, anti-circumvention analogue; breaches product rule R7 | High | Low-medium: layout changes, anti-bot, JS | Potentially fresh but fragile | **Never** in production without written merchant permission; if ever piloted, a separate ingestion job (not the runtime agent), indicative only |

**Quote-line gate (all must hold):** merchant-originated and addressed to this buyer or an authenticated buyer-specific price; merchant-stated validity not expired; licence permits storage and display to this tenant for this purpose; commercial basis complete (UoM, currency, VAT basis, delivery, MOQ and breaks); provenance and licence metadata present and grounding check passed; human approval before any PO (R1). Anything failing a condition is **indicative** and must be labelled "indicative, not a quote" with its observed_at.

**Conditions and mitigations by method**
- Manual quote or phone: log who, when, channel; confirm by email with validity and VAT basis; store the merchant's reply as the evidence; no personal data beyond business contacts.
- Trade-account feed, EDI, PunchOut: bring-your-own-credentials per tenant; written confirmation from the merchant that a platform acting for the tenant is acceptable; read-only credentials where offered (an API key can bind the account, so the agent must have no order path); honour cache and deletion limits automatically; keep terms snapshot and acceptance record; contract prices never leave the tenant.
- User-supplied files: tenant warranty and indemnity in customer terms; parse in the no-network sandbox with the quarantined extractor and grounding check; require validity date or prompt for it; delete on exit; never use for cross-tenant benchmarks.
- Official API or feed: written licence per merchant covering multi-merchant display, caching, LLM processing and multi-tenant use; implement attribution, timestamp and disclaimer displays; respect rate limits; kill switch per source; monitor terms changes (Amazon updated Apr 2026; PA-API retired May 2026).
- Affiliate feed: only if the commercial model requires it; "Ad" or affiliate labelling upfront; neutral ranking; B2B-versus-consumer display check; do not use for firm lines.
- Search or shopping API: contract warranties on lawful collection and UK-law indemnity (vendor cover is US-only); store only facts and the source reference; treat results as untrusted (R6); plan for vendor loss (Google v SerpApi pending; Google CSE closing 1 Jan 2027; Bing retired); do not use grounding-API output as a database (terms ban caching and analysis).
- Scraping: obtain written permission and move to method 4; otherwise do not do it.

### Gaps
- Reliability and freshness entries are qualitative; no measurements of UK MRO merchant feed refresh rates or error rates were collected.
- The relative order of ranks 2, 3 and 4 depends on merchant-specific terms; ranks could swap after reading actual agreements.

## 8. What should a solicitor review before launch?

### Takeaway
A focused list of about 25 questions on database right and copyright, contract and access, AI and TDM, competition and confidentiality, price-display and advertising, and vendor contracts, with the most launch-critical (P1) first. It extends the existing repo checklist (docs/uk/04-counsel-and-adviser-checklist.md, items A5, A7, C2, C4, D1).

### Cited Findings
- The repo already carries UK counsel questions on GDPR roles (A5), PECR (A7), licensed reference data (C2), TDM watch (C4) and insurance (D1) — [docs/uk/04-counsel-and-adviser-checklist.md](../../docs/uk/04-counsel-and-adviser-checklist.md) (repo)
- Basis for each question is the cited material in Sections 1 to 7 (the section number follows each question).

### Inferences
Priority: P1 blocks any stage 2 source going live; P2 needed before scaling; P3 monitor. All SOLICITOR items.

**A. Database right and copyright (Section 1)**
1. P1. For each pilot merchant, is its catalogue and price data protected by UK database right (reg 13, reg 18 qualification, "spin-off" exclusion for price data) or database copyright (s3A)? What volume or frequency of lookups becomes "substantial" or "repeated and systematic" (reg 16)? Does showing one merchant's data to many tenants count as re-utilisation (reg 12, s20)?
2. P1. What may we lawfully store and display from merchant pages and feeds: facts only (SKU, price, UoM, stock, validity) versus descriptions, images, merchant logos? Draft a data-minimisation rule and image policy.
3. P2. Trade-mark and comparative-advertising limits when naming merchants in comparisons (TMA s10(4)(e), BPRs reg 4).
4. P3. Weight of CJEU case law (Innoweb, CV-Online, BHB) in UK database-right disputes after Brexit and effect of SI 2019/605 transitional rules.

**B. Contract and access (Section 2)**
5. P1. Are website terms that bar automated access (for example Screwfix 4.2, Bearing Boys 7(III)) enforceable against the platform, the tenant, or both, when an agent never clicks "accept"? Interaction with reg 19(2) and s50D (lawful-user carve-outs) and with Ryanair v PR Aviation.
6. P1. For trade-account and API terms (for example Farnell UK): is a platform acting for a tenant within "internal business use", is it a "third-party software provider" needing flow-down, and may it show one merchant's prices beside another's despite no-aggregation clauses? What written confirmations to obtain from each merchant?
7. P1. Computer Misuse Act 1990 and anti-circumvention: confirm a firm policy that the product never bypasses technical measures, never ignores cease-and-desist letters, and never accesses logged-in areas it is not entitled to; assess s3 exposure from request rates; confirm no use of anti-bot evasion by any vendor we use.
8. P2. Whether robots.txt and AI-use reservations should be treated as binding policy, given Google-style terms that incorporate them.
9. P2. Agent-acting-for-user liability under UK law (the Amazon v Perplexity reasoning is US CFAA): who "causes the computer to perform the function" under s17.

**C. AI, TDM and licensing (Section 3)**
10. P1. Does runtime grounding or storing merchant content in prompts, caches or vector stores involve s17 copying needing a licence (the Government's own statement on RAG)? Draft the licence language needed for "store", "display", "process by LLM", "no training" for each source.
11. P2. Is facts-only extraction by an LLM from a copyrighted page "copying a substantial part"? Position if model processing happens outside the UK (Getty territoriality; appeal pending).
12. P3. Monitor Getty Court of Appeal, any 2026-27 UK reform (Government report 18 Mar 2026) and EU decisions (Like Company C-250/25).

**D. Competition (Section 4)**
13. P1. Confirm no feature pools, benchmarks or exposes tenants' non-public negotiated prices, and that no merchant-facing analytics will be offered; design guardrails against a hub-and-spoke finding (CMA184 paras 8.66-8.69). If a benchmark is ever wanted, what aggregation, historic delay and minimum-contributor rules would be safe?
14. P2. RFQ flows: may the product tell Merchant B about Merchant A's quote, and under which merchant confidentiality terms; concerted-practice risk from automated multi-merchant RFQs.

**E. Consumer, B2B and advertising (Section 4)**
15. P1. Confirm B2B-only scope and gating (sole traders versus consumers, mixed-use purchasers); if any consumer ever sees prices, what DMCC s230, drip-pricing and price-comparison duties apply (CMA207 para 4.13, CMA209).
16. P1. Review of marketing claims such as "best price" or "savings" against BPRs 2008 (regs 3-4, offence in reg 6) and CAP rules 3.17-3.18, 3.34, 3.38-3.39; VAT-exclusive display wording for business audiences.
17. P2. If any affiliate or commission arrangement is contemplated: disclosure wording (CAP 2.1; ASA guidance), neutrality of ranking, and whether it undermines the buyer-agent role.

**F. Confidentiality and data (Section 5)**
18. P1. Customer terms: tenant warranties that it may upload trade prices and credentials; merchant confidentiality clauses that might forbid sharing with a platform or LLM sub-processor; indemnities; liability cap.
19. P1. Trade-secret and breach-of-confidence posture: are our security measures "reasonable steps" (Trade Secrets Regs reg 2); incident response if one tenant's prices leak to another; contract with the LLM provider (no training, retention, UK/EEA processing).
20. P2. UK GDPR for contact data inside feeds and files (roles, Art 14 notice, retention); align with checklist A5, A7, A8.

**G. Reliance, records and vendor contracts (Sections 6 and 7)**
21. P1. Liability for displayed prices: wording of "indicative" versus "quote", disclaimers (UCTA reasonableness), negligent misstatement exposure, and how merchant terms (invitation to treat, 30-day validity) interact with our labelling; insurance wording (D1).
22. P1. Licence-deletion duties (for example Farnell monthly deletion with evidence, Amazon 24-hour refresh) versus the six-year audit log: what can be retained (hashes, metadata) and for how long.
23. P2. If a third-party search or shopping API vendor is used: warranties on lawful collection, indemnities under English law (vendor cover is US-only), termination rights, sub-processors, and a fallback plan.
24. P2. Operating policy: takedown and cease-and-desist procedure, per-source kill switch, terms-change monitoring cadence, and keeping acceptance records and terms snapshots.
25. P3. Scotland and Northern Ireland differences in contract and confidentiality law (profiles are unreviewed).

### Gaps
- This list is research-derived and not exhaustive; it assumes B2B-only, UK-hosted, tenant-private pricing and no merchant-facing features. Different product choices change the questions.
