# Product identity and product-data sources for matching UK trade order lines to catalogue SKUs (verification of the matching spec's reference layer)

Research date: 2026-10-06 (UK). Scope: extends `research_notes/UK refurbishment job templates data/classification_standards.md` (Uniclass/ETIM/bSDD basics not repeated). Spec under review: `docs/product/07-product-matching-engine-spec-v2.md`.

Label key used on every finding: **[V]** = primary page/API read in this session (or a primary document fetched and text-extracted); **[U]** = unverified (search-engine extract, vendor-summary only, blocked page, or inferred from a URL). Quotes are short and sourced. Counts marked "computed" were produced by me from the cited public endpoint on 2026-10-06 (bSDD API, public sitemaps, Wikidata API) and are approximate where stated.

Access limits that affect confidence (details in the Gaps sections): gs1uk.org and gs1.org are behind bot protection (HTTP 403 / Imperva interstitial), so GS1 UK costs are secondary; Icecat's `robots.txt` forbids its search/REST endpoints and AI crawlers, so its brand list was not queried and nothing was registered; the web-search budget ran out part-way, so some leads (Trade Data Services, BEAMA/ECMA, BMF Product Data Templates) could not be chased; Wikidata's SPARQL endpoint rate-limited to 1 request/minute after the first query.

## 1. Identifiers and who supplies them: GTIN/EAN (GS1 UK, Verified by GS1, GEPIR, Digital Link), manufacturer part numbers, merchant SKU conventions

### Takeaway
GTINs are issued to brand owners by GS1 member organisations, and the free lookup route is tiny (30 GTIN queries per 24 hours) and returns identity attributes (brand, description, category, content), not size, grade, class or pack. The identifiers that make a catalogue row matchable (merchant SKU + manufacturer part number + EAN + dimensions + pack quantity) come from the merchant's own product record: Jewson's public product pages carry all of them, Wickes' structured data carries only SKU, brand and price, and merchant SKU formats are merchant-specific so they cannot be cross-merchant keys.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Verified by GS1 on gs1.org allows "up to 30 single GTIN queries in any 24-hour period"; beyond that the user is told to contact their GS1 Member Organisation — [GS1 support: query limit](https://support.gs1.org/support/solutions/articles/43000734075-how-many-queries-can-a-user-perform-using-the-verified-by-gs1-service-on-gs1-org-)
- [V] Verified by GS1 "answers the question: 'Is this the product that I think it is?'"; the public gets 30 queries/day, while GS1 members get "more advanced querying capabilities like an API connection" through their member organisation (no price given) — [GS1 support: what is Verified by GS1](https://support.gs1.org/support/solutions/articles/43000734077-what-is-verified-by-gs1-)
- [V] The legacy GEPIR address `gepir.gs1.org/index.php/about` now returns a 301 to `gs1.org/services/verified-by-gs1/gepir-transition` tagged `utm_campaign=vbg_replaces_gepir`, i.e. GEPIR has been replaced by Verified by GS1 (destination page itself returned HTTP 403 to my fetcher, so the replacement is read from the redirect, not the page text) — [GEPIR about (redirect)](https://gepir.gs1.org/index.php/about)
- [U] GS1 UK's Verified by GS1 page (search-engine extract only): six globally verifiable attributes (brand name, product description, product image, global product category, net content/unit of measure, target market); search limited to 30/day; "premium" via support@gs1uk.org; a GTIN Check API is offered only to the "GS1 UK partner community" — [GS1 UK Verified by GS1](https://www.gs1uk.org/standards-services/data-services/verified-by-gs1), [GS1 UK GTIN Check API](https://gs1uk.org/standards-services/data-services/gtin-check-api)
- [U] GS1 UK membership/GTIN licence fees (search-engine extract of the page; annual, ex VAT, by turnover): up to £99,999 = £50 for 10 GTINs; £100k-£499,999 = £100 for 100; £500k-£999,999 = £130 for 1,000; £1m-£9.99m = £200 for 1,000; £10m-£49.99m = £450 for 10,000; £50m-£249.99m = £1,750 for 10,000; £250m-£999.99m = £3,250 for 100,000; over £1bn = £4,250 for 100,000. GTINs are licensed annually with membership — [GS1 UK membership cost](https://www.gs1uk.org/knowledge-hub/membership/how-much-does-gs1-uk-membership-cost)
- [V] GS1 Digital Link: the GS1-conformant resolver standard (Release 1.2.1, ratified August 2026) defines link types such as `gs1:pip` (product information page); a free GS1 resolver exists at `id.gs1.org`, and a GTIN is expressed as `.../01/{gtin}` — [GS1 resolver standard](https://ref.gs1.org/standards/resolver/)
- [U] GS1 UK publishes a "how to use the GS1 UK resolver service" guide (page not readable through bot protection) — [GS1 UK resolver guide](https://www.gs1uk.org/standards-services/get-market-ready/connect-to-consumer/guides-resources/how-to-use-the-gs1-uk-resolver-service)
- [V] ETIM is attributes only: it "does not include, for example, manufacturers' part numbers, prices or illustrations" — [EDA white paper "Starting Your ETIM Journey" (V2, Nov 2025)](https://www.eda.org.uk/starting-your-etim-journey-v2-nov-2025/); the ETIM UK FAQ says the same ("no provision for pricing data, no pictures, or free text") — [ETIM UK FAQ](https://www.etim-uk-and-ie.org/faqs/)
- [V] The exchange formats that do carry part numbers and logistics: BMEcat (XML) accommodates "product part numbers and descriptions, links to images, data sheets" and "packaging and logistics data"; ETIM xChange is a JSON format "suitable for API transfer" that "will ultimately supersede BMEcat" — [ETIM UK FAQ](https://www.etim-uk-and-ie.org/faqs/); xChange v1 was released in 2024 and is "not yet widely adopted" — [EDA white paper](https://www.eda.org.uk/starting-your-etim-journey-v2-nov-2025/)
- [V] Jewson product page, Gyproc plasterboard 2400 x 1200 x 15 mm: merchant SKU `JF124100`, Manufacturer Part Number `01177/1`, EAN `5015341016905`, brand Gyproc, manufacturer British Gypsum, product type "Tapered Edge Board", width 1200 mm, length 2400 mm, thickness 15 mm, pack quantity 1, colour Ivory — [Jewson Gyproc Duplex 15 mm](https://www.jewson.co.uk/p/gyproc-duplex-wallboard-2400-x-1200-x-15mm-natural-JF124100)
- [V] Jewson product page, British Gypsum drywall screws: SKU `BGF29453`, MPN `29453/2`, EAN `5015341292392`, length 75 mm, pack quantity 250, finish "Phosphate-coated", compliance "EN 14566"; Jewson SKUs seen are alphanumeric with a letter prefix (`JF124100`, `BGF29453`, `LDEVO024`, `EQSYC241`) — [Jewson drywall screws](https://www.jewson.co.uk/p/british-gypsum-drywall-screws-75mm-length-black-box-of-250-BGF29453)
- [V] Wickes product pages carry schema.org Product JSON-LD with `name`, `sku` (6-digit numeric, e.g. `101260`, which equals the `/p/101260` URL id), `brand` (e.g. "BOSTIK" for an Evo-Stik product), price and GBP currency; no `gtin` or `mpn` value appeared in the JSON-LD I parsed — [Wickes Evo-Stik Gripfill](https://www.wickes.co.uk/Evo-Stik-Gripfill%E2%84%A2-Grab-Adhesive---350ml/p/101260)
- [V] Travis Perkins product URLs end in a numeric id (`/p/104015`, `/p/672497`, `/p/352159`); the sampled product pages' server HTML had no JSON-LD block and no extractable attribute lines (likely client-rendered) — [Travis Perkins sitemap](https://www.travisperkins.co.uk/sitemap.xml)
- [V] Icecat itself tells users to match on identifiers: "taking care that your GTINs (EAN, UPC, etc.) are known by Icecat" and "use also Brand and Brand Product Code for matching" — [Icecat FAQ](https://iceclog.com/faq-for-merchants/)
- [V] Screwfix returned HTTP 403 to an automated request for `robots.txt`, so its SKU/identifier conventions were not examined — [Screwfix robots.txt](https://www.screwfix.com/robots.txt)

### Inferences
- GTIN presence follows brand-owner discipline: boxed branded goods (plasterboard, screws, sealants) show EANs on merchant pages, but I found no evidence either way for cut-to-length timber, cement or aggregates (my timber sample failed), so the matcher cannot rely on GTIN as the primary key. Use GTIN as a high-confidence join when present, and `(brand, manufacturer part number, attributes)` otherwise.
- Both Jewson EANs share the prefix `5015341` and the same manufacturer (British Gypsum), so brand can usually be inferred offline from the EAN prefix once a prefix-to-brand table is built from merchant data (inference from two examples; the GS1 company-prefix concept and the 500-509 UK range are general knowledge, not verified here).
- Free GTIN lookup (30/day) is unusable at catalogue scale. Verifying many GTINs would need a member route (GS1 UK partner API or premium), price unknown, and it would still return only brand/description/category/content, so it cannot validate size, grade or class.
- Merchant SKUs are merchant-specific (Wickes 6-digit numeric; Jewson letters+digits; Travis Perkins numeric): the canonical product row should store `(merchant, sku)` plus `brand`, `mpn`, `gtin`, ETIM class + features, and pack quantity/unit, which confirms the spec's line that merchant feeds must supply SKUs and prices. Jewson's page layout (SKU, MPN, EAN, dimensions, pack quantity) is a good model of the minimum record to ask any merchant for.
- Merchant "compliance" fields need validation before use: Jewson lists "EN 14190" on a Gyproc Duplex board; EN 14190 is the standard ETIM ties to boards made from reprocessed gypsum (feature EF016747), so that field may be mis-assigned (my inference, unverified).

### Gaps
- GS1 UK primary pages (fees, Verified by GS1 premium, GTIN Check API conditions, resolver service) could not be read: gs1uk.org served an Imperva interstitial/403 and gs1.org returned 403, so every GS1 UK cost and access condition above is a search-engine extract. One browserless fetch of a GS1 UK page returned only a cookie banner after the tool escalated to a captcha strategy, and I stopped using it.
- No primary source found for "GS1 Data Hub" in the UK (what it is, whether it is the API behind Verified by GS1 premium, cost).
- Whether Verified by GS1 terms permit commercial batch/automated use is not known.
- No evidence collected on GTIN coverage for timber, cement or aggregates; Screwfix and Toolstation SKU conventions not examined (403 / no product sitemap found).

## 2. Open Icecat versus Full Icecat: terms, registration, formats and API, UK coverage of building/plumbing/electrical brands, commercial-use licence

### Takeaway
The spec's "terms unclear" is out of date: both Icecat licences were re-issued on 11 February 2026 with explicit AI clauses. Open Icecat's licence voids itself on "downstream commercial use ... inside a generative AI framework", and the Full Icecat licence forbids any system that trains on or dynamically queries Icecat data "via Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)" an LLM, and is "explicitly not devised as an ASP ... or other type of multi-merchant license". Brand coverage for building/plumbing/electrical lines could not be verified and Icecat's published verticals do not include building products. For an LLM-judged, multi-merchant matcher, Icecat is not usable without a negotiated commercial licence.

- [V] Open Icecat is free (registration required): "Up to 2M Products", "600 Sponsoring Brands", "Download Limit: 1M", "70+ Languages"; Full Icecat Starter "from € 375 / monthly" (up to 5K extra products/500 extra brands), 1 Vertical "from € 650 / monthly" (up to 1M extra products/5K extra brands), Complete "from € 3,750 / monthly" (up to 40K extra brands, "All Verticals Supported") (the page's two renderings disagree on whether Starter is limited to one vertical); "Subscriptions plans are on an annual basis and invoiced yearly upfront" and fees can change without notice — [Icecat pricing for content users](https://icecat.com/pricing-content-users/)
- [V] Icecat's own pages disagree on scale: Open Icecat "400+ Sponsor Brands" vs Full "40K+ Brands" and "20M+ Products" — [Icecat upgrade page](https://icecat.com/full-icecat-upgrade/); "more than 500 brands" — [Icecat subscription plans](https://iceclog.com/icecat-subscription-plans/); "600+ brands" — [Icecat content-user page](https://icecat.com/structured-data-content-users/); whole catalogue "more than 31545607 data-sheets & 29877 brands" — [Icecat brands page](https://icecat.biz/en/brands)

- [V] Full Icecat Content License v1.34 (11 Feb 2026), clause 1.c(vi): the client service must "strictly exclude any system, functionality, or software application that uses Icecat Information to train, fine-tune, align, retrain, or dynamically query via Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) any artificial intelligence, machine learning, deep learning, or large language model, whether for internal use or third-party exposure"; clause 1.c(v): "explicitly not devised as an ASP (Application Service Provider or webshop hoster) or other type of multi-merchant license"; embeddings/model artefacts derived from Icecat data are "legally deemed a contractual derivative work" owned by Icecat — [Icecat Content License (Full Icecat)](https://iceclog.com/content-license-icecat/)
- [V] Icecat's `robots.txt` on icecat.biz disallows `/search`, `/rest/*`, `/search/rest/*` and a named list of AI crawlers (including GPTBot, anthropic-ai, ClaudeBot, CCBot) from the whole site — [Icecat robots.txt](https://icecat.biz/robots.txt)
- [V] Bosch example: "#26 in our global brand ranking", "around 67K products – but active and historic", "464 different categories", from "white-goods and small kitchen appliances, to DIY tools" (blog post of 14 July 2020; it does not say whether Bosch is an Open Icecat sponsor) — [Icecat blog on Bosch](https://iceclog.com/bosch-leader-household-electronics-brand/)

### Inferences
- The spec row "Free tier, terms unclear" should read "terms clear and restrictive": an LLM judge that is fed Icecat specs is exactly "dynamically query via RAG" (Full) and "use inside a generative AI framework" (Open). Fine-tuning on approvals that include Icecat attributes (spec step 7/Phase 4) would also breach both. Open Icecat's no-fee and share-alike clauses and Full Icecat's multi-merchant exclusion also conflict with a multi-tenant SaaS.
- Even for non-AI use, building-trade coverage is unproven: published verticals are consumer/IT, so overlap is most likely limited to power-tool brands (Bosch, Makita) and perhaps some electrical/bathroom brands. The spec's open question ("test 20 products") only makes sense if Icecat agrees a commercial AI licence first.
- Annualised entry cost if a licence were negotiated: about €4,500 (Starter), €7,800 (1 vertical), €45,000 (Complete), computed from the "from" monthly prices billed annually upfront; any AI-use licence would be priced separately and is not published.

### Gaps
- Brand-level coverage (Open vs Full) for the listed building, plumbing and electrical brands is unverified; a registered human test is the only route and I did not register.
- The "commercial license" price/terms for AI use ("standard commercial syndication rates") are not published.
- Sponsor-brand counts vary by page (400+/500+/600) and product counts differ (up to 2M Open vs 18M+/20M+/31.5M for larger sets); I could not reconcile them.
- Manuals for the Open Icecat XML/JSON/Live APIs and rate limits sit on paths excluded by robots.txt and were not read.
- Icecat's robots.txt disallows AI crawlers; I only read its public terms, pricing and FAQ pages once each and did not touch search/REST endpoints.

## 3. Manufacturer and industry product-data sources: ETIM (templates), CPA and LEXiCON, NBS Source, BIMobject, bSDD, manufacturer portals, DoP/UKCA documents

### Takeaway
For attribute templates, ETIM 10.1 (free via the bSDD API, ODC-By) is the strongest source and is deeper than the spec's red-team view assumed: gypsum board has 50 features, timber 23, plywood 28, tile adhesive 44 with EN 12004 class values, steel studs 18, WC pan 31 and basin mixer 68. It is thin on sealants and foams (no silicone-sealant or PU-foam class) and carries no part numbers, GTINs, prices or pack sizes. There is no open source of product records: NBS Source forbids text/data mining and scraping, BIMobject forbids AI use and redistribution, LEXiCON (CPA/BRE) is templates without a confirmed live data service, and DoPs hold performance classes rather than SKU attributes.

### Inferences
- The spec's weakest assumption ("ETIM and Uniclass are thin on bulk materials") is only partly right. For templates, ETIM already has EN-coded feature sets for plasterboard (EN 520 types, H1-H3), timber (strength class C14-C35), plywood, tile adhesive (EN 12004 codes), steel studs, sanitaryware and fittings, which map directly onto the checks the spec wants (size, grade, class). The real gaps are (a) sealants/foams/cement (thin or absent classes), (b) pack quantity and GTIN/MPN, which ETIM excludes, and (c) any open source of actual product records.
- Because ETIM values are fixed lists, they suit deterministic validation (hard rule R3: attributes with provenance), but the LLM must map merchant text ("C16", "tapered edge", "15mm", "TE") to enumerations; "plasterboard screw" and "drywall screw" will land in the generic wood/tapping screw classes, so sub-type distinctions need our own namespace (as the earlier note recommended for Uniclass).
- NBS Source is valuable for human lookup and Uniclass cross-checks only; its terms make bulk extraction a breach, so the spec's "manual lookup only" stance should be hard-wired (no scrapers, no AI ingestion) until NBS grants a licence.
- DoPs help with classes (reaction to fire, etc.) per product type but not with pack size, weight per sheet, GTIN or merchant SKU, and the largest plasterboard/adhesive makers block automated access, so "public on manufacturer sites" overstates bulk availability.

### Gaps
- BMF "Product Data Templates" (the UK building-materials layer on top of ETIM, cited by the BMF/NMBS announcement) could not be located online; they may be member-only. They are probably the best UK-specific template source for bulk materials and should be requested directly.
- No NBS Source API documentation or data-licence offer was found; the NBS terms read were the website terms, not a separate Source data agreement.
- The CPA/NBS/BIM4M2/CIBSE 2015 product-data-template initiative page was not read (only a search snippet), and LEXiCON's current status in 2026 is unclear.
- ETIM classes were checked only for the families above; ceramic tiles, expanding foam, silicone sanitary sealant and basin waste outlets were not found as dedicated classes, and cement has only 8 features.
- Crabtree, JG Speedfit and Hep2O sites did not load (timeouts/SSL), and the Geberit, Grohe, Mira, Triton, MK, McAlpine, Makita and fischer homepages showed no ETIM, data-portal or API link in my link scan (only generic download/literature pages for MK and McAlpine); deeper pages were not checked. A keyword scan that appeared to find "ETIM" on several of these homepages was a false positive (case-insensitive match inside other words) and is not reported.
- DoP availability and format per manufacturer (PDF only vs machine-readable) was not sampled.

## 5. Wikidata and WDC Products: usefulness and licence for the stated roles (synonyms/multilingual labels; LLM-judge benchmark)

### Takeaway
Wikidata's CC0 licence is ideal, but its content for UK trade products is thin and noisy: only 2,864 items carry a GTIN, and of 20 UK trade terms looked up by label, 2 returned no item and 4 resolved to the wrong sense or to noise (timber to a surname, wall plug to an AC power plug, silicone sealant to a scientific article, grout to "grist").
