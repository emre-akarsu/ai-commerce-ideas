## 3. Pricing models observed
- Enterprise suites: annual licence, custom quote (Zip, Coupa, Ariba).
- SMB/mid: platform fee + per-pro-user seats (Procurify), flat monthly (Precoro $499+, Fairmarkit ~$415+ per Capterra).
- Fintech-bundled: free/low software, monetized via card/interchange (Ramp; the interchange point is my inference, not sourced).
- Outcome/transaction-volume: Fairmarkit scoped on volume; Pactum-type savings-share is common but I did not verify terms.
- Nobody found charging per approved order or per quote at SMB scale.

## 4. Stats
- Tail spend: McKinsey puts it at 10-20% of spend across 80-90% of items; benchmarks 8.9% (TMT) to 26.5% (public sector) ([Beyond Intranet summary](https://www.beyondintranet.com/blog/tail-spend-management/)). About 80% of transactions ([Tradogram](https://www.tradogram.com/blog/tail-spend-management), secondary).
- Hackett: ~$700-1,400 internal cost to source/set up/manage each supplier; 2025 study: 48% say tail more of a priority, 64% dissatisfied, only 4% actively manage most of tail ([Zycus summary of Hackett](https://www.zycus.com/blog/spend-management/tail-spend-vs-tactical-spend-vs-maverick-spend), [Verusen](https://verusen.com/mro-inventory-optimization-2/what-is-tail-spend-and-why-its-costing-you-millions/)). Vendor-republished.
- Maverick spend: average 30%, best-in-class under 10%; non-compliant dollar costs 12-18% extra (Ardent, via [Varisource](https://www.varisource.com/blog/procurement-metrics-benchmarks-formulas-that-matter) / search summary; secondary).
- Cost per PO: $14-54+ (APQC, via same secondary source).
- AI adoption: Hackett 2025 CPO Agenda: 49% piloted GenAI in 2024, only 4% at scale ([AI Assembly Lines](https://aiassemblylines.com/resources/state-of-ai-procurement-2026-benchmarks)). Ardent: 58% using or piloting AI; 67-84% plan agentic in 24-36 months; 2026 "Year of Pilots" ([CPO Rising](https://cporising.com/2026/07/27/ai-rising-2026-pt-1-state-of-procurement-series/), [Ivalua/Ardent PR](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-research-by-ardent-partners-and-ivalua-reveals-gap-between-ai-ambition-and-execution-in-procurement-302832556.html)).
- Deloitte 2025 CPO Survey (250+ CPOs, 40 countries): top GenAI use cases spend analytics 53%, RFP/RFQ generation 42%, contract summaries 41%; Digital Masters 3.2x GenAI ROI vs ~1.5x followers ([Deloitte PDF](https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/assets-zone3/us/en/docs/services/consulting/2025/us-deloitte-2025-global-cpo-survey.pdf), [procurementmag](https://procurementmag.com/news/deloitte-global-cpo-survey-2025)).
- Gartner May 2026 (101 CPOs): only 36% very confident redesigning function for AI; GenAI gains stay individual-level, decline at team level ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-19-gartner-survey-shows-just-36-perecent-of-chief-procurement-officers-are-very-confident-in-ability-to-redesign-function-for-ai), [procurementmag](https://procurementmag.com/news/gartner-cpo-confident-ai-redesign-procurement)).
- McKinsey: autonomous category agents 15-30% efficiency; ~20% savings from AI analytics; BCG: up to 15% savings and ~60% buyer capacity freed with redesigned S2P ([BCG 2026](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/ai-in-procurement-drives-competitive-advantage), [BCG 2025](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/from-buzz-to-bottom-line-cost-reductions-using-genai); figures via search summary).
- Time: Suplari ~10.6 automatable hours per professional per week (vendor, [AI Assembly Lines](https://aiassemblylines.com/resources/state-of-ai-procurement-2026-benchmarks)); Didero claims 70% of team time is mundane tasks (vendor). No credible independent "time spent on quotes" figure found.

## 5. Analyst views on failure
- Gartner (Jun 2025): over 40% of agentic AI projects canceled by end-2027 due to cost, unclear value, weak risk controls; of 3,400-org poll, 19% had significant agentic investment; also "agent washing" ([BigDATAwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/bigdatawire/this-just-in/gartner-predicts-over-40-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027/)). General, not procurement-specific.
- 74% of procurement leaders say data not AI-ready; McKinsey: ~8 in 10 firms cite data limits; under 10% scaled agents (secondary, [Speclens](https://www.speclens.ai/blog/agentic-ai-procurement-guide-2026)). Treat as low-confidence.
- Intake tools became "a front door to nowhere" because the downstream sourcing of non-catalog requests isn't automated ([Zycus, vendor](https://www.zycus.com/blog/intake-management/procurement-intake-failure-front-door-nowhere); [Spend Matters](https://spendmatters.com/2025/12/02/top-6-procurement-orchestration-mistakes-and-how-to-avoid-them/)).
- Security/risk is top barrier to scaling agents for ~two-thirds of respondents ([AI Assembly Lines/Art of Procurement](https://artofprocurement.com/blog/state-of-ai-in-procurement)).

## 6. What incumbents and new players do NOT serve
- Businesses with no procurement team (Ramp cites 98% of US firms lack one) and micro/SMB, though Ramp now reaches into this.
- Physical direct materials/parts/MRO requiring spec matching, lead times, and supplier-outside-network RFQs: served only by Didero/Tacto/Magentic, mostly manufacturers.
- Non-catalog, one-off, supplier-unknown purchases: intake tools stop at routing (see above).
- Per-transaction pricing: nearly everyone sells seats or platforms.
- Execution on the supplier side (many small suppliers answer by email/phone/PDF; Aron/Didero handle email only).

## GAPS & OPPORTUNITIES I SEE (ranked)
1. **Quote-to-approved-order agent for SMB/mid-market direct/MRO/parts and services, outside supplier networks.** Incumbents want enterprise suites; Ramp covers software/services-style indirect; Didero/Tacto target manufacturers. Differentiator: finds suppliers, RFQs by email/phone, normalizes into comparable quotes, approver one-click.
2. **Tail-spend agent priced per completed sourcing event or per order**, not seat. Tail is ~80% of transactions yet only 4% manage it (Hackett) and per-supplier admin costs $700-1,400; fits a usage-based business-charged model.
3. **Spec/BOM normalization layer** (turning vague requests or photos into comparable RFQs); the hard part for non-catalog items and where generic LLM intake fails.
4. **Wedge via a vertical** (e.g., maintenance parts, facilities services, packaging) to avoid competing head-on with Ramp/Zip distribution.
5. **Governance-first positioning**: audit trail, approval limits, no-autonomous-commit by default, responding to Gartner cancellation/risk concerns and 36% CPO confidence figure.
6. **Mid-market competitors to watch**: Ramp (distribution + card data), Fairmarkit (tail, enterprise-priced), Aron (RFQ email), Didero. Ramp is the largest threat for the services/indirect niche.
