## 2. Data protection (UK GDPR / DUAA 2025)

**2.1 DUAA changes that touch the product.**
- Recognised legitimate interests (Sch 4, new Annex 1) list: disclosure for another's public-task processing, national security/defence, emergencies, crime, safeguarding. None covers B2B purchasing, so the product still needs an ordinary Art 6(1)(f) legitimate-interests assessment [opened] https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/schedule/4 . The ICO's own page still lists direct marketing, intra-group admin and network security as examples of legitimate interests and keeps the three-part test [opened] https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/legitimate-interests/what-is-the-legitimate-interests-basis/ .
- ADM (s.80, new Arts 22A-22D): a decision is "solely automated" if there is no "meaningful human involvement"; a "significant decision" has a legal or "similarly significant" effect; restrictions bite mainly with special category data; safeguards are information, representations, human intervention and contest. In force from 5 Feb 2026 [opened] https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/section/80 . The product's human approval on every send (R1) and human award keep it outside Art 22A for ordinary supplier ranking; the exposure is automatic exclusion or scoring of a sole-trader supplier with no human review (see DP-08).
- PECR breach time periods: s.111 is a duty on communications service providers (24h to 72h from 20 Aug 2025) [opened] https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/contents ; https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/report-a-breach/personal-data-breach/personal-data-breaches-a-guide/ . It does not apply to a SaaS vendor that is not a CSP; the UK GDPR 72-hour rule is the one that applies.
- Complaints (s.103): controllers must facilitate complaints, acknowledge within 30 days (automated acknowledgement is enough for electronic complaints); processors are not subject. ICO guidance published 12 Feb 2026 [snippet] https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/data-use-and-access-act-2025-new-statutory-rules-on-handling-data-protection-complaints-from-19th-june-2026 . I could not open the ICO page itself (404 at the URL tried).
- Section numbers for PECR enforcement (s.115) and penalty notices (s.101) are in the contents page [opened]; the fine cap is in [repo] UK-DPL-04.

**2.2 DPIA triggers.** The ICO's list of high-risk operations includes innovative technology (AI/ML named), data matching, invisible processing (data obtained indirectly without privacy information), large-scale profiling and denial-of-service decisions; most operate "in combination with other criteria" [opened] https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/data-protection-impact-assessments-dpias/when-do-we-need-to-do-a-dpia/ . An LLM agent that reads supplier replies and builds contact records plausibly meets "innovative technology" plus "invisible processing", so a DPIA before pilot is the prudent reading [judgement]. The ICO's January 2026 agentic-AI report flags cascading hallucinations and unintended inference of special category data [snippet] https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/01/22/ico-tech-futures-report-agentic-ai-102mdki .

**2.3 AI lawfulness.** ICO guidance separates development from deployment purposes; contract necessity usually cannot justify training; a documented LIA is expected [opened] https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/artificial-intelligence/guidance-on-ai-and-data-protection/how-do-we-ensure-lawfulness-in-ai/ . Implication: cross-tenant learning (R10) and any model fine-tuning on tenant data is a separate purpose needing its own basis, notice and DPA permission.

**2.4 Roles and the SaaS DPA.** A processor may act only on controller instructions; becomes a controller if it acts outside them or for its own purposes; sub-processor terms must give equivalent protection and the processor stays liable [opened] https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/controllers-and-processors/controllers-and-processors/what-does-it-mean-if-you-are-a-processor/ . The processor must notify the controller of a breach "without undue delay", which the contract should specify [opened, breach guide above]. Art 28(3) mandatory terms (subject-matter/duration/nature/purpose/data types/categories of subjects; documented instructions; confidentiality; Art 32 security; sub-processor authorisation and flow-down; assistance with data-subject rights and Arts 32-36; deletion or return at end; audit and information) [memory] (the ICO contracts page returned 404). Roles per [repo] UK-DPL-07.

**2.5 Transfers and the model provider.** Routes: UK adequacy regulations, IDTA/UK Addendum with a transfer risk assessment, narrow exceptions [opened] https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/international-transfers/international-transfers-a-guide/ . Data bridge: the UK sender must confirm the US recipient is on the DPF List and signed up to the UK Extension; if not, IDTA/Addendum [opened] https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-us-data-bridge-supporting-documents/uk-us-data-bridge-factsheet-for-uk-organisations . Existing risk flags are in [repo] UK-DPL-08/09.

**2.6 ICO registration/fee.** The ICO page confirms sole traders and organisations using personal data must pay unless exempt but gave no tiers in the fetch [opened] https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/ . Tiers stay as in [repo] UK-DPL-10; re-confirm the tier and payee at registration because I could not re-verify them today.

**2.7 Art 14 notice.** The ICO page returned 404; the rule set is as in [repo] UK-DPL-07.

## 3. Direct marketing and one-to-one RFQ emails

What the sources say:
- PECR reg 2(1) defines direct marketing as "the communication (by whatever means) of advertising or marketing material which is directed to particular individuals" [opened] https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/2 . Reg 22(1) restricts "unsolicited communications for the purposes of direct marketing" by email to individual subscribers unless prior consent or the soft opt-in; the Feb 2026 charity exception (para 3A) is irrelevant here [opened] https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/22 . The page fetched shows the operative test as "advertising or marketing material"; an RFQ asking for a price from a supplier is a request to buy, not an advertisement. This supports, but does not prove, UK-DPL-02.
- ICO wording: "The PECR rule on direct marketing by electronic mail does not apply to corporate subscribers" (identity not concealed, valid opt-out address still required); sole traders and partnerships get the individual-subscriber rules (consent or soft opt-in); an address that identifies a person (name@company) is personal data, so UK GDPR applies even in business contexts [opened] https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/ . (The ICO page at the electronic-mail-marketing URL returned 404; this B2B page carries the quoted text.)
- Solicited quotes: I found no ICO page, case or enforcement notice that addresses RFQs, purchase enquiries or "solicited" quotes in a procurement setting (negative finding, scope-limited; consistent with [repo] UK-DPL-02). An ICO landing page says "direct marketing covers promoting aims and ideals as well as selling products and services" [opened] https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/direct-marketing-guidance/ , i.e. promotion is the hook.

Defaults the MVP should apply [judgement]: (a) RFQ templates contain no promotion of the product or buyer; the AI footer states identity and purpose only; (b) no "follow-up" text that pitches or upsells; (c) treat sole-trader and English/NI partnership recipients as individuals: same template, but honour any "do not contact" reply immediately and per tenant suppression list; (d) throttle first-contact volume per supplier and per tenant so a bad prompt cannot become unsolicited bulk mail; (e) never email a consumer address (gmail/outlook personal) found by scraping; use buyer-supplied supplier contacts or published business addresses.

## 4. Consumer-facing angle (if homeowners are users)

- B2B-only avoids most of it: the unfair-commercial-practices regime protects consumers, defined as "an individual acting for purposes that are wholly or mainly outside the individual's business"; CMA guidance gives the example of a tractor-parts seller dealing only with businesses falling outside; platform operators and agents acting for traders are "traders" [opened] https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/unfair-commercial-practices-cma207/unfair-commercial-practices . The DMCC Act Part 4 Ch 1 (ss.224-252) is consumer-only protection and has applied to practices from 6 Apr 2025, with CMA fines up to 10% of global turnover (or £300,000) [opened] https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/section/224 (structure only); [snippet] https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/cma-publishes-final-unfair-commercial-practices-guidance-under-uk-dmcca-what-your-business (fine detail).
- "Consumer" and "trader" in CRA 2015 s.2: if a trader disputes consumer status, the trader bears the burden of proof [opened] https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/2 . So a B2B-only gate needs a real business check at sign-up (company number, VAT number or business-domain email) and terms that say so; a ticked box alone is weak [judgement].
- If a homeowner became a user: the SaaS contract is a consumer contract (CRA s.49 reasonable care and skill applies to services [opened] https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/49 ); the Consumer Contracts Regulations give a 14-day cancellation right with listed exclusions (reg 28 lists exclusions such as personalised goods and urgent repairs) [opened] https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/28 , and off-premises services contracts above £42 carry written cancellation-notice duties [snippet] (a local trading-standards summary; not opened). Subscription regime from January 2027 adds renewal reminders and a second 14-day renewal cooling-off [opened/secondary, link above]. Fake-review duties apply to a platform that publishes reviews [opened, CMA207 page]; the MVP should publish no supplier ratings or reviews.
- Home-improvement angle: TrustMark describes itself as the "Government Endorsed Quality Scheme" for work in or around the home; the opened page says nothing about platforms or lead-generation rules [opened] https://www.trustmark.org.uk/ . I found no CMA home-improvement guidance for tooling; the search returned a withdrawn OFT document [snippet]. Do not display or imply TrustMark, Gas Safe or NICEIC status except as a supplier-declared field with the source and date (links to R3).
- The "intent" proposal (`05-intent-driven-quotes-proposal.md`) is homeowner-shaped (Victorian bathroom). Shipping it to homeowners in the MVP would bring in consumer law, TrustMark expectations and the regulated-work gates; keep it for contractor buyers, or defer.

## 5. Security baseline buyers will ask for

- **Cyber Essentials/Plus.** Five controls: firewalls, secure configuration, security update management, user access control, malware protection; Plus adds independent technical testing; Standard from £320 + VAT by size [opened] https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/cyberessentials/overview . NCSC supply-chain guidance names Cyber Essentials as "a tangible, efficient way" to get assurance from suppliers [opened] https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/supply-chain-security . Danzell changes (27 Apr 2026): MFA on cloud services is mandatory where available [snippet, link in s1].
- **ISO 27001, SOC 2.** Commentary says UK/EU buyers ask for ISO 27001 first and SOC 2 mainly for US sales; entry costs quoted: Cyber Essentials from about £330, ISO 27001 from £3,000, SOC 2 Type 2 from £15,000; one claim that ISO cuts questionnaires 70-90% is vendor marketing [snippet] https://presencis.com/regulations/comparisons/soc-2-vs-cyber-essentials ; https://www.orbiqhq.com/compliance-automation/iso-27001-for-saas.md . I could not open iso.org (403). Evidence on what UK SME buyers actually require is weak; I found no survey.
- **NCSC/DSIT AI security.** Guidelines for Secure AI System Development (NCSC and partners, 27 Nov 2023): secure design, development, deployment, operation, with logging, monitoring, update management and incident response [opened] https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/guidelines-secure-ai-system-development . DSIT Code of Practice for the Cyber Security of AI (31 Jan 2025, voluntary, 13 principles incl. "Enable human responsibility", "Secure your supply chain", "Document your data, models and prompts", "Monitor your system's behaviour", "Ensure proper data and model disposal"); it addresses developers, system operators and data custodians [opened] https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-cyber-security-code-of-practice/code-of-practice-for-the-cyber-security-of-ai ; link to ETSI EN 304 223 [snippet] https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2025/02/government-publishes-new-ai-cyber-security-code-of-practice-and-implementation-guide/ .
- **Prompt injection.** NCSC (8 Dec 2025): it "may never be fully eliminated"; use deterministic safeguards; "when an LLM processes information from a party, the privileges it has drops to that of the party"; avoid deny-lists; log comprehensively [opened, URL in s1]. This matches R1 and R4 (quarantined extractor, no link fetching, no mail credentials in the planner) and is the citation to give buyers.
- **Software Security Code of Practice** (7 May 2025, updated 15 Jan 2026, voluntary, 14 principles in four themes: secure design/development, deployment/maintenance, customer communication, vulnerability management) [opened] https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/software-security-code-of-practice ; theme detail [snippet] https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2025/05/rd-uk-government-publishes-software-security-code-of-practice-for-software-vendors .
- **Shared responsibility.** For SaaS the provider takes most security duties; the customer keeps configuration and deciding what data to store [opened] https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/cloud/understanding-cloud-services/cloud-security-shared-responsibility-model . Put this split in the security whitepaper and DPA annex.
- **CAF and NIS.** The Cyber Assessment Framework is aimed at essential-service operators; a commercial SaaS is not a normal subject [opened] https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/cyber-assessment-framework . The Cyber Security and Resilience Bill (introduced 12 Nov 2025) would bring medium and large managed service providers into scope; its present stage and final definitions were not verified [snippet] https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2025/the-uk-cyber-security-and-resilience-bill-and-what-it-means-for-your-business . Monitor; probably not in scope for an MVP SaaS, but ask counsel.
- **Email authentication.** NCSC: use SPF, DKIM and DMARC plus TLS; free email security check [opened] https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/email-security-and-anti-spoofing . Gmail (from 1 Feb 2024): all senders need SPF or DKIM, valid PTR, TLS, RFC 5322 format, spam rate below 0.3%; bulk senders (5,000+ per day) need SPF and DKIM, DMARC with aligned From domain, and one-click unsubscribe for marketing and subscribed mail; transactional mail is exempt from unsubscribe but not from authentication [opened] https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126 . Yahoo: SPF or DKIM minimum, both plus DMARC (at least p=none) for bulk, spam rate below 0.3% [opened] https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/ . Outlook.com high-volume rules (about 5,000/day, SPF/DKIM/DMARC, from May 2025) [memory]; the Microsoft page fetched empty. Design effect: one shared platform sending domain across tenants pools reputation and can reach the bulk threshold; prefer per-tenant DKIM (or send via the buyer's own mailbox) and aligned DMARC [judgement].
- **Breach notification.** Controller to ICO "without undue delay, but not later than 72 hours after becoming aware"; reasons for delay; all breaches recorded; processor tells controller without undue delay [opened] https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/report-a-breach/personal-data-breaches-a-guide/ . A SaaS DPA should set a processor-to-controller window shorter than 72 hours (24 hours is a common norm [memory]).
- **Logging/retention.** UK GDPR sets no fixed period [repo] UK-DPL-12. NCSC and DSIT both ask for monitoring and logging [opened above]; the NCSC security-monitoring page returned 404. Hash-chained events (R6) satisfy integrity; keep personal data out of the chain per [repo] A8.

## 6. Accessibility, equality, approvals, records, payment terms

- **Equality Act 2010 s.29:** a person "concerned with the provision of a service to the public or a section of the public (for payment or not)" must not discriminate and must make reasonable adjustments (Sch 2); it covers private businesses [opened] https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/section/29 . Whether a B2B-only tool counts as a service to "a section of the public" is arguable; the safe course is to assume yes [judgement].
- **WCAG 2.2** is a W3C Recommendation (12 Dec 2024); new AA criteria include 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum), 2.5.7 Dragging Movements, 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum), 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (Minimum); Level A additions 3.2.6, 3.3.7 [opened] https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/ . GOV.UK requires WCAG 2.2 AA of public-sector bodies and does not impose it on the private sector, but expects accessibility to be written into supplier procurement [opened] https://www.gov.uk/guidance/accessibility-requirements-for-public-sector-websites-and-apps . So a public-sector tenant (out of scope per [repo] A11) would require AA, and many enterprise buyers copy the standard into questionnaires [judgement].
- **Approvals as e-signatures.** ECA 2000 s.7: electronic signatures and their certification are admissible as evidence [opened] https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/7/section/7 . UK eIDAS Art 25(1): an electronic signature is not denied legal effect or admissibility solely because it is electronic [opened] https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2014/910/article/25 . Law Commission 2019: an electronic signature can satisfy a statutory signature requirement if the signer intends to authenticate and formalities are met; typed names in emails and website tick-boxes have been accepted [opened] https://lawcom.gov.uk/project/electronic-execution-of-documents/ . The Approval here is an internal authorisation, not a signature on a contract, so no qualified signature is needed; the real question is evidence that a named, authenticated person approved the exact content [judgement]. Contract-formation and agency questions stay with [repo] A2/A3.
- **Records.** VAT records at least 6 years [repo] UK-CTL-05. Company accounting records 6 years and sole-trader self-assessment records 5 years after the 31 January deadline [snippet] https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/how-long-should-you-keep-records-in-the-uk/ ; the GOV.UK individual page I opened (22 months after the tax year) is a different regime and is not the business-record rule [opened] https://www.gov.uk/keeping-your-pay-tax-records/how-long-to-keep-your-records . I could not open HMRC's business-records page. Treat 6 years as the conservative outer bound for POs and approved RFQs.
- **Late payment.** Statutory interest is 8% plus the Bank of England base rate for B2B transactions only [opened] https://www.gov.uk/late-commercial-payments-interest-debt-recovery/charging-interest-commercial-debt ; rates and bill status [repo] UK-CTL-12. Requirement effect: capture quoted payment terms as a structured field and flag silence; the product gives no debt advice.
