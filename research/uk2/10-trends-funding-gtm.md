## 1. Verified headline facts

1. **PMI:** S&P Global UK Manufacturing PMI was 51.9 in September 2026 (final, 1 Oct), 51.7 in August; above 50 for 11 straight months. Small manufacturers' output and new business "contract sharply" while medium and large firms grow; delivery times are lengthening; input and output price inflation re-accelerated [M1-M3].
2. **Vacancies:** ONS (15 Sep 2026): 702k in Jun-Aug 2026; manufacturing 48k (50k a year earlier); construction 29k (31k) [M6-M8]. The dataset has no "skilled trades" occupation split [M9].
3. **Energy:** the British Industrial Competitiveness Scheme (BICS) is real but narrow: exemption from Renewables Obligation, Feed-in Tariff (from April 2027) and Capacity Market (from October 2027) levies, "up to 25%" off electricity bills, for eligible Great Britain manufacturers; applications 1 Oct-30 Nov 2026 [E1-E4].
4. **Made Smarter Adoption (NOW):** nine English regions, 50% match funding, £1,000-£20,000, SME manufacturers only [MS1-MS3]. The £4.5bn figure belongs to a different programme [MS8].
5. **Tax reliefs:** SEIS up to £250,000 (trade at most 3 years old, gross assets at most £350,000, under 25 FTE); EIS companies can raise £10m a year since 6 April 2026; VCT relief is now 20% [TX1-TX5].
6. **Seed norms (2025):** median seed deal £0.6m, seed deals down 27% to 704, AI took 44% of SME equity value [V1-V4].
7. **Comparables:** Procure AI $13m seed (5 Nov 2025) confirmed at source; Omnea $50m Series B (17 Sep 2025) [PR1-PR2].
8. **Exits:** Autodesk-MaintainX about $3.6bn, announced 28 May 2026, closed 3 Aug 2026; Rubix-ERIKS UK and Ireland closed 3 Nov 2025 with no disclosed price [X1-X2].
9. **Events:** Smart Manufacturing Week (Maintec co-located) is 9-10 June 2027, NEC Birmingham; Informa has retired The Facilities Show [EV1, EV3].

## 2. Macro, energy, trade (task 1)

**PMI (NOW).** Headline strength hides a size split: S&P's September release says small manufacturers contracted sharply, so the SME end of the market is weaker than the headline [M2]. Supplier delivery times lengthened to the greatest extent since June (port congestion, shipping delays, geopolitics) and input costs rose again [M3]; that is the closest primary evidence for parts-sourcing pressure. The series is now titled "S&P Global UK Manufacturing PMI" (CIPS no longer in the title). Make UK separately says one in four members are offshoring or weighing it, and the Autumn Budget is on 28 October [O1].

**Vacancies (NOW).** ONS VACS02 is by industry only. Round 1's "52,000 maintenance/trades vacancies" equals ONS all-manufacturing vacancies for Nov-Jan 2026, a different thing [M10]. Manufacturing vacancies of 48k are well below the 62k of Jun-Aug 2024 (all industries: 853k to 702k), so the official series shows looser labour demand than two years ago; lower vacancies do not by themselves show fewer shortages (inference).

**Energy (NOW/UPCOMING).** BICS is not "energy costs down 25%". It removes policy levies on electricity only, for firms that are registered at Companies House, hold an eligible SIC code, make eligible HS-coded products at a Great Britain site and use at least 33 MWh a year of grid power per site [E1-E3]. The Supercharger (energy-intensive industries) is separate and cannot be claimed for the same activity [E6]. I could not verify a total cost (a "£600m" figure appeared only in a search summary) [E5].

**Trade (verifiable facts only).** Under the UK-EU Trade and Cooperation Agreement only UK or EU content counts towards origin (bilateral cumulation); origin is proved by a statement on origin or importer's knowledge [T1]. UK accession to the Pan-Euro-Med convention was put to a call for evidence (17 Nov-22 Dec 2025; outcome 26 Mar 2026); I found no decision [T2]. UK CBAM starts 1 Jan 2027 for aluminium, cement, fertiliser, hydrogen, iron and steel, with a £50,000 registration threshold; I did not check whether downstream parts are in scope [T3]. The May 2025 UK-EU summit explainer covers SPS, ETS linking and steel quota, nothing on industrial-parts origin [T4].

## 3. Made Smarter and Innovate UK (task 2)

**Made Smarter Adoption (NOW).** Regions: East Midlands, East of England, London, North East, North West, West Midlands, South East, South West, Yorkshire and the Humber; premises must be in England, so "UK-wide" is wrong [MS1]. Support is fully funded advice, roadmaps, leadership and skills training plus 50% match-funded grants (£1,000-£20,000) [MS2]. Eligibility is SME manufacturing and engineering businesses; GOV.UK metadata for the London scheme lists industry "manufacturing" and sizes under 10 and 10-249 [MS3]. FM and building-services contractors are not manufacturers, so they likely fall outside (inference). Technologies are the eight Industrial Digital Technologies including "AI and cognitive learning" and "data and systems integration" [MS4]. **Whether AI procurement software qualifies is not stated anywhere I could open**; the 2026-published DBT/Ipsos evaluation says grants mostly funded data capture and systems and robotics/process automation, and that firms struggled to find AI expertise [MS5, MS7]. Funding: £8m a year 2022-25, £16m in 2025-26 for all nine English regions [MS6]. The evaluation found 1,400+ roadmaps and 78% reporting technology adoption, but no statistically significant robust effect on turnover, jobs or productivity [MS7].

**Innovate UK (NOW).** Funding Service snapshot on 3 Oct 2026: no open competition for procurement, digital procurement or MRO software (keyword "procurement" returns 0; "Digital technology", "Digital industries", "Emerging technology" areas return 0) [I1]. Nearest, all long shots: Frontier AI: SME Champions Phase 1 (up to £3.1m pot, SMEs, 1 Oct-11 Nov 2026) [I2]; Efficient, Structured and Controllable AI Systems (up to £1.66m, SMEs, 12 Oct-18 Nov 2026) [I3]; Innovation Loans Expression of Interest (SMEs, closes 9 Oct 2026) [I4]; KTP 2026-27 Round 3 (university-led, business partner, closes 14 Oct 2026) [I5]. Competition pages need sign-in (403), so intensity and fit were not verified. Round 1's three competitions are closed or no longer listed [I7].

## 4. SEIS, EIS, VCT (task 3, GOV.UK pages updated to 6 Apr 2026)

SEIS: company raises up to £250,000; gross assets at most £350,000; fewer than 25 FTE; qualifying trade not carried on for more than 3 years; no prior EIS or VCT money; investors get 50% income-tax relief on up to £200,000 a year [TX1-TX2]. EIS: under 250 FTE; gross assets at most £30m before and £35m after; up to £10m in 12 months and £24m lifetime (£5m/£12m for "specified companies"); within 7 years of first commercial sale; investors get 30% on up to £1m (£2m if at least £1m goes into knowledge-intensive companies) [TX3-TX4]. VCT: 20% relief on up to £200,000, held 5 years [TX5]. HMRC data quoted by BBB: tax year 2024-25 SEIS £276m (+14%), EIS £1.6bn (flat) [TX6]. Round 1 had "under 2 years" and "£5M per annum"; both wrong. I did not open the knowledge-intensive or advance-assurance pages.

## 5. Seed and Series A norms; procurement rounds (task 4)

BBB Small Business Equity Tracker (published July 2026, Beauhurst data, calendar 2025): smaller businesses raised £12.3bn in 2,002 deals (-4% value, -17% deals); whole market £17.0bn (-3%) [V1]. Seed: 704 deals (-27%), £2.1bn, mean £3.2m (record, inflated by one £445m round), **median £0.6m**, median pre-money £3.2m, 14.4 months between seed rounds (12.4 in 2024) [V2]. Venture stage (not labelled "Series A"): 835 deals, £4.5bn, **median £1.1m** (-32%), median pre-money £8.2m [V3]. AI: 44% of SME equity value, 26% of deals [V4]. Beauhurst The Deal H1 2026: £14.4bn in 2,799 deals, record mean round £5.4m, three AI companies 29% of capital, median seed pre-money £2.0m [V5]; this differs from BBB's £3.2m, so do not mix the two. Beauhurst first-time investment 2025: £4.4bn (+11.7%), newly funded companies +29.9% [V6]. Atomico (data to 30 Sep 2025, extrapolated): UK $14bn (+22%), Europe about $44bn (+7%), European median seed and Series A rounds +23% and +25% [V7]. Round 1's "seed £500k-£1m, Series A £3-10m" is not a median in any source I opened; its "£18.9bn, +35%" contradicts BBB and Atomico. Seedcamp's site gives no cheque-size or portfolio-count statement [V8]; Dealroom not retrieved.

**Procurement rounds.** Procure AI $13m seed, London dateline 5 Nov 2025, led by Headline with C4 Ventures and Futury Capital; positioned at Fortune 500/FTSE 350, including spot-buy and quote-to-order intake [PR1]. Omnea $50m Series B, 17 Sep 2025, Insight Partners and Khosla, total over $75m [PR2]. Zip $190m Series D at $2.2bn, 21 Oct 2024; no later round found [PR3]. ## 9. UK GTM hypothesis (judgement, not evidence)

- **Segment:** start with manufacturers of roughly 50-249 staff rather than micro and small, because S&P shows small firms contracting; treat building-services and FM contractors as a second wedge reached through trade bodies and field-service software partners (Joblogic, Simpro/BigChange).
- **Subsidy:** do not build the plan on BICS (electricity levy relief for a narrow product list) or Made Smarter (manufacturers only; no robust productivity effect found). Test with a regional Made Smarter adviser whether a 50% match grant could fund a purchase; that is an upside case.
- **Funding:** the 2025 seed market is selective (median £0.6m, 14.4 months between seed rounds, AI taking 44% of value). Plan a small seed with enough runway for slow rounds; SEIS then EIS is the tax-advantaged angel route if eligibility holds. Innovate UK lists nothing aimed at this product now; a KTP with a university partner (closes 14 Oct 2026) is the one structural route I found, and fit is untested.
- **Competition and partners:** investors fund AI procurement at enterprise level (Procure AI, Omnea, Zip); SME MRO quoting looks open. Distributors are buying AI, so expect channel conflict; Rubix is the UK consolidator but no AI move found.
- **Pricing and cycle:** nothing verified for the UK. Measure from pilots; do not reuse Round 1's £15-35k ACV or 4-9 months.
- **Channels:** Smart Manufacturing Week/Maintec (June 2027), IWFM and trade-body newsletters.
