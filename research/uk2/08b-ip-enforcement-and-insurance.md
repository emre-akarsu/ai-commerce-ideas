# UK round 2: IP enforcement, counterfeit evidence, AI insurance, liability caps (08b)

Accessed 2026-10-03. Compliance and insurance research, not legal or insurance advice. All 30 permitted web calls used; three returned nothing usable (Kennedys 403, Operations Engineer 503, Drives & Controls blank). Fetched pages pass through a summariser (statutes requested verbatim); PDFs were read as images. C-numbers refer to the ledger.

## Headlines

- "Up to ten years" is real but belongs to Trade Marks Act 1994 (TMA) s.92(6)(b), not the General Product Safety Regulations 2005 (GPSR, not opened).
- Hiscox's 2025 "Affirmative AI" is a wording enhancement in its technology PI policy, not a standalone AI-liability policy; "first" is contested (C13-C15).
- Evidence that UK PI/Tech E&O policies carry AI exclusions is thin: market discussion and a specimen clause, no prevalence data (C19-C21).

## 1. Trade marks: offences, remedies, due diligence

**Offences, s.92 (opened; "Latest available (Revised)"; C01-C04).** An offence needs "a view to gain for himself or another, or with intent to cause loss to another, and without the consent of the proprietor", plus one of: applying to goods or packaging "a sign identical to, or likely to be mistaken for, a registered trade mark"; selling, offering, exposing for sale or distributing such goods; or possessing them "in the course of a business" with a view to doing those things. Section 92(4) limits it to registered goods or marks with a UK reputation. Defence, s.92(5): belief "on reasonable grounds" that the use "was not an infringement". Penalties, s.92(6): on summary conviction, up to six months or a fine not exceeding the statutory maximum, or both; on indictment, "a fine or imprisonment for a term not exceeding ten years, or both."

My reading (not advice): the offences target applying, selling, distributing or holding for supply. A buyer using parts only in its own plant is not obviously caught; a maintenance contractor or distributor that supplies parts to customers could be. Recording why a part was believed genuine may matter for the s.92(5) defence.

**Civil, s.14 (opened; C05).** An infringement "is actionable by the proprietor", with "damages, injunctions, accounts or otherwise" available as for "any other property right". Not opened: the provisions defining infringing acts, erasure and delivery-up remedies, exhaustion (grey goods), and the 2006 IP enforcement regulations.

**Government due-diligence guidance.** None specific to buyers of branded industrial parts found. The GOV.UK IP enforcement collection lists Trading Standards IP-crime surveys (latest 8 Sep 2026) and the IP Counter-Infringement Strategy 2022-2027, with no annual IP Crime and Enforcement Report (C07); the 2025-26 survey has no buyer guidance (C06). Closest UK-government material is MOD DEF STAN 05-135 (counterfeit avoidance for MOD suppliers), seen only in secondary summaries: buy from original manufacturers or authorised distributors, inspect and verify, take a risk-based approach (C12). SKF's 2011 advice is to use its authorised distributor network (C08). I found no official UK source recommending traceability, authorised-distributor sourcing or certificates of conformity to MRO buyers.

## 2. UK evidence on counterfeit industrial parts

- **Bearings:** Trading Standards seized about one tonne of counterfeit SKF bearings in a 9 March swoop on "a non-authorised SKF bearing distributor"; article dated 14 Mar 2011 (C08). An undated World Bearing Association campaign item has no UK figures (C09). No BBA statement found (C10).
- **Electrical:** the Trading Standards survey (8 Sep 2026) says 30% of responding authorities investigated electrical goods; the category is generic (C06). Gambica and BEAMA warnings about copy contactors, overload relays and circuit-breakers appeared only in undated snippets (page fetch blank): unverified (C11).
- Net: opened UK evidence is 15 years old or generic; no government dataset on counterfeit MRO parts surfaced.

## 3. Insurance

**Hiscox (C13-C14).** Hiscox's broker factsheet (v3, June 2025; "correct as at 14 May 2025") lists "Affirmative AI: Explicit cover for those who use, build and advise on artificial intelligence" as one evolved area of its Technology wording, with no limits, exclusions, premium or eligibility stated. Insurance Business UK (date reported as 3 Jul 2025) calls it "the first in the UK" and gives examples: reliance on an algorithm's output, faulty data fed into an AI tool, negligent AI-suitability advice. Other outlets date coverage 5 Jun 2025 and say "one of the first" (C17). Neither source mentions bias or chatbots.

**Other AI-specific cover.** Armilla (a Lloyd's coverholder) announced AI Liability Insurance underwritten at Lloyd's, including Chaucer, on 30 Apr 2025, covering "failure of the AI solution to perform as intended, generate critical errors, hallucinations or inaccuracies leading to damages" (C15; limits, eligibility and UK availability not stated). A July 2026 aggregator reports CFC adding affirmative AI wording to seven products including technology E&O (C16, unverified).

**Exclusions.** ISO's US general-liability forms (CG 40 47, CG 40 48, CG 35 08; effective January 2026) exclude "generative artificial intelligence" for bodily injury, property damage and personal/advertising injury, with no UK or PI reference (C18). At Lloyd's, a DAC Beachcroft deck for the LMA Wordings Forum (21 Apr 2026) sorts wordings into endorsements, product enhancements, standalone AI wordings, and conditions and exclusions, with a specimen "absolute exclusion" for claims "arising out of" any "use, deployment, or development of Artificial Intelligence"; it is a law-firm specimen, not an LMA clause (C19). Chubb UK's July 2026 flyer warns of "silent AI risk" and tells buyers to ask about "AI, algorithm or model-related exclusions" and sub-limits (C21). One broker says "most policy wordings don't specifically address the question" (C22). Aggregator-reported figures (Beazley/QBE AI sublimits about 10%; Chaucer/Armilla limits from $25m) are unverified (C16). No published typical limit for UK AI-startup PI found.

**What insurers ask.** The LMA deck's suggested proposal-form questions include "Who would be checking the output and what are their qualifications?", user training, client consent and ring-fencing of confidential data, output licensing, "limitations of liability (consequential losses excluded)", disclaimers and cyber resilience (C20). Chubb: "What level of board oversight and governance evidence improves underwriting outcomes?" (C21). Neither is a named insurer's form. Ask a broker to confirm in writing that AI-driven mismatches are covered.

**Premium indications (not quotes).** Only generic broker/aggregator snippets, unopened: PI "£9.08 to £45.42 per month", Superscript PI "from £7.58 per month", cyber "£10.79 to £43.65 per month", and an estimate of £750-£2,500 for a SaaS firm with £500k turnover and a £1m cyber limit (C25). These are micro-business figures, not AI-specific Tech E&O pricing; Hiscox and Armilla publish none.

## 4. Liability-cap norms in UK B2B SaaS

No authoritative or well-sourced UK survey found. Practitioner marketing articles (search snippets, unopened) say caps "often sit between 100% and 200% of annual fees", with formulas such as 12 months' fees, 125% or 150% of annual fees, or a fixed £50,000-£250,000 (C26). A non-UK (Israeli firm) "What's Market in SaaS" deck exists (C27). Treat all as low-confidence opinion.

## Round-1 claims: confirmed / contradicted / unverifiable

**Confirmed**
- ISO form numbers and January 2026 effective date (C18).
- Hiscox added explicit AI cover to its technology wording in 2025 (C13).
- A ten-year maximum exists for trade-mark counterfeiting offences (C04).
- The cited Insurance Business and independentagent.com URLs exist and match their subjects.

**Contradicted (wholly or in part)**
- Ten years "under GPSR 2005": it is TMA s.92(6)(b) (C04). "Unlimited fine": s.92(6)(b) says "a fine" with no cap stated.
- "UKCA preferred; CE recognised until 31 Dec 2027": GOV.UK (updated 21 Aug 2026) gives CE recognition no end date and calls UKCA optional; the 2027 date applies to placing UKCA on a label or accompanying document (C28).
- "Standard CGL policies now exclude generative-AI errors" (overstated): the ISO forms are US GL endorsements, not PI, with no UK reference (C18).
- "First UK affirmative AI liability policy ... bias, AI-assisted decisions, chatbots": a wording enhancement; "first" is contested by Armilla/Chaucer (30 Apr 2025); that scope list is not in Hiscox's sources (C13-C15). "May 2025" fits the wording's unveil; AI coverage is June.
- '"Silent AI" exclusion' is imprecise: Chubb frames silent AI as "coverage gaps, exclusions or ambiguous wording" (C21), and the LMA deck lists exclusions as a separate wording type (C19).

**Unverifiable**
- Buyer "liable for procurement negligence": no opened source; s.92 needs a view to gain or intent to cause loss.
- GOV.UK vehicle-parts and Peterborough pages (not fetched); Kennedys (403). The "misinformation supplied directly" idea appears only in a 3 Jul 2025 LexisNexis UK headline (C23, C24).
- "Inherited GL does not protect agent error", "may require specialized endorsement", "coverage minimum >= max PO value": inference or recommendation, no source.
