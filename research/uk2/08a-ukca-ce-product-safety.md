# UKCA, CE and product-safety law for GB sourcing software (round 2 fact-check)

Prepared 2026-10-03. Compliance research, not legal advice. Corrects `research/uk/04-uk-legal.md` section 6. Product: software that helps maintenance buyers compare quotes for replacement parts and shows certification marks as vendor-declared, with no warranty. IDs (C1...) refer to the ledger (section 2). "Fetched yes" = opened via the GOV.UK content API or legislation.gov.uk XML (same text and dates as the web page). 25 retrieval calls used (1 WebFetch, 3 WebSearch, 21 direct fetches).

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 UKCA vs CE in Great Britain
1. **No CE end date is stated.** Current GOV.UK guidance (published 31 Mar 2026, updated 21 Aug 2026) says the UK "continues to recognise the CE marking, alongside or in place of the UKCA marking" under SI 2024/696 (C1). That SI (in force 1 Oct 2024, GB only) removed the expiry of the EU-route provisions (C2); the 2024 GOV.UK page said "indefinitely" (C11).
2. **Regimes.** GOV.UK's 31 Mar 2026 table gives GB "UKCA or CE" for machinery (SI 2008/1597), electrical equipment (SI 2016/1101), pressure equipment (SI 2016/1105) and PPE (Regulation (EU) 2016/425 as applied in GB), each with legislation "to continue recognition of the CE marking" from 1 Oct 2024. EMC, ATEX and RoHS match (C3).
3. **Construction products:** separate track; CE accepted "subject to system wide reform", with "a minimum 2-year transitional period" for any change; UKCA needs a UK approved body (C4).
4. **"31 December 2027" is not a CE deadline.** It ends the option to put the UKCA mark on a label or document instead of the product, and relaxed importer-details rules for EEA/Swiss imports (C5).
5. **Marks are self-declared and not universal.** "Not all products must bear a conformity marking" (C6); the mark shows the "manufacturer believes" the product complies (C7). Older, stale-but-live GOV.UK text dates a spare part from the original product's placing on the market (C9).
6. **Mutual recognition:** no UK-EU product-safety conformity-assessment MRA found; GOV.UK's MRA page lists Australia, New Zealand, Switzerland and the USA (C28). The Aug 2026 guidance keeps UK Approved Bodies (UKCA only) and EU Notified Bodies separate (C29); CE recognition and "Fast-Track UKCA" are the bridge (C30).
7. **Judgement:** the wording is conditional ("Where the aims and requirements ... meet the needs of the UK"); read "indefinite" as "no current end date", not a guarantee.

### 1.2 Product Regulation and Metrology Act 2025 (PRAM Act) and GPSR 2005
- **The Act** (2025 c. 20, enacted 21 Jul 2025) is enabling: the Secretary of State "may by regulations make provision" on marketing or use of products, including marking, "the making of statements about products" and "the marketing of products through an online marketplace", binding marketplace controllers and "an intermediary" (C13, C17). It does not itself change CE/UKCA recognition or the GPSR.
- **Commencement:** "The rest of this Act comes into force on the day on which it is passed" (s.15(2)). Only s.11(1) and (3) (omitting Parts 2 and 4 of the Consumer Protection Act 1987, and parts of the Weights and Measures Act 1985) await regulations (C14, C16); legislation.gov.uk shows them "not in force" and no commencement regulations (C15; confidence M). GOV.UK's "came into force on 21 July 2025" (C19) holds for the rest.
- **"Online marketplace"** is broad: a platform service "which facilitates the marketing of products in the United Kingdom"; "made available on the market" includes "advertised"; no consumer-only limit (C18).
- **GPSR 2005** (SI 2005/1803) remains the core framework; a replacement was proposed in a consultation (31 Mar to 23 Jun 2026) and I found no response or successor (C23, C26; M). It covers consumer products only (C21). The proposal would cover "business products" such as "machinery or other products used in the workplace" and impose due-care duties on producers, onward suppliers and online marketplaces (C24, C25). Not law.
- **Other 2025-26:** Construction Products (Amendment) Regulations 2025 in force Jan 2026 (C4); outdoor-noise regulations (Apr 2026) and a Jul 2025 machinery call for evidence (C20; M).

### 1.3 B2B software intermediary showing marks (judgement, labelled)
Facts: marking duties sit with manufacturers, authorised representatives, importers and distributors (C8). I found no instrument obliging a software intermediary that does not supply, import or run a marketplace to verify or display marks; future regulations could reach marketplaces and "intermediaries", but none were found (C15, C17).

Judgement, must-level: if the product takes title or possession, sells under its own name, imports, arranges warehousing or dispatch, or lets vendors list items for purchase on it, treat it as a possible distributor, importer, "onward supplier" or "online marketplace" and take legal advice first.

Judgement, should-level for display:
1. Label every mark "vendor-declared, not verified", with source document and capture date.
2. Show the mark as stated (UKCA, CE, UKNI+CE); never convert or infer. GB treats CE and UKCA as equal (C1); for Northern Ireland show whether CE is present (C27).
3. Show "no mark stated", never "non-compliant": many parts need none (C6) and spares follow the original product's date (C9).
4. Avoid "certified", "compliant", "safe" or "approved" in the tool's own voice; keep the no-warranty text beside the marks.
5. Keep any declaration-of-conformity reference with the quote record; prompt "buyer to verify" on safety-critical parts, as professional end users can have their own duties (C12).
6. Be able to hide or flag a listing on a safety alert or recall (proposed for onward suppliers, C25).
7. Keep this wording in the deployment profile, not code.

### 1.4 Northern Ireland (two sentences)
Under the Windsor Framework relevant EU rules on manufactured products apply in Northern Ireland, so GOV.UK's NI column lists only "CE, or UKNI and CE" for machinery, electrical, pressure, PPE and construction products (UKCA is not listed) (C3, C27). The UKNI mark "is never used on its own", always accompanies an EU mark such as CE and applies where a UK-based notified body did the third-party assessment, while qualifying NI goods can be placed on the GB market on CE or CE-and-UKNI marks (C27).

## 3. Round-1 claims: confirmed / contradicted / unverifiable

Contradicted:
- "CE remains recognized until 31 December 2027": contradicted (C1-C3, C5); 2027 is a UKCA labelling date.
- "Products for GB must bear UKCA or CE marking": overstated; only where a regime requires it (C6).
- "UKCA is preferred": unsupported; the 2026 guidance treats them as alternatives (C1, C3). A 2021 note encouraging UKCA is superseded (C11).
- Counterfeit offence under the GPSR 2005, "up to 10 years": contradicted; reg 20 allows 12 months on indictment (C22), and the GPSR covers consumer products, not UKCA/CE duties (C21). Source of "10 years" not identified.

Confirmed (partly):
- The cited GOV.UK URL exists and is the right page (C1) but contains neither claim (C5).
- Flagging parts with no stated mark is sound if worded "not stated" (C6); a design choice, not a legal duty.

Unverifiable:
- "Buyer organization is liable for procurement negligence if a supplied part is later found counterfeit"; round 1's counterfeit and Trading Standards pages were not opened.
