## 1. Short answers

1. **Tier B can legitimately cite three BSI-listed standards for deep-groove ball bearings:** BS ISO 15:2017 (boundary dimensions), BS ISO 5753-1:2009 (radial internal clearance), BS ISO 492:2023 (tolerances). Each is "Current" at BSI and "Identical to" ISO (L01-L07). They fix dimensions, clearance and tolerance only; no standard for seal class, grease, cage or rating equivalence was located (none was specifically searched for).
2. **V-belts have a weaker source.** BS 3790:2006 shows "Withdrawn (as of 22 May 2026)" and "Not equivalent to" ISO 4183/4184 (L08-L09). ISO 4183/4184 are the likely live references, but edition and status are unverified (iso.org returned 403).
3. **Manufacturer cross-reference tools exist** (SKF, NTN-SNR, Timken, NSK, Schaeffler, Gates, ContiTech) but **no licence permitting reuse was found.** Terms seen via search summaries bar copying without written permission; the one API licence opened (Schaeffler) is non-sublicensable and non-transferable.
4. **No commercial text-and-data-mining exception was found.** CDPA s.29A covers non-commercial research only, and the 18 March 2026 government report says an opt-out exception "is no longer the government's preferred way forward". Database right (reg 13, 16, 17) is the main exposure for bulk or systematic extraction.
5. **Beachhead (JUDGEMENT):** metric deep-groove ball bearings. Evidence is strong for the three standard-backed attributes, weak for market dominance and seal class.

**Interchangeability.** The standards make envelopes fit; they do not make parts "the same". Seal suffixes, grease, cage, published ratings and belt construction (wrapped or raw-edge, cogged, antistatic) sit outside them (INFERENCE). Tier B for "6205-2RS" therefore needs a second named source for the seal attribute, normally the maker's designation table, which re-opens the licensing problem.

## 5. UK law (primary text opened unless stated)

- **Database right (SI 1997/3032).** Reg 13: database right "subsists...if there has been a substantial investment in obtaining, verifying or presenting the contents". Reg 16(1): infringed by extracting or re-utilising "all or a substantial part"; reg 16(2): "repeated and systematic extraction or re-utilisation of insubstantial parts...may amount to" a substantial part. Reg 17(1): fifteen years from the end of the calendar year of completion. Reg 19(2) voids terms stopping a lawful user extracting insubstantial parts. So automated one-lookup-at-a-time use stops being safe once systematic; no bright line.
- **Copyright in tables and databases (CDPA s.3, s.3A):** not opened. KNOWLEDGE: a table or compilation can be a literary work; a database is protected where selection or arrangement is the author's own intellectual creation; facts and ideas are not.
- **CDPA s.29A (text and data mining):** copying only "for the sole purpose of research for a non-commercial purpose", with lawful access and acknowledgement; no transfer or other use (s.29A(2)); contract terms unenforceable only to the extent they restrict copying s.29A permits (s.29A(5)). Unavailable to a commercial product.
- **AI and copyright policy.** DUAA 2025 ss.135-136 required an impact assessment and report before 18 March 2026 (L28, L30). The report, "Published 18 March 2026", says "a broad copyright exception with opt-out is no longer the government's preferred way forward"; it proposes more evidence-gathering, monitoring of transparency rules abroad and "not to intervene in the licensing market at this stage" (L22-L24). The opened page does not mention database right or s.29A. Later developments were not checked.
- **Contract.** API terms with registration (Schaeffler) are a real licence; enforceability of browse-wrap website terms was not assessed.

## 7. Recommendation (JUDGEMENT)

**Beachhead:** metric deep-groove ball bearings (60xx, 62xx, 63xx; open, shielded, sealed; normal and C3 clearance). Three current, dated standards cover envelope, clearance and tolerance. Evidence: H for those three attributes; L for seal class, grease, cage; L for market dominance. V-belts second, after verifying ISO 4183/4184 and obtaining a belt-maker licence.

**Gate:** until written permissions exist, use only standards-derived rules (after BSI/ISO confirmation) and buyer-confirmed data; do not ingest manufacturer cross-reference tables.

**Five permissions to seek first:**
1. BSI (and ISO): written permission to hold ISO 15, 5753-1 and 492 values (later 4183/4184) as structured rule data with edition and date.
2. SKF: licence to store and display Croesus or API results with attribution; the only documented public cross-reference API found.
3. Schaeffler: Developer Portal agreement stating storage, derived-data and AI-use rights, which the terms leave silent.
4. One UK distributor product-data feed (Rubix, RS, Cromwell or a bearing specialist) with ETIM or BMEcat attributes and equivalence-check rights.
5. One V-belt maker (Gates, Optibelt, Fenner or ContiTech): cross-reference and catalogue licence. Follow-ups: Timken, NSK, NTN-SNR.

**For the spec owner:** docs/product/04-product-spec.md lines 30-31 list "manufacturer cross-reference" under Tier B but "a competitor's marketing cross-reference" under Tier C; a maker's map from a rival's designation to its own is arguably the latter, so decide per source. Keep a per-source licence record (line 137): edition, date, URL, licence status, proof of permission.
