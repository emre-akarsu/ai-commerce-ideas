# UK MRO / trade suppliers: corporate facts (round 2)

Date: 2026-10-03. Replaces the corporate-fact parts of `research/uk/02-uk-suppliers.md`.

Method and caveats:
- Primary sources first: Companies House (CH) filings, results announcements, SEC filings, suppliers' own sites. Aggregators (Wikipedia, CB Insights) and search-result summaries are labelled and rated low.
- Larger UK subsidiaries file scanned-image PDFs. I read them by tesseract OCR; "V" in the ledger means I also checked the page image. One OCR error was caught (Rexel UK change % read as 14.5%; the page says 11.5%), so treat OCR-only percentages with caution.
- Figures are legal-entity figures unless stated. The 30-call research budget is spent; open items are in section 4.
- CH document URLs follow `https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/<no>/filing-history/<id>/document?format=pdf&download=0`.

## 5. What the ownership map implies for the product (judgement)

My interpretation, not sourced fact.
- The suppliers a buyer is likely to name are large branch networks with central accounts, not small shops. "Mid-tier" was wrong for CEF, Edmundson and City Plumbing (£1.2-1.9bn each).
- Private equity now owns Rubix, Cromwell, Wolseley UK, City Plumbing and Jewson. Cromwell changed hands in Dec 2025, Rexel UK is cutting branches at a loss, and City Plumbing has had ERP and cash problems in 2026. Supplier records should carry legal entity, trading brands and a last-verified date, since contacts and branch lists may change.
- One relationship can span several brands (Rexel UK covers Rexel and Newey & Eyre; Wolseley UK covers Plumb Center). Deduplicate by legal entity before sending RFQs.
- Nothing here points to a supplier that is an obvious acquirer or partner. RS, Rubix and Amazon Business are the digital incumbents; whether they would treat a buyer-side agent as a competitor is untested.
- Sonepar is not behind CEF, Edmundson or Newey & Eyre; its UK link found here is Routeco and Mayflex only.
