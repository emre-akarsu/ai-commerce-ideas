# VC Partner Review: MRO Purchasing Agent (2026-10-02)

## 1. Verdict: WATCH
No customer contact, no TAM, "unfair advantage: none on day 1", and the docs' own A7 kill trigger (a competitor targeting this segment) looks already tripped.

## 2. Eight objections, ranked

1. **"None targets small maintenance buyers"; Aron has an "enterprise tilt" (canvas §5).** Stale. [web] Aron launched 14 Sep 2026 with $8M (Menlo, Storm), sells to "mid-market to Fortune 10", began in MRO (TransPak), runs email-native RFQs with no portals. Ramp shipped mid-market procurement agents on 29 Apr 2026, with clarifying-question intake. Only gap: spec equivalence, unmentioned in Aron's release. *Evidence:* three plants choosing you over Aron/Ramp.
2. **A1: vendors answer a third party's RFQs.** Counter reps are paid on catalog volume; chasers from a disclosed "assistant" (rule 8) on small orders get low priority, and distributors are building their own agents (comps §3). No evidence, yet make-or-break. *Evidence:* ≥70% 24h reply on ≥100 real RFQs.
3. **ROI is soft.** Value is time saved. My assumption: 1-2 hrs per quote loop at $35/hr. At A3's 10 buys/month that is $350-700 saved against a $660 price ($600 + 10×$6): break-even. Aron's customer cites time, not cost; comps §5 calls savings contested. *Evidence:* measured loop minutes, 5 sites.
4. **Beachhead contradicts the pitch.** Bearings/power transmission were picked because equivalence is deterministic, yet distributors' cross-reference tools already serve them, mostly as catalog buys. A3 is untested. *Evidence:* email history showing ≥10 truly non-catalog buys/month.
5. **Retention.** Flat per-site fee, multi-site deferred to P2, so NDR is under 100%. The docs' SMB churn is 42-58%/yr; their 10-15x multiples need >120% NDR. *Evidence:* a paid cohort expanding.
6. **Accuracy gates contradict "zero wrong parts".** 95% Tier-1 and 90% Tier-2 precision means 1-10 wrong per 100; "zero" then rests on human approvers doing the engineering check you claim to remove. Canvas A2 (95% at "safe substitute", kill <90%) conflicts with spec (95% T1 / 90% T2). *Evidence:* blind expert-labelled ≥500 items.
7. **Liability vs price.** A6 tolerates a $20k/yr E&O premium; five pilot accounts at $7.2k bring $36k, so that is 55% of revenue. Tier 2 is an engineering recommendation; downtime dwarfs $600/month. *Evidence:* bound E&O quote; capped contract two buyers accept.
8. **Unit-cost maths is off.** "≤10 operator minutes ≤ $2.50" implies $15/hr labour; at $30-40/hr it is $5-6.67, already at the A5 kill line ($5). The WTP gate "≥$500/mo or ≥$6/order equivalent" differs ~10× ($6×10 = $60). Three partners is anecdote. *Evidence:* operator minutes at real wages; gate restated at $500/mo.

## 3. Market size and ACV
The docs contain **no TAM**. Using their numbers: $600×12 = $7,200 + 10 orders×$6×12 = $720, so ACV ≈ **$7.9k**, below the $10-25k band that earns 12-month payback (comps §4). $100M ARR needs 100M/7.9k ≈ **12,700 paying sites**. My unsourced estimate: US plants of 50-500 staff number tens of thousands, so roughly a third of them. At 65% GM, gross profit ≈ $5.1k/site; at 42-58% churn, life ≈ 2 yrs, LTV ≈ $10k; at 14-20 month payback the affordable CAC is ≈ $6-8.5k, and founder-led outbound will sit at the top. Acquirer comps (Fictiv $350M, AWM $210M) imply $100-350M talent buys: fine for a small seed cheque, not venture-scale. MaintainX at $3.6B on >$135M ARR [web] is ~27×: a strategic outlier, not an underwriting multiple. Scale needs multi-site FM and take-rate: a different product.

## 4. Moat test: FAIL
Canvas concedes no day-1 advantage. ChatGPT already decodes and cross-references ISO-standard parts; intake/RFQ/approval is plumbing the docs call cheap ("engineering cost is low"), so cheap for Aron, Ramp and CMMS vendors too. The data moat collides with hard rule 10 (shared only with consent, de-identified) and the open licensing question; 3-5 partners yield ~30-50 labels a month. Passes only if cross-tenant vendor-responsiveness plus a consented equivalence graph beat public tables.
