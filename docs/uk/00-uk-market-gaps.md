As of 2026-10-03. Replaces v1 (2026-10-02), which came from six small-model passes with documented quality problems. v2 rests on eleven stage-1 research reports (`research/uk2/01` to `11`, each with a claim ledger) and twelve independent verification reports (`research/uk2/verify/V1a` to `V10`). Status legend: **V** verified by an independent route · **P** partly verified (one part holds, single primary source, or a caveat) · **S** single source, not independently checked · **X** round-1 claim contradicted · **U** unverified or not found · **A** assumption or judgement.

## 1. Bottom line: what the second round changes

1. **The reachable pool is about 33,000 enterprises** with 10-249 employees in manufacturing, machinery repair/installation, building-services installation and combined facilities support (ONS-26 33,075; DBT BPE-25 32,875; both recomputed from the raw workbooks). Only 17,290 have 20+ employees; 47.7% have 10-19. [V] UK-POP-01, 02
2. **There is no defensible TAM.** The best anchor for UK industrial MRO distribution is about £14bn, a competitor's estimate quoted by the CMA in its 2025 Rubix/ERIKS decision (the merging parties said £10-20bn, another competitor £66bn and called the addressable market debated). Parts spend by 10-249-employee firms is not published anywhere we could reach. [P] UK-SPD-01
3. **Per-request pricing gives small revenue ceilings:** £0.7m to £5.6m ARR at 1-5% of all 33,075 firms and £12-20 per request (£0.3m to £11m with halved or doubled volume), and only £0.2m to £1.9m if just the mid-case buying base of 11,576 firms is counted. The base assumes 10 and 30 non-catalogue requests a month, which is unsourced (£2.0-3.4k per business-year). Round 1's £6-35k ACV and £95-115bn TAM are rejected. [A/X] UK-SPD-07, 08
4. **Parts delay is the best-evidenced pain, its frequency is unmeasured.** 83% of 199 UK manufacturers report maintenance delays from unavailable or long-lead parts, but only about one in five "regularly" (four capital-intensive sectors, vendor-commissioned, no question text). No source measures how often non-catalogue or urgent buying happens. Test T1 stays the kill test. [P] UK-VOC-01, 06
5. **No UK-based AI RFQ agent aimed at 10-249-employee maintenance buyers was found** (absence of evidence from two thin sweeps, so a hypothesis, not a finding). Closest to our workflow and not yet followed up (snippets only): Trade Parts Finder (London; AI part identification and stock check across merchants) and Mandel AI (YC; RFQ-to-invoice for manufacturers). Adjacent: Prolo (construction materials), Joblogic (field-service software, Vista £100m+), Fiix (already emails RFQs), enterprise agents, and five US RFQ/PO agents that could sell remotely. US distributors (Fastenal, Grainger) are buying AI companies; no UK distributor deal was found. [U/A] UK-CMP-01; funding facts [P] UK-CMP-02 to 09
6. **Suppliers are large networks, not small shops** (Screwfix £2.7bn, Edmundson £1.9bn, Wolseley UK £1.8bn, CEF and City Plumbing £1.2bn each), several private-equity owned and changing hands. No terms read bar receiving an emailed RFQ; website and API scraping bans exist at Screwfix, Farnell, Bearing Boys and Amazon, and element14's partner API terms would conflict with a multi-supplier quote tool. Owners change often (Cromwell to AURELIUS on 17 Dec 2025; ERIKS UK&I into Rubix on 31 Oct 2025). [P] UK-SUP-*, UK-ACC-*
7. **Law (judgement for a solicitor to confirm; not advice).** PECR probably does not catch non-promotional RFQs (inference; no authority either way). UK company trading-disclosure rules need more care than round 1 gave them (it missed them). Text: the registered name is required on all business correspondence and documentation (reg 24(1)(g); reg 29(c) covers electronic form). Interpretation: the UK part of registration, number and registered office are required on business letters and order forms (reg 25), and whether an RFQ email is a "business letter" is not settled by the text; Registrar guidance for LLPs lists "business emails", the limited-company guidance does not mention email, and a law-firm note advises the full set in the standing footer. The product therefore prints all four and blocks sending without them, a cautious design choice that a solicitor must confirm (sole traders and partnerships follow CA 2006 ss.1200-1206 and are not modelled). Reg 25 shows amendment notes (SI 2016/423; a temporary restriction by SI 2024/233) that were not read. A supplier contact's privacy notice may be due by the first email (UK GDPR Art 14(3)(b)). DUAA 2025 is in force; the Information Commission replaced the Information Commissioner on 30 Sep 2026. [P/V] UK-DPL-*, UK-CTL-01
8. **Product safety.** For the regimes SI 2024/696 covers (machinery, electrical equipment, pressure equipment, PPE and others) CE is recognised in Great Britain with no end date; 31 Dec 2027 is a UKCA-labelling date, not a CE deadline. Construction products and some other sectors have their own rules. [P] UK-PSL-01
9. **Data licensing gates Tier B.** Current standards exist for bearings (ISO 15:2017, ISO 5753-1:2009 under revision, ISO 492:2023) and V-belts (ISO 4183:2026, ISO 4184:2025 replaced the 1995/1992 editions; BS 3790 withdrawn 22 May 2026), but BSI requires written permission to store standards text in a retrieval system (its subscriber terms say copies "should not be copied in order to build a database") and no manufacturer cross-reference data is licensed. [V/P] UK-PRT-01, 02, 06, 07
10. **Funding, grants and price.** The 2025 seed market is selective (median £0.6m); Made Smarter Adoption covers English SME manufacturers only and no Innovate UK competition fits; stated willingness to pay for generic AI is low (12% of UK SMBs would pay £44-87 a month, the top band reported), against about £170-280 a month implied on average by the base revenue model and £360-600 a month for 50-249-staff firms at 30 requests a month. No reliable UK sales-cycle or contract-value benchmark exists. [V/P] UK-FND-*

### 2.3 Revenue ceilings (arithmetic, not a forecast)

Assumptions: A1 base 33,075; A2 non-catalogue requests per business per month 10 (10-49 staff) and 30 (50-249 staff), unsourced; A3 price £12/£16/£20 per completed request, or flat £1,800 and £4,800 a year; A4 adoption 1/3/5% (331/992/1,654 customers) from day one, no churn, no free tier.

| Pricing | 100% | 1% | 3% | 5% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| £12 per request | £66.9m | £0.67m | £2.01m | £3.34m |
| £20 per request | £111.4m | £1.11m | £3.34m | £5.57m |
| flat £1,800 / £4,800 | £79.6m | £0.80m | £2.39m | £3.98m |

The grid applies 1-5% to all 33,075 firms, from day one, with no churn and no free tier (the "first 10 free" in the pricing test would lower year one further). Filtered cases at £12-20 per request and 1-5% adoption: the mid-case buying base of 11,576 firms (x0.35) gives £0.23-1.95m; counting only firms with 20 or more employees (17,290 firms; they carry 66% of base revenue) gives £0.44-3.68m; both filters together (x0.23) give £0.15-1.29m. Volume halved (5 and 15 requests a month) gives £0.33-2.79m on the unfiltered base; doubled (20 and 60) gives £1.34-11.14m. A flat £45-120 a month (£540-1,440 a year, 16-71% of the modelled £2.0-3.4k) gives £0.18-2.38m at 1-5% of the full base; 4 requests a month for every firm at £12-20 gives £0.19-1.59m. Base volume is 168.5 requests per business-year, so £2.0-3.4k per business-year at £12-20. £1m ARR needs about 371 customers at £16 per request (1.1% of the base). [A] UK-SPD-07. Even 100% adoption at £16 is 0.14% of the £62.5bn wholesale ceiling. Volume (A2) is the dominant unsourced input: replace it with interview data before using any cell.

What follows (judgement): at these assumptions the UK business is not venture-scale on per-request pricing alone, so Phase 0 must establish whether request volume per account is far higher than assumed, whether a seat or flat price is accepted, or whether the UK case is a regional wedge for a larger product. Reference prices: Joblogic from £45 per user per month (purchase orders sit in higher tiers), ServiceM8 £0-269 per business per month, Precoro $499-999 per month. [S] UK-CMP-03

### 4.3 Design implications (judgement)

- Keep "no scraping, no portal automation, no link fetching" as a design rule (R7): it matches what the terms allow.
- Address the buyer's named account manager or branch at portal-quote majors; use published email routes elsewhere; put the buyer's account number in the RFQ (needs a vendor field: backlog).
- Record quote validity, VAT basis and delivery basis on every quote; thresholds, cut-offs and credit terms are per-supplier data.
- Supplier terms can let the supplier substitute goods (Hayley 2.5: "a product of similar style, quality and price"; RS 4.2: where a product has been superseded). The product never substitutes across tiers (R2), so the PO should say that substitutes need the buyer's written approval and goods-in should be matched to the PO part number (wording needs counsel; backlog). RFQs should say "quotation only, not an order": where suppliers accept email orders (RS, Cromwell, Hayley), an RFQ that carries an account number could be read as an order (inference). [A/P] UK-ACC-02, 04, 09
- Supplier records need legal entity, group, trading brands and a last-verified date; deduplicate by legal entity before sending (Rexel UK covers Rexel and Newey & Eyre; Wolseley UK covers Plumb Center).
- Supplier APIs are not drop-in for a multi-tenant agent (buyer's own trade account, keys that place binding orders, caching limits, 4-6 weeks of onboarding at Amazon).
- Nothing points to a supplier that is an obvious partner; RS, Rubix and Amazon Business are the digital incumbents and whether they would treat a buyer-side agent as a competitor is untested.
