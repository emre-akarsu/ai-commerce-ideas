# Assemble UK refurbishment kits from open parts

No public dataset gives licence-clean, quantified material kits for UK bathroom, kitchen, wet-room, full-house or maintenance jobs. The product will have to author its own template library. The good news is that every input to that library has an open or citable source. The vocabulary can come from **Uniclass 2015 (CC BY-ND 4.0)** and **ETIM (ODC-By)**, both reachable through the free bSDD API. Item skeletons per job type can come from **OGL government documents** (Decent Homes guidance, Approved Documents F, M and P) and from council and housing-association specifications that are publicly posted but not openly licensed. "Not supplied" dependency rules can come from manufacturer installation guides. Consumable quantities can come from datasheet constants and simple geometry. Price drift can be tracked with the **OGL DBT construction material price indices**. The richest structured sources are all closed to embedding: the M3NHF Schedule of Rates is "all rights reserved", SFG20 is subscription-only, BCIS and Spon's are paid, the DDC CWICR estimate database is non-commercial, and BIMobject bans AI training. They can inform design but cannot seed data. The product should therefore ship a small set of synthetic, labelled, tradesperson-reviewed templates. Each should be modelled as a feature model with constraint rules, with quantities computed deterministically, every line coded to Uniclass/ETIM with provenance, and every market-specific number (waste factors, extract rates, socket counts, TMV temperatures, VAT) read from the deployment profile. Whether buyers want this at all is still unproven, so the first template is an experiment, not a catalogue.

The pattern is consistent. Anyone can read the work-item vocabulary of real UK refurbishment jobs, because buyers attach M3NHF extracts and brand tables to public tenders. Almost none of it may be redistributed. Hillingdon's long-running kitchen and bathroom replacement programme, for example, prices against "a pre-determined basket of rates developed using the … M3NHF Schedule of Rates version 7.1" rather than a bespoke BoQ ([bidstats](https://bidstats.uk/tenders/2023/W06/792265018)). The SOR items themselves are also composite labour-plus-material operations, not SKU-level bills of materials. Item 371021 reads "Renew any type of prefinished single 300x500mm base unit including plug and scribe… excluding worktop and plinth" ([Sell2Wales 357559](https://www.sell2wales.gov.wales/Assets/NoticeBuilder_FileDownload.aspx?id=357559)). Even a licensed SOR would leave the kit-content problem unsolved.

## Public specifications already sketch each job-type kit

Read together, the OGL documents and the publicly posted specifications give a defensible skeleton for every job type the product targets. Brand names in them are one buyer's "or equal approved" choice, not a recommendation.

**Bathroom full refit.** The Decent Homes disrepair test defines the bathroom as three items (bath, wash hand basin, WC) and the kitchen as six (cold drinking water, hot water, sink, cooking provision, cupboards, worktop) ([DHS PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7968b740f0b63d72fc5926/138355.pdf)). That OGL minimum is the mandatory core of a feature model. A Welsh social landlord's November 2025 bathroom specification fills in the rest. It has a sanitaryware table (dual-flush 4.5/3 L WC, 1700x750 slip-resistant bath, pedestal basin, pillar taps, **TMV3 pre-set at 43°C**, 8.5 kW electric shower, SELV extract fan, LED bulkhead, R10 2 mm safety vinyl, wall tile). It also lists the ancillaries cost guides never itemise: isolation valves on every feed, 38 mm traps back to the 110 mm SVP, a rigid pan connector, plug and chain, bath overflow, white and clear sealants, plastic tile trim, waterproof grout, pull-cord switch, supplementary bonding, latex levelling compound, 18 mm MR chipboard or ply overlay, and decorating materials ([Sell2Wales 376193](https://www.sell2wales.gov.wales/Assets/NoticeBuilder_FileDownload.aspx?id=376193)). Broadland HA's 2023 specification is a second, independent list with manufacturer codes ([Broadland spec](https://www.north-norfolk.gov.uk/media/8753/broadland-housing-association-affordable-housing-specification.pdf)). Screwfix's guide confirms why the sundries line matters: it budgets "plumbing sundries" at £300–£600 without listing them (unverified marketing-grade figure) ([Screwfix](https://www.screwfix.com/guides/bathrooms-kitchens/bathrooms/new-bathroom-costs-guide)).

**Cloakroom and WC unit.** Separate WC replacement appears as its own line in Hillingdon's phases ("40 Kitchens & 40 Bathrooms & 12 Separate WC's", from a search snippet) ([Contracts Finder](https://www.contractsfinder.service.gov.uk/Notice/17827971-84e2-4670-ac4a-6c13c871fc77)). Decent Homes treats a WC without a basin on the same floor as inappropriately located ([DHS PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7968b740f0b63d72fc5926/138355.pdf)). A cloakroom kit is therefore WC plus a basin, a subset of the full bathroom. For ventilation, Approved Document F sets sanitary accommodation at 6 l/s against 15 l/s for a bathroom ([ADF Vol 1](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/61deba42d3bf7f054fcc243d/ADF1.pdf)).

**Wet room and level-access shower.** Walsall Council's DFG specification is the most complete item-level public schedule read in full. It has 17 lines: strip-out, Aquapanel board or full-height tiling on at most two walls, an LST radiator, a wall-hung basin with a **blender valve limiting the hot feed to 38°C**, a low-level WC, a GRP tray of at most 35 mm with a pumped-gully option, curtain or bi-fold screens, two 32 mm x 600 mm grab rails, 2.0 mm safety vinyl laid to BS 8203, a humidistat extract fan, a downflow heater, a sealed luminaire, a 9.8 kW electric thermostatic shower, tiling and decoration. It is dated February 2015, so its models are placeholders ([Walsall](https://go.walsall.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2022-08/specification_for_dfg_low_level_tray-3.pdf)). Approved Document M (explicitly OGL v3) adds the regulatory items: a floor gully connected to drainage (capped for a "potential" shower), wall reinforcement to **1.5 kN/m²** for rails and seats, and for M4(3) a WC pan at least 400 mm high, a front lever on the transfer side, a wall-hung basin with rim at 770–850 mm and a ceiling able to take a 200 kg hoist ([ADM Vol 1](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7f8a82ed915d74e622b17b/BR_PDF_AD_M1_2015_with_2016_amendments_V3.pdf)). ADM gives **no shower floor fall**. The commonly quoted gradients must come from the former or gully manufacturer. No public source read in full lists wet-room formers for timber floors, liquid tanking, drop-down WC rails or wash/dry toilets, so those lines start as uncited placeholders.

**Kitchen.** Cheshire West and Chester publishes a full package order: strip-out, first-fix electrics, first-fix plumbing, plastering, units and worktops, second-fix plumbing, three tile courses above the worktop (to floor in the cooker space), second-fix electrics with alarms and testing, joinery, decoration, smoothing compound plus DPM plus vinyl, clean and mastic ([CWaC](https://www.cheshirewestandchester.gov.uk/asset-library/council-housing-kitchen-installation.pdf)). Clackmannanshire and Canterbury add variant-driving items: gas and electric cooker points, a fused spur for a future hood, three double sockets above the worktop with switched appliance spurs, a washing-machine valve, waste and spur, a humidistat fan, and a possible consumer unit replacement ([Clackmannanshire](https://www.clacks.gov.uk/document/2663.pdf), [Canterbury](https://www.canterbury.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-06/Void%20Lettable%20Standard%20policy.pdf)). The M3NHF 371xxx–375xxx families show how unit lines are dimensioned (single base 600x500, double base 800, corner base, larder, 1950 mm appliance housing, worktop by linear metre, upstand, end panels, plinth) ([Sell2Wales 357559](https://www.sell2wales.gov.wales/Assets/NoticeBuilder_FileDownload.aspx?id=357559)). The most granular kitchen specifications (carcass, worktop, sink, trap) sit in Contracts Finder attachments that returned 403 to automated fetches and still need a manual browser pass.

**Full-house refurbishment and voids.** No single source gives a whole-house materials list. Canterbury's 2026 void standard supplies the element checklist: key safe, clearance, kitchen and bathroom to Decent Homes, locks, window restrictors, 270 mm loft insulation, gas-fire removal and LGSR, asbestos register review, LD2 detection with hard-wired smoke and CO alarms, slip-resistant floors, and mist-coat decoration ([Canterbury](https://www.canterbury.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-06/Void%20Lettable%20Standard%20policy.pdf)). Approved Document P defines when electrical work is notifiable: a new circuit, a consumer unit replacement, or work in a special location ([AD P 2013](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/441872/BR_PDF_AD_P_2013.pdf)). York's rewire page gives per-room socket minima (kitchen 3 double plus cooker point, living room 4, bedrooms 2, hall 1) ([York](https://www.york.gov.uk/council-homes/rewiring-council-homes)). PAS 2035 frames retrofit measures as optional packages gated by assessment ([BSI](https://www.bsigroup.com/siteassets/pdf/en/insights-and-media/insights/brochures/pas_2035_2023.pdf)).

**Maintenance kits.** This is the weakest area. SFG20 tells you which tasks happen on which asset class, but its published structure (task, frequency, criticality, time, skill) shows no parts list, and Joblogic's SFG20 import documents no materials fields ([Joblogic](https://support.joblogic.com/docs/sfg20)). CMMS practice attaches a BOM to the asset and links it to the PM routine ([MAPCON](https://mapcon.com/blog/2024/06/bill-of-materials-success-in-work-orders)). The free CIBSE 2026 tool gives service lives down to the lowest maintainable component ([CIBSE](https://cibse.org/knowledge-research/knowledge-portal/indicative-economic-life-expectancy-tool-2026/)). The right schema is therefore asset class → maintainable item → part role, with the actual part number resolved against the specific asset and never assumed from the template. That is consistent with hard rule R2. No open dataset enumerates PM kit contents, so maintenance kits must be authored from OEM manuals per asset family.

## Uniclass codes the line, ETIM describes it, bSDD resolves both

For line identity, **Uniclass 2015 Pr** is the only UK-native, free and commercially usable classification fine enough for template lines. It has Pr_40_20_93_94 WC pans, _93_93 WC pan connectors and _93_62 pre-plumbed installation frames. It covers wastes and jointing (Pr_65_52_25_98 waste traps, _62_65 PTFE tapes), finishes (Pr_20_31_02_89 tile adhesives, Pr_20_31_53_16 cementitious grouts, Pr_30_31_76_77 silicone sealants, Pr_25_71_35_52 moisture-resistant plasterboard) and fixings (Pr_20_29_76_98 wood screws, _76_65 plasterboard screws). Pr and Ss are at v1.43 (July 2026), released quarterly ([Uniclass download](https://uniclass.thenbs.com/download), [taxon pages](https://uniclass.thenbs.com/taxon/pr_40_20)). **Ss** codes such as Ss_40_15_75_97 WC systems and Ss_25_45_88_40 internal wall tiling systems work as kit headers. Uniclass publishes no Ss→Pr membership, though: the WC-systems page links to no products ([Ss_40_15_75_97](https://uniclass.thenbs.com/taxon/ss_40_15_75_97)). Kit content is the product's own authored work. The licence matters here. CC BY-ND permits verbatim commercial use with attribution but arguably not distributing modified tables. Store Uniclass codes and titles unaltered with their version, and put sub-types such as "basin waste, unslotted, click-clack" in a separate namespace that references the parent code. Uniclass appears to lack an outlet-only basin-waste code, a WC pan fixing kit code and a flexible tap connector code, so those sub-types are needed from day one.

For line specification, **ETIM** is the attribute schema. Its ODC-By licence permits adaptation, and its classes carry the features an RFQ needs. The wood screw class EC002385 has thread diameter, length, head form, drive (PZ/PH), surface protection and material ([bSDD EC002385](https://api.bsdd.buildingsmart.org/api/Class/v1?Uri=https://identifier.buildingsmart.org/uri/etim/etim/10.1/class/EC002385&IncludeClassProperties=true)). The toilet class EC011289 has flush volume, rimless, outlet location and wall-hung fixing centres ([ETIM Viewer](https://viewer.etim-international.com/class/EC011289)). A "4.0x40 countersunk PZ2 zinc wood screw" therefore becomes a supplier-neutral, structured spec a merchant can quote against. That is what hard rule R3 asks for: attributes with source and confidence, not free text. ETIM is thin on tiles, grout and plaster accessories, and the openness of the English (UK) translation is unconfirmed. Older ETIM releases in bSDD are marked "no license", so pin to 10.x ([bSDD dictionaries](https://api.bsdd.buildingsmart.org/api/Dictionary/v1)).

**bSDD** ties the two together. Its read API worked without a key and hosts Uniclass 2015 (CC BY-ND), ETIM 10.1 (ODC-By) and IFC 4.3, each with a resolvable URI. Storing bSDD URIs gives stable identifiers and, potentially, ready-made cross-mappings, though whether bSDD holds Uniclass↔ETIM relations for these classes was not checked. Avoid the Lendlease "Uniclass 2025.10" entry, which is marked rights reserved. NRM2 section numbers (28 finishes, 32 FF&E, 33 drainage above ground) are useful as a secondary grouping that quantity surveyors recognise. The NRM rules themselves cannot be reproduced, and which NRM2 section sanitary appliances fall in is unverified. IFC types, UNSPSC and GPC are optional mapping columns for interoperability, not template content. The classification choice is market-specific, so it belongs in the profile (`classification.canonical: uniclass2015`) per ADR-011.

## Quantities come from datasheet constants and geometry, not from lists

No public priced BoQ for a single UK bathroom or kitchen was found, and the cost guides give totals, not quantities. Quantities therefore have to be computed. The research supports a small set of deterministic formulas, each needing a **product-specific constant**. That argues for storing the constant on the SKU family, not in the template.

| Line | Formula / rule | Constant source |
|---|---|---|
| Wall tiles | area × (1 + waste) | Wickes: "add at least 10% extra" ([Wickes](https://www.wickes.co.uk/how-to-guides/tiling/tile-a-wall)); no manufacturer source found, so the waste factor is a profile parameter |
| Tile adhesive | area × kg/m²/mm × bed mm × (1 + waste) | Mapei Keraflex Maxi S1 1.2 kg/m²/mm (retailer quoting TDS) ([listing](https://www.skroutz.gr/s/12196759/Mapei-Keraflex-Maxi-S1-Klebstoff-Kacheln-23kg.html)) |
| Grout | kg/m² = 0.18 × joint width × tile thickness × (L+W)/(L×W) | Weber joint formula ([Weber TDS](https://www.middleeast.weber/files/sodamco/2025-11/TDS_weberjoint-weberjoint_thick.pdf)); about 0.29 kg/m² for 300x300x8 tiles at 3 mm (derived) |
| Silicone | cartridges = ceil(joint length ÷ (ml ÷ (w × d))) | Geometry; 310 ml at 10x10 ≈ 3.1 m ([Truly PVC](https://www.trulypvc.com/blogs/how-to-guides/how-to-estimate-silicone-cartridge-yield)) |
| Jointing compound | per 100 lm of joint: 9 kg taping + 5 kg finish (flat) | British Gypsum White Book (snippet) ([BG](https://www.british-gypsum.com/documents/white-book/british-gypsum-wb-jointing-1.pdf)) |
| Studs, boards, screws | studs = ceil(length/centres)+1; boards = ceil(area/2.88 m²); ≈27 screws per 2400x1200 board at 600 mm centres (derived) | Screwfix stud-wall guide; BG fixing centres |
| Backer board fixings | 200 mm centres, ≥12 screws per Hardiebacker board (forum, unverified) | [TilersForums](https://www.tilersforums.com/threads/hardie-backer-board-info.48531) |
| Tanking | kits = ceil(area ÷ kit coverage) | Mapei kit "up to 4 m²" with primer, membrane, 10 m tape ([Toolstation](https://www.toolstation.com/mapei-shower-waterproofing-kit/p71005)) |
| Paint | litres = area × coats ÷ spread rate | Dulux Trade Vinyl Matt up to 17 m²/L ([Dulux](https://www.duluxtradepaintexpert.co.uk/en/products/dulux-trade-vinyl-matt)) |

Many of these constants come from snippets, forums or non-UK datasheets (Ardex NZ, US Ardex kit sizes). They are fit for illustrative seed data and unfit for production until checked against the current UK TDS.

The second quantity layer is **dependency rules** from manufacturer guides, and here the evidence is primary text. Ideal Standard's i.life bath guide says front and end panels and the fixing pack are "Sold and supplied separately", sealant and battens are "not supplied", and waste and overflow are fitted "where supplied" ([Ideal Standard](https://heatandplumb.com/media/ideal-standard/fitting-instructions/file-512508.pdf)). Mira bar valves need "suitable thread sealant (not supplied)" and wall fixings "(not supplied)", and state "Isolating valves must be installed". Reflex and Discovery ship as bar valves only, while Atom ERD includes the riser kit ([Mira Reflex](https://images.victorianplumbing.co.uk/products/mira-reflex-ev-thermostatic-shower-mixer-chrome-2-1744-020/instructions/21744020.pdf), [Mira Discovery](https://www.showerspares.com/downloads/mira-discovery-bar-mixer-1609.001.pdf)). Triton requires a 45 A double-pole isolator with 3 mm contact gap, a 30 mA RCD, an MCB of 40 A for 8.5 kW, and "strongly recommend[s]" 10 mm² cable ([Triton T70Z](https://showerspares.com/downloads/triton-t70z-electric-shower-7.5kw-white-sp7007z.pdf)). Vent-Axia needs a local all-pole isolator, 100 mm duct and, for ducted moist air, a condensation trap ([Vent-Axia](https://assets.cef.co.uk/downloads/pdg/vent_axia_439253_instructions/vent_axia_439253_instructions.pdf)). Bristan Easyfit taps include isolation valves, while the Melba only "recommends" them ([Bristan Lemon](https://images.victorianplumbing.co.uk/products/bristan-caramel-easyfit-kitchen-sink-mixer-crm-efsnk-c/instructions/lmnefsnkbn.pdf), [Bristan Melba](https://www.bristan.com/product-files/576949/fitting-instructions.pdf)). The lesson is that "supplied vs not" is **SKU-specific**. Rules should key on SKU attributes when a part is chosen and fall back to a type default when it is not. Electric-shower and new-circuit rules should also raise a regulated-work flag, since AD P makes new circuits and special-location work notifiable.

## Trade software exports flat lists; research favours solver plus LLM

Customer data is the cheapest source of realistic kits, but trade tools export them poorly. Simpro exposes Pre-Builds (catalogue items plus labour) through a documented REST API ([Simpro API](https://developer.simprogroup.com/apidoc/)). ServiceM8 supports bundles through its API ([ServiceM8](https://servicem8.com/updates/bundles-api-support)). Buildxact exports estimates as clean Excel and imports recipes from Excel ([Buildxact](https://help.buildxact.com/en/articles/5638324-estimate-and-job-costings-excel-export)). Xactimate exports macros to Excel or PDF ([Xactware](https://xactware.helpdocs.io/l/enUS/article/ism738njgh-exporting-macros)). Tradify, Fergus, Jobber and Houzz Pro document templates but no kit export that was found. No tool was shown to export quantity formulas. Imported customer templates will arrive as fixed quantities and must be re-parameterised. A generic one-row-per-line CSV (template, item, unit, qty, cost, labour flag), close to Buildern's minimum catalogue schema of Name, Cost Type, Unit and Unit Cost ([Buildern](https://help.buildern.com/en/articles/15203910-how-to-import-a-cost-catalog-from-excel-in-buildern)), would accept all of them.

The academic evidence points the same way as the hard rules. The knowledge-based configuration community, summarised in a 2026 JAIR survey ([JAIR](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/21207)), models variant products as feature models with cross-tree constraints, and numeric quantities as constraint satisfaction problems. Its LLM work (ConfWS 2024–2025) uses models to *draft* constraint knowledge bases and act as a conversational front end, with the solver guaranteeing consistency ([CEUR-WS 3812](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3812/), [CEUR-WS 4149](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4149/)). Pure-LLM BoQ generation performs badly. On CEQuest, GPT-4.1 scored 75.4% and open models 62–65% on construction-estimation questions, with errors such as rounding quantities down ([arXiv 2508.16081](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.16081)). An agentic substation BoQ pilot landed within ±70% of a human BoQ, and for one option it fabricated pricing by escalating another option's figures ([arXiv 2511.14478](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.14478)). The most useful result for evaluation design is a vendor's own preprint, so it is not independent: on 1,348 scored residential takeoff lines, "coverage exceeds precision for every one of the seven models". Models find scope better than they quantify it. Professional estimators covered only 65.5% of primary items, and a spec mismatch (plain board where a bathroom needs moisture-resistant board) counted as a miss ([arXiv 2608.15032](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.15032)). That supports a clear split. Retrieval or an LLM may *propose* lines and parse intent. Deterministic rules compute quantities and enforce dependencies. A gold kit per job type measures recall with spec-level matching. Co-purchase mining over merchant baskets has only small Apriori case studies behind it ([MERAL](https://meral.edu.mm/records/3641)), so it fits a "did you forget?" suggestion layer, never the template itself.

## How the product should build its template library

The research supports a five-layer library, each layer with a clear licence posture and home.

| Layer | Content | Built from | Lives in |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vocabulary | Uniclass Pr/Ss codes verbatim, ETIM classes and features, bSDD URIs, own sub-type namespace | Uniclass (CC BY-ND), ETIM (ODC-By), bSDD | Versioned reference data with attribution text |
| Job-type feature models | Mandatory, optional and alternative groups per job type (bathroom full, cloakroom/WC, wet room/level access, kitchen, full-house/void, electrics first/second fix, maintenance by asset class) and variant questions | DHS, ADM/ADF/ADP (OGL); council and HA specs rewritten in own words; earlier WP0–WP12 scope | Template data files, labelled synthetic/illustrative, each with an owner, version and reviewer |
| Dependency rules | "Requires / not supplied / compatible with" rules keyed on type and SKU attributes | Manufacturer guides (short factual extraction, cited), ADH trap sizes | Rule data with per-rule source and confidence |
| Quantity formulas | Area- and count-driven formulas with named parameters | Datasheet constants per SKU family; geometry | Formulas in templates; constants on SKU families |
| Market parameters | Waste factors, extract rates and run-on, shower areas, TMV/blender temperatures, tile courses, socket counts, DHS ages and lifetimes, VAT, classification system | ADF, ADM, DHS, landlord standards, customer specs | Deployment profile (`profiles/uk.yaml`, `uk-scotland.yaml`, `uk-ni.yaml`), never code |

Build order matters more than breadth. Start with one bathroom-full feature model, with cloakroom/WC and wet-room variants derived from it, because those are best evidenced (Welsh spec, Broadland, Walsall, ADM, the dependency table). Have a UK tradesperson review every line, as the earlier design note required. Write the gold kit and the coverage/quantity-precision eval before the template, consistent with the repo's test-first rule and offline evals. Code every line to Uniclass Pr with an ETIM spec, and mark each default line's provenance as `default_template`, so it can never satisfy a critical attribute (R3). Template lines stay generic specs. Tier A part numbers are resolved per supplier quote, and nothing substitutes across tiers without approval (R2). Manufacturer PDFs should be read by people or by the quarantined extractor with grounding checks, never fetched by the planner at runtime (R4). Any new template field that touches `domain.py` goes to `CONTRACT_CHANGES.md`, not an edit.

Four legal and data tasks remain open and block wider seeding. A lawyer needs to read whether an internal Uniclass extension served to customers is a "derivative" under CC BY-ND. Manufacturer site terms on factual extraction need checking, since none were retrievable. Someone must decide whether storing bare M3NHF code numbers as a cross-reference is acceptable without an M3 Housing licence. The CIBSE tool's reuse terms need reading before its lifetimes drive replacement triggers. On the data side, a manual browser pass over the 403-blocked Contracts Finder attachments would supply the missing kitchen detail (carcass, worktop, sink, trap, valve types), wet-room formers and tanking, and a full-house void BoQ.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of the template problem. The constraint is not missing knowledge: UK social-housing buyers publish detailed kitchen, bathroom and adaptation item lists every month, and manufacturers publish exactly which ancillaries their boxes omit. The constraint is that this knowledge is scattered across documents nobody has licensed for reuse, while the openly licensed layer (Uniclass, ETIM, OGL regulations, DBT indices) is vocabulary and thresholds with no kit content. A defensible product therefore owns the *assembly*, meaning the feature models, dependency rules and formulas, and cites everything beneath it. Because that assembly is authored rather than scraped, it is also the asset a competitor cannot copy from public pages.

The sharpest risk is not a missing line but a confident wrong default. The disputed pan-connector rule, the 32 mm versus 40 mm basin waste, and the 38°C versus 43°C outlet limit are all cases where a plausible generic answer is wrong for a given SKU or job variant. The evidence that frontier models find scope better than they quantify it suggests where any value lies. It is in catching omitted ancillaries, and only if quantities and dependencies are computed by rules that a tradesperson has reviewed. Whether UK buyers will use a kit wizard rather than paste a list is untested. The first bathroom template plus its gold-kit eval is the cheapest way to find out.
