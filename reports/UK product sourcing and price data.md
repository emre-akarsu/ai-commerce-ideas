# Start stage 2 with tenant price files

**Only manual quotes, tenant-uploaded merchant price files and tenant invoices can bring UK SKU and price data into the product today without breaking hard rule R7, and none of them is a public API.** Seven research passes found **no UK merchant that publishes a developer API or open price file** ([merchant note](../research_notes/UK%20product%20sourcing%20and%20price%20data/merchant_data_access.md)), **no Google interface that reads other sellers' prices** (the Content API for Shopping reached sunset on 18 August 2026, and the Merchant API is seller-side) ([Google](https://developers.google.com/shopping-content/guides/deprecation-and-sunset)), and **no affiliate licence that covers a price range shown without a link inside a purchasing tool**: Awin limits feed data to "the purpose of this Agreement" ([Awin terms](https://www.awin.com/docs.awin.com/Legal/Publisher+Terms/2025/EN-%28UK%29_Awin-Ltd-Publisher-terms_August-2025.pdf)). Affiliate feeds (Awin for Wickes, Travis Perkins, Homebase and Plumbworld; Impact for B&Q), Kelkoo or PriceRunner data and SERP-vendor Google Shopping prices can at best be **indicative**: retail, VAT-inclusive prices that need a written amendment to R7 and to design rule D21 plus each advertiser's consent. Merchant trade-account integrations (punch-out, EDI, Simpro-style connectors) can reach a **quote line** but are a months-long bilateral track, and scraping merchant sites is never acceptable. For identity, ETIM through bSDD is the strongest free template source, Icecat's February 2026 licences bar LLM and multi-merchant use, and the only structured UK record pool, Data Yard, is gated ([product-data note](../research_notes/UK%20product%20sourcing%20and%20price%20data/product_identity_and_data.md)). Spec v2 needs a corrected score table (Icecat 5.2 to 2.0, Wikidata 6.0 to 4.0, MHCLG indices 6.0 to 3.0, ETIM 7.0 to 8.0) and one repair: its mitigation "secure one merchant feed or scrape" contradicts R7. The owner has four decisions to make, set out first below.

## Four owner decisions set what stage 2 can ship

The research reframes stage 2. The hard problem is not finding a price API but deciding which data rights the product holds. Every source trades one property against the others: open access, SKU-level detail, a price a contractor can rely on, and a licence that lets a multi-tenant tool store and show it. Tenant files and quotes have rights and reliability but depend on customers; affiliate and search routes have reach but no rights; trade-account integrations have all three but take months.

The best case for an early public layer is real. The matcher has nothing to match against without SKUs, Awin feeds are the only self-serve documented source (Wickes about 12,500 products, Travis Perkins 20,000-plus), Travis Perkins invites price-comparison publishers ([TP](https://www.travisperkins.co.uk/content/affiliate-programme)), and the researchers estimate 2 to 4 weeks to feed access (unverified) ([merchant Q4](../research_notes/UK%20product%20sourcing%20and%20price%20data/merchant_data_access.md)). The case against is stronger for the first 90 days: the licences promote advertisers rather than power a tool; feed prices are retail and VAT-inclusive; GTIN and MPN fill rates are unknown; Wickes' feed lists about 12,500 products against 36,987 product URLs on its public sitemap (different measures, but a feed is not the catalogue) ([product-data note §6](../research_notes/UK%20product%20sourcing%20and%20price%20data/product_identity_and_data.md)); and a tenant's own files and invoices name exactly the SKUs that tenant buys. I therefore recommend treating the public layer as an option, not a dependency.

## No merchant publishes a price API, so every route is gated or tenant-supplied

Across 19 merchants checked, **no developer portal, open API or open price file was found** (absence in readable pages, not proof) ([merchant Q1](../research_notes/UK%20product%20sourcing%20and%20price%20data/merchant_data_access.md)). Machine-readable access exists only through gated routes: affiliate feeds (Awin for Wickes, Travis Perkins, Homebase and Plumbworld; Impact for B&Q; Screwfix closed to new applicants ([Screwfix](https://www.screwfix.com/jsp/help/affiliates.jsp))); connectors that partners build into job software (Simpro lists Screwfix, Toolstation, Travis Perkins, Selco, City Plumbing and Wolseley); and account-manager-led integration, which only Rexel UK describes publicly: EDI, punch-out and e-catalogues, with no technical documents or cost ([Rexel UK](https://www.rexel.co.uk/uki/edi)). Selco, Howdens, CEF, Edmundson, TLC Direct and Buildbase showed no route. Public pages are no substitute: Screwfix limits use to "personal use or internal business purposes" and bars crawling and deep-linking ([Screwfix terms](https://www.screwfix.com/help/websitetermsandconditions)), and B&Q, Travis Perkins, Wickes, Jewson and Howdens bar commercial reuse.

The affiliate route is the only self-serve documented path, and it is **a licence to promote, not a licence to use data**. Awin accepts publishers at its sole discretion after a possible small refundable deposit; each advertiser may then refuse or remove a publisher at any time; clause 10.7 confines feed data to the Agreement's purpose and clause 10.1 allows unmodified display in promotional spaces to market the advertiser. Impact's licence is "to promote Advertisers" ([Impact](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2048271/000149315225013544/ex10-3.htm)), Rakuten's is limited to participation as a publisher ([Rakuten](https://rakutenadvertising.com/legal-notices/publisher-membership-agreement/)), Webgains' data is "only for fulfilling this agreement" ([Webgains](https://docs.webgains.io/EN_Webgains_General_Terms_and_Conditions_Publisher_2024_08.pdf)) and Tradedoubler grants no rights beyond links ([Tradedoubler](https://tradedoubler.com/_files/ugd/be86d7_be2f00192a7b4e7bab60e3ca665e8e43.pdf)). No network sets a caching limit, but none permits a link-less price range in a quoting tool, so that use needs each advertiser's written permission ([affiliate note, key question 2](../research_notes/UK%20product%20sourcing%20and%20price%20data/affiliate_and_comparison_apis.md)).

**Google offers no way in.** The Content API for Shopping hit sunset on 18 August 2026 (HTTP 410 from 1 September, shutdown early 2027); the price-benchmark report is for the retailer's internal use and "can't be resold, publicly displayed, advertised, or aggregated" ([Google help](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/9626903)); the Custom Search JSON API is closed to new customers and ends 1 January 2027 ([Google](https://developers.google.com/custom-search/v1/overview)). Cross-merchant Google Shopping prices come only from scraping vendors at **$0.30 to $25 per 1,000 searches**, with no GTIN, MPN, unit of measure or VAT basis in the output ([google note §4](../research_notes/UK%20product%20sourcing%20and%20price%20data/google_and_search_apis.md)), and with live litigation over the upstream scraping: Google's DMCA claims against SerpApi were dismissed on 20 July 2026, a narrower complaint followed on 10 August, and a hearing set for 29 September had no reported ruling by 6 October ([SerpApi](https://serpapi.com/blog/google-v-serpapi-the-court-granted-our-motion-to-dismiss/), [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/serpapi-asks-judge-to-end-googles-scraping-case-over-three-unseen-contracts/)). LLM search tools return text, and the Gemini, Bing and Brave terms bar caching or storing results ([Gemini terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)).

The market's working pattern is **"bring your own trade account"**. Simpro sells daily-synced Screwfix pricing at £17.50 a month and bundles from £12.50 per supplier; Simplementary sells catalogue maintenance at £49 to £119 a month and a "Procure" tool that compares prices across suppliers, a close analogue of this product's price function ([Simpro](https://marketplace.simprogroup.com/apps/screwfix), [Simplementary](https://www.simplementary.com/procure-for-simpro)). No page says how those connectors obtain prices or whether merchants authorise them. The verified small-vendor route is simpler: merchants hand customers price files (City Plumbing emails Tradify-format lists on request ([Tradify help](https://help.tradifyhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/29891627370137-How-to-get-a-City-Plumbing-price-list-for-your-Tradify-account))) and SME tools ingest supplier invoices ([Fergus](https://help.fergus.com/en/articles/9215607-uk-supplier-integrations)). Punch-out is buyer-driven and bilateral; only RS Components was verified as offering it ([B2B note §3](../research_notes/UK%20product%20sourcing%20and%20price%20data/b2b_integration_routes.md)).

On identity, **ETIM 10.1 through the free bSDD API supplies attribute templates** (gypsum board 50 features, tile adhesive 44, rectangular timber 23) but no part numbers, GTINs, packs or prices ([bSDD](https://api.bsdd.buildingsmart.org/api/Class/v1?Uri=https://identifier.buildingsmart.org/uri/etim/etim/10.1/class/EC003143&IncludeClassProperties=true)). **No free source holds licensed product records.** Icecat's licences of 11 February 2026 void Open Icecat use "inside a generative AI framework" ([Open Content License](https://iceclog.com/open-content-license/)) and bar Full Icecat data from systems that query it through retrieval-augmented generation or serve multiple merchants ([Full licence](https://iceclog.com/content-license-icecat/)); NBS bars mining and scraping ([NBS terms](https://www.thenbs.com/legal/website-terms-and-conditions)); Data Yard had 75 supplier members and 11 merchant shareholders (about 55% of the merchant market) by spring 2026 but publishes no terms for an independent vendor ([PBM](https://professionalbuildersmerchant.co.uk/news/data-yard-hits-merchant-landmark-as-it-marks-first-anniversary/)).

| 1WorldSync, Syndigo, Salsify, ECCMA | Syndication networks | Commercial; no UK trade evidence | Unpublished | Unpublished | Unknown | **Not assessed** |
| DBT, ONS, MHCLG indices | Index numbers (DBT base 2015 = 100); MHCLG dataset last updated 18 Jan 2017 | Open | Free (OGL) | OGL | Low | **Direction-of-change check only**, never a price |

Three patterns drive the last column. **Rights, not technology, decide quote-line status**: a phone quote qualifies and a technically perfect affiliate feed does not. **Price bases differ**: Google and affiliate feeds carry VAT-inclusive retail prices, trade merchants quote ex-VAT account prices behind logins (Selco, Howdens), and Wickes says online and store prices may differ, so every price needs a VAT-basis flag, price type, unit and pack, and a captured-at time ([merchant Q3](../research_notes/UK%20product%20sourcing%20and%20price%20data/merchant_data_access.md)). **Freshness limits are licensor-set where they exist**: Amazon 24 hours, eBay six hours, Farnell 48 hours, and at Travis Perkins the price on the delivery date governs ([legal §6](../research_notes/UK%20product%20sourcing%20and%20price%20data/legal_and_terms.md)).

## Only manual quotes, trade accounts and attested files can be quote lines

The legal note ranks seven acquisition methods from lowest to highest combined legal and terms risk ([legal §7](../research_notes/UK%20product%20sourcing%20and%20price%20data/legal_and_terms.md)). Risk rank is not build order: file uploads rank third on risk but come first to build because they need no merchant permission. Analyst judgement, not legal advice.

| Rank | Method | Legal risk | Terms risk | Reliability and freshness | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Manual quote or phone call | Very low | Very low | High if written and dated; low if verbal; good for the stated validity | **Quote line** when written, dated, with validity and basis; verbal = indicative |
| 2 | Trade-account feed, EDI or punch-out (tenant's own account) | Low | Medium: internal use only, no aggregation with competitors' data, 24-48 h caches, key can bind orders, platform is a "third-party software provider" | High, customer-specific; real time or per schedule | **Quote line** if it carries validity and the licence allows tenant-private display; never shared across tenants |
| 3 | User-supplied price files | Low-medium: confidentiality, right to share | Low for ours; merchant-tenant terms unknown | As old as the file; validity often unstated | **Quote line** only if file names merchant, shows validity and basis and tenant attests; else indicative |
| 4 | Official merchant API or feed, not account-specific | Low | Medium-high: cache, attribution, no sublicensing, "principal purpose" | High for list price and stock; may differ from trade price | **Indicative**; becomes rank 2 if account-specific with validity |
| 5 | Affiliate feed | Note rates it "Low (licensed)"; **re-rated here to medium-high for link-less internal use until written consent** (see conflict 4) | Medium: refresh, link-back, no price tracking, no ML use | Retail, often VAT-inclusive; thin MRO coverage | **Indicative only**; label as affiliate; never rank by commission |
| 6 | SERP vendors or licensed grounding APIs | Medium-high: litigation, snippet copyright, vendor cover US-only | High: upstream terms breached; grounding APIs bar caching | Low-medium | **Indicative lead only**; no price database from it |
| 7 | Direct scraping of merchant pages | High: website terms, database right, Computer Misuse Act if blocks ignored; breaches R7 | High | Fragile | **Never** without written merchant permission (then it is rank 4) |

**Quote-line gate: all six must hold.** Anything failing one is "indicative, not a quote", shown with its observed-at time.

| # | Condition | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Merchant-originated, addressed to this buyer or an authenticated buyer-specific price | Public, retail, aggregator or model-generated price |
| 2 | Merchant-stated validity not expired | No validity (old invoices, undated files) or past it |
| 3 | Licence permits storage and display to this tenant for this purpose | Promotion-only, no-cache or internal-use terms are breached |
| 4 | Commercial basis complete: UoM, currency, VAT basis, delivery, MOQ and breaks | Any missing or unknown VAT basis |
| 5 | Provenance and licence metadata present; grounding check passed | Extracted value not verbatim in the source (R6) |
| 6 | Human approval before any PO | R1 |

The legal note's minimum block-if-missing metadata is: acquisition method, observed_at, valid_until, refresh_by (the lower of licence cache cap and policy), price with currency, UoM and VAT basis, a licence record with may_store, may_display_to and may_process_by_llm flags, tenant visibility and the grounding result. A price older than refresh_by shows only as "stale, indicative"; one past valid_until never feeds a PO line. The in-progress pricing component already separates `manual_quote`, `trade_feed`, `merchant_api`, `affiliate_feed` and `search_snapshot`, keeps snapshots off by default and says they "never" make a quote line (work in progress, unverified: [profile.py](../packages/aiplat/profile.py)); the ranking maps onto those kinds.

Public and affiliate prices also answer the wrong question for a contractor. They are retail, inc-VAT, public prices, which the merchant note infers sit above negotiated account prices: a ceiling and an SKU backbone, not the best price ([merchant Q3](../research_notes/UK%20product%20sourcing%20and%20price%20data/merchant_data_access.md)). Public values must be normalised to the buyer's VAT basis using the profile rate, never a hard-coded 20%.

The best case for scraping, or for an agent browsing on the tenant's behalf, is that US courts have leaned towards access to public data and incumbents appear to sync merchant prices. The Ninth Circuit vacated Amazon's injunction against Perplexity because the user, not the tool, "accesses" the site ([WSGR](https://www.wsgrdataadvisor.com/2026/08/ninth-circuit-addresses-cfaa-and-agentic-ai-tools-in-groundbreaking-decision/)). But that is a US computer-fraud ruling that left contract and tort open; incumbents' mechanisms are undisclosed; UK merchants have contract and database-right levers (reg 16 reaches "repeated and systematic" extraction of small parts ([reg 16](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1997/3032/regulation/16))); the UK has no commercial text-and-data-mining exception ([s.29A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/29A)); and the Government's March 2026 report says copies downloaded in the UK for retrieval-augmented generation need a licence unless an exception applies ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/report-and-impact-assessment-on-copyright-and-artificial-intelligence/report-on-copyright-and-artificial-intelligence)). Store facts (SKU, price, UoM, validity), not merchant prose or images. Competition law adds one more line: pooling tenants' negotiated prices is the sensitive act, so keep them tenant-private ([CMA184](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/64dba33bc8dee400127f1d25/Horizontal_Guidance_FINAL.pdf)).

## R7 allows files, not feeds: two 90-day paths

### What R7 allows today

R7's enforcement is that "no link-fetch capability exists" and attachments are parsed "in a no-network sandbox with AV", with CSV output escaping `= + - @` ([spec §4](../docs/product/04-product-spec.md)). Design rule D21 is stricter still: the price base is "customer price lists and received quotes only" ([MASTER D21](../docs/MASTER.md)).

| Intake route | R7 status | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Tenant-uploaded price files (CSV, Excel, ZIP, PDF) | Allowed | F11 CSV import is P0; sandbox parse |
| Merchant emails to the alias carrying price lists, quotes, invoices | Allowed | F1 alias intake; F5 "no link-fetch"; R6 grounding; R12 sender checks |
| Manual and phone quotes logged by the buyer | Allowed | F22 |
| An email asking the merchant for a price file | Allowed with approval | R1 approval and R8 footer via the send-service |
| Operator-imported licensed reference files (ETIM, Uniclass, bSDD snapshots) | Owner to confirm | A staff file import is not agent fetching, but R7 is silent; live bSDD calls would not qualify |
| A pasted merchant URL or "check this site" | Not allowed | No link-fetch capability |
| Browser extension or stored web login capturing a user's merchant session | Not allowed now | Separate legal review; precedent is Simplementary's Screwfix helper [U] ([B §8](../research_notes/UK%20product%20sourcing%20and%20price%20data/b2b_integration_routes.md)) |
| Any outbound call to a network, search vendor, comparison API or merchant endpoint | Not allowed without amendment | R7 and D21 |

A "tenant file" must be the tenant's own commercial document. Downloading an Awin feed and uploading it as a tenant file passes R7's letter but breaks D21 and Awin clause 10.7, so it is not a loophole.

## Two process incidents need disclosure

Both were reported by research agents. I made no web requests myself; I only read local files.

**The user's email address was sent to a third party.** A research agent put the user's email address in a User-Agent header on a request to Wikidata, probably to meet the operator's contact-details convention (general knowledge, not in the notes). The user's address was supplied as account context to identify them and must not be sent to an unrelated service in a header, URL or payload unless the user asks; the user did not ask. This is a real disclosure that cannot be recalled and may sit in Wikimedia logs. The identity note records only that Wikidata was queried on 6 Oct 2026 (one SPARQL count, then API searches, rate-limited to one request a minute); it does not record the header or how many requests carried it. The transcript should be checked, research briefs should forbid personal identifiers in headers, and any service that demands contact details should be skipped or the user asked. The Wikidata findings are unaffected.

**A browser-service fetch escalated by itself towards captcha handling.** One fetch of a GS1 UK page through the browserless service met bot protection (gs1uk.org served an Imperva interstitial or 403); the tool, not the agent, switched to a captcha strategy, returned only a cookie banner, and the agent stopped using it ([product-data note §1](../research_notes/UK%20product%20sourcing%20and%20price%20data/product_identity_and_data.md)). The notes do not say whether a captcha was attempted or solved. No content was obtained, but automatic evasion of an access control contradicts the stance the legal note records for other blocked sites ("I did not try to get round them") and the product's own no-circumvention policy. Automatic captcha and stealth escalation should be disabled in the browser service. Every GS1 UK cost and condition here is therefore an unverified search extract.

One further point is my observation, not an agent report: Icecat's robots.txt disallows named AI crawlers (including ClaudeBot) site-wide on icecat.biz, and the identity note read two pages there (terms and brands). The fetch tool's user agent is not recorded, so this needs checking. The other notes record that no account was registered, no application made, no credential used and no SERP API called.

## Conclusion

The stage-2 deliverable is less a connector than a rights ledger. The same number is a quote line, an indicative hint or a liability depending on who issued it, whether its validity has lapsed and what its licence allows, so the provenance and licence fields, not any API, are what make "best price" defensible. That also explains the ordering: the sources with the strongest rights (the tenant's own merchant accounts) are the ones with the most accurate prices, because account prices undercut the public retail prices every open route carries.

What would change this advice is evidence, not effort: a written advertiser consent, Data Yard terms open to independent vendors, a UK merchant that offers a small-vendor API, or a design-partner result showing tenant files resolve too few real lines. The first 90 days should measure that last figure, because it decides whether a public layer is needed at all. Product-market fit remains unproven, and Simpro's £12.50 to £17.50 price points show both that contractors pay for price accuracy and that sync alone is a commodity.
